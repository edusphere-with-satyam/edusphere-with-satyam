🏭 Production Function and Returns to a Factor

Production is one of the most important economic activities performed by a producer or firm. A producer combines different inputs such as labour, capital, land and raw materials to produce goods and services. The study of production helps us understand how the quantity of output changes when the quantity of inputs changes.

This topic introduces the concept of production function, fixed and variable factors, short run and long run, Total Product, Average Product, Marginal Product and the Law of Variable Proportions.

📌 Main focus of this topic:

Production → Inputs and Output → Production Function → Short Run and Long Run → TP → AP → MP → Law of Variable Proportions → Three Stages → TP, AP and MP Relationships

1. 📘 Meaning of Production

Formal Definition:
Production is the process of transforming inputs or factors of production into goods and services that satisfy human wants.
Simple Definition:
Production means using available resources to create goods and services.

Production does not necessarily mean manufacturing a physical product. The creation or addition of utility through economic activity is also considered production.

Examples of Production

  • A farmer uses land, seeds, labour and machinery to produce crops.
  • A factory uses machines, raw materials and workers to produce goods.
  • A teacher provides educational services.
  • A transport company provides transportation services.
  • A bank provides financial services.
▶ Example: Production in a Bakery

Suppose a bakery uses flour, labour, ovens, electricity and a building to produce bread.

Here, flour and electricity are inputs, workers provide labour, the oven and building are capital/fixed resources, and bread is the final output.

The producer's objective is to organise these resources efficiently so that the required output can be produced.

2. ⚙️ Inputs and Output

Input

Formal Definition:
An input is a resource or factor used by a producer in the production process.

Output

Formal Definition:
Output refers to the quantity of goods or services produced by a firm during a given period.
In simple words:
Inputs are what a producer uses, while output is what the producer produces.
Basic Production Process:

Inputs → Production Process → Output
▶ Example: Garment Factory

Inputs:

  • Workers
  • Machines
  • Cloth
  • Electricity
  • Factory building

These inputs are combined through the production process to produce garments.

Output: Number of garments produced.

3. 🌍 Factors of Production

Factors of production are the resources used for producing goods and services.

Land

Land refers to all natural resources used in production, such as soil, water, forests and minerals.

Labour

Labour refers to the physical and mental effort of human beings used in the production of goods and services.

Capital

Capital refers to man-made resources such as machines, tools, equipment and buildings that are used for further production.

Entrepreneur

An entrepreneur organises different factors of production, takes business decisions, bears risk and coordinates production activities.

Remember: Land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship are commonly discussed as the major factors of production.

4. ⚖️ Fixed Factor and Variable Factor

Fixed Factor

A fixed factor is a factor whose quantity cannot be changed during the relevant short-run production period.

Examples may include the size of a factory building, heavy machinery or the amount of land available to a farmer.

Variable Factor

A variable factor is a factor whose quantity can be changed during the relevant production period.

Labour is commonly treated as a variable factor in short-run analysis.

Basis Fixed Factor Variable Factor
Quantity Cannot be changed in the short run Can be changed in the short run
Example Factory building Labour
Short-run role Remains fixed Can be increased or decreased
⚠️ Important:
Fixed does not mean that the factor can never be changed. It means that its quantity is fixed with respect to the particular short-run period being considered.

5. 📊 Production Function

Formal Definition:
A production function shows the technical relationship between the quantities of inputs used and the maximum quantity of output that can be produced with those inputs, given the state of technology.
Simple Definition:
Production function tells us how much output can be produced from different combinations of inputs.

General Form

Q = f(L, K)

Where:

  • Q = Quantity of output
  • L = Labour
  • K = Capital
  • f = Functional relationship between inputs and output

Important Features of Production Function

  • It shows a technical relationship between inputs and output.
  • It relates quantities of inputs with quantity of output.
  • It assumes a given state of technology.
  • It considers the maximum possible output from given inputs under the specified conditions.
  • It does not directly describe the monetary cost of production.
▶ Example 1: Factory Production Function

Suppose a factory uses labour and machines to produce tables. If the number of workers changes while the machines remain fixed, the quantity of tables produced may also change.

The production function helps us study this technical relationship between the inputs and output.

▶ Example 2: Agricultural Production

A farmer uses land, labour, seeds, fertilisers and machinery to produce wheat.

If the farmer changes the amount of labour while keeping land fixed, the resulting change in wheat production can be studied through the production function.

📌 Technology:
A change in technology can change the production possibilities of a firm. Therefore, production function is generally discussed for a given state of technology.

6. ⏳ Short Run and Long Run

Short Run

The short run is a period in which at least one factor of production is fixed while other factors may be varied.

Long Run

The long run is a period in which all factors of production can be varied.
⚠️ Very Important:
Short run and long run are not defined simply by the number of days, months or years. The distinction is based on whether factors of production can be changed.
Basis Short Run Long Run
Fixed factor At least one factor remains fixed All factors can be varied
Variable factors Some factors can be varied All factors can be varied
Production flexibility Relatively limited Greater flexibility

7. 📈 Total Product (TP)

Formal Definition:
Total Product is the total quantity of output produced by all units of the variable factor employed with given quantities of fixed factors.
Simple Definition:
TP means the total output produced by the firm.

Suppose a factory has a fixed number of machines and keeps increasing the number of workers. The total quantity produced by all the workers together is Total Product.

TP = Total Output
▶ Example

If 5 workers together produce 60 units of output, then:

TP = 60 units

TP does not mean output per worker. Output per worker is measured by Average Product.

8. 📊 Average Product (AP)

Formal Definition:
Average Product is the output produced per unit of the variable factor.
Simple Definition:
AP tells us the average output produced by each unit of the variable factor.
AP = TP ÷ Units of Variable Factor

If 5 workers produce 60 units of output:

AP = 60 ÷ 5 = 12 units per worker
📌 Remember:
AP is output per unit of the variable factor. It is not the total output.

9. 📉 Marginal Product (MP)

Formal Definition:
Marginal Product is the change in Total Product resulting from employing one additional unit of the variable factor, while other factors remain constant.
Simple Definition:
MP tells us how much total output changes when one more unit of the variable factor is employed.
MP = ΔTP ÷ ΔVariable Factor

If the number of workers increases from 4 to 5 and TP increases from 52 units to 60 units:

MP = (60 − 52) ÷ (5 − 4) = 8 units
⚠️ Important:
MP can be positive, zero or negative.

10. ⭐ Law of Variable Proportions

Formal Statement:
The Law of Variable Proportions states that as more and more units of a variable factor are employed with fixed factors, Total Product initially increases at an increasing rate, then increases at a diminishing rate, and eventually may decline.
In simple words:
If we keep adding more units of a variable input to fixed inputs, output may first increase rapidly, then increase slowly, and eventually start falling.

Why is it called Variable Proportions?

In the short run, at least one factor remains fixed while the quantity of another factor is increased. Therefore, the proportion between the fixed and variable factors changes.

Example:
Suppose a factory has a fixed number of machines. If the number of workers increases from 1 to 2, 3, 4 and so on, the ratio between workers and machines keeps changing.

🔍 Detailed Graph Analysis

Stage I — Increasing Returns

In the beginning, the TP curve rises at an increasing rate. This means each additional unit of the variable factor adds more to output than the previous unit. MP rises during the initial portion and AP also rises.

The fixed factor is initially underutilised. As more units of the variable factor are added, better utilisation, division of labour and specialisation can increase productivity.

End of Stage I

Stage I ends when AP reaches its maximum point. At this point:

MP = AP

Stage II — Diminishing Returns

During Stage II, TP continues to rise but at a diminishing rate. Therefore, MP falls but remains positive.

AP also falls during this stage after reaching its maximum.

At the end of Stage II:

MP = 0

At this point, TP reaches its maximum.

Stage III — Negative Returns

After TP reaches its maximum, additional units of the variable factor can cause total output to fall. MP becomes negative.

MP < 0 → TP falls

The excessive use of the variable factor creates overcrowding and interference with the fixed factor.

11. 📚 Three Stages of the Law of Variable Proportions

Stage I — Increasing Returns to a Factor

During this stage, Total Product increases at an increasing rate initially. Marginal Product rises and Average Product also rises.

The main reason is better utilisation of the fixed factor along with division of labour and specialisation.

Stage I ends where Average Product is maximum and:

MP = AP

Stage II — Diminishing Returns to a Factor ⭐

During this stage, Total Product continues to increase but at a diminishing rate.

Marginal Product decreases but remains positive. Average Product also decreases after reaching its maximum.

Stage II ends when Marginal Product becomes zero.

MP = 0 → TP is maximum

This stage is generally considered the rational stage of production because the variable factor is still adding positively to total output, although at a diminishing rate.

Stage III — Negative Returns

During Stage III, Total Product begins to decline. Marginal Product becomes negative.

MP < 0 → TP decreases

The variable factor has become excessive relative to the fixed factor. Additional units may interfere with one another and reduce overall production.

12. 🔗 Relationship Between TP, AP and MP

The relationship between Total Product, Average Product and Marginal Product is one of the most important parts of this topic. It is especially important for numerical questions, graphs and MCQs.

📈 Relationship 1 — MP and TP

Marginal Product shows the change in Total Product.

When MP is positive, TP increases.

When MP is zero, TP reaches its maximum.

When MP becomes negative, TP begins to fall.

MP > 0 → TP rises
MP = 0 → TP is maximum
MP < 0 → TP falls
📊 Relationship 2 — MP and AP

Marginal Product has an important relationship with Average Product.

MP > AP → AP rises
MP = AP → AP is maximum
MP < AP → AP falls

The logic is similar to the effect of a new observation on an average. If the marginal value is greater than the current average, the average rises. If it is lower, the average falls.

📉 Relationship 3 — TP and MP

The slope of the TP curve is related to MP.

When MP is increasing, TP rises at an increasing rate. When MP is falling but remains positive, TP rises at a diminishing rate. When MP becomes zero, TP reaches its maximum.

📊 View Relationship Graph

The important idea is that MP tells us the change in TP caused by an additional unit of the variable factor. Therefore, MP determines whether TP is rising, reaching its maximum or falling.

13. 🤔 Why Does the Law of Variable Proportions Operate?

The Law of Variable Proportions operates because the fixed factor does not change while the variable factor keeps increasing.

During the Initial Stage

  • The fixed factor may be underutilised.
  • Additional workers can use the fixed factor more effectively.
  • Division of labour becomes possible.
  • Specialisation may increase productivity.

During the Diminishing Returns Stage

  • The fixed factor becomes relatively scarce.
  • Additional workers have less fixed capital available per worker.
  • Efficiency gains from additional workers become smaller.
  • Marginal Product starts falling.

During the Negative Returns Stage

  • The variable factor becomes excessive.
  • Overcrowding may occur.
  • Workers may interfere with one another.
  • The fixed factor becomes inadequate.
  • Additional units can reduce total output.

15. 🧮 Detailed Examples

▶ Example 1 — Calculate AP and MP

Suppose a firm employs workers as follows:

Workers TP
1 10
2 24
3 39

For 2 workers:

AP = 24 ÷ 2 = 12
MP = 24 − 10 = 14

Therefore, AP is 12 units and MP is 14 units.

▶ Example 2 — Identify the Stage

Suppose MP is positive for several additional workers and then becomes zero.

When MP is positive, TP continues to increase. When MP becomes zero, TP reaches its maximum.

If MP becomes negative after that, TP starts declining.

Conclusion:
Positive MP → TP rises
Zero MP → TP maximum
Negative MP → TP falls

16. 📌 Important Relationships at a Glance

▶ View Key Relationships
MP > AP → AP rises
MP = AP → AP is maximum
MP < AP → AP falls
MP > 0 → TP rises
MP = 0 → TP is maximum
MP < 0 → TP falls
Very Important Exam Point:
When MP is falling but remains positive, TP is still increasing. It is incorrect to say that TP falls merely because MP is falling. TP falls only when MP becomes negative.

17. ⚠️ Common Student Confusions

▶ Short Run vs Short Period
Short run does not necessarily mean a few days or months. It means that at least one factor remains fixed.
▶ TP vs AP
TP is total output, whereas AP is output per unit of the variable factor.
▶ Falling MP vs Negative MP
A falling MP can still be positive. TP continues to increase as long as MP remains positive.
▶ Maximum TP vs Maximum AP
Maximum TP occurs when MP = 0. Maximum AP occurs when MP = AP. These are different conditions.
▶ Diminishing Returns vs Negative Returns
Diminishing returns means MP is falling but still positive. Negative returns means MP has become negative.

🧮 19. Five Strong Numerical Questions

These questions are designed to test calculation as well as conceptual understanding. Each answer is kept expandable so the page remains compact.

Question 1.
A firm employs 5 workers and produces 60 units of output. Calculate Average Product.
View Answer
AP = TP ÷ Labour

AP = 60 ÷ 5

AP = 12 units per worker
Question 2.
The Total Product of a firm increases from 52 units to 60 units when labour increases from 4 workers to 5 workers. Calculate Marginal Product.
View Answer
MP = Change in TP ÷ Change in Labour

MP = (60 − 52) ÷ (5 − 4)

MP = 8 units
Question 3.
Complete the missing AP and MP values:
Labour TP AP MP
1 10 ? ?
2 24 ? ?
3 39 ? ?
View Answer
For Labour = 1: AP = 10 ÷ 1 = 10 MP = 10 − 0 = 10

For Labour = 2: AP = 24 ÷ 2 = 12 MP = 24 − 10 = 14

For Labour = 3: AP = 39 ÷ 3 = 13 MP = 39 − 24 = 15
Question 4.
A firm's TP is 50 units at 5 workers and remains 50 units at 6 workers. What is the MP of the sixth worker? What does this indicate?
View Answer
MP = 50 − 50

MP = 0

When MP = 0, TP is maximum. Therefore, TP is maximum at 50 units.
Question 5.
The TP of a firm is 70 units with 6 workers and falls to 64 units when the seventh worker is employed. Calculate MP of the seventh worker and identify the implication.
View Answer
MP = 64 − 70

MP = −6 units

Since MP is negative, TP falls. This indicates the operation of negative returns to the variable factor.

⭐ 20. Important Examination Concepts

  • Production function shows a technical relationship between inputs and output.
  • It is considered for a given state of technology.
  • In the short run, at least one factor is fixed.
  • In the long run, all factors can be varied.
  • TP represents total output.
  • AP represents output per unit of variable factor.
  • MP represents the change in TP caused by an additional unit of variable factor.
  • When MP is positive, TP rises.
  • When MP is zero, TP is maximum.
  • When MP is negative, TP falls.
  • When MP is greater than AP, AP rises.
  • When MP equals AP, AP is maximum.
  • When MP is less than AP, AP falls.
  • Stage II is the rational stage of production because MP is positive but diminishing.
