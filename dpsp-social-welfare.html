📘 Articles 39A–47

This section deals with some of the most important social and economic objectives of the Directive Principles. These provisions focus on access to justice, local self-government, employment, education, labour welfare, maternity relief, living wages, cooperative societies, civil laws, children, weaker sections and public health.

⚖️ Article 39A — Equal Justice and Free Legal Aid

Article 39A
Constitutional Provision

“The State shall secure that the operation of the legal system promotes justice, on a basis of equal opportunity, and shall, in particular, provide free legal aid, by suitable legislation or schemes or in any other way, to ensure that opportunities for securing justice are not denied to any citizen by reason of economic or other disabilities.”

Explanation

Justice should not become something available only to people who can afford expensive legal services. The State should work to make the legal system accessible on the basis of equal opportunity.

Where a person's economic or other circumstances make it difficult to obtain legal assistance, the State should provide appropriate legal aid through laws, schemes or other suitable measures.

💡 Example

A person who cannot afford a lawyer may receive legal assistance through an appropriate legal aid mechanism.

🏘️ Article 40 — Organisation of Village Panchayats

Article 40
Constitutional Provision

“The State shall take steps to organise village panchayats and endow them with such powers and authority as may be necessary to enable them to function as units of self-government.”

Explanation

The Constitution expects the State to establish and strengthen village-level institutions so that local people can participate in governing their own communities.

The idea is that governance should not remain completely centralised. Local institutions should receive sufficient powers and authority to deal with local matters.

💡 Example

The Panchayati Raj system provides a constitutional framework for local self-government in rural areas.

👷 Article 41 — Right to Work, Education & Public Assistance

Article 41
Constitutional Provision

“The State shall, within the limits of its economic capacity and development, make effective provision for securing the right to work, to education and to public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness and disablement, and in other cases of undeserved want.”

Explanation

The State should work towards providing opportunities for employment and education and should provide appropriate public assistance to people facing certain situations such as unemployment, old age, sickness or disability.

However, Article 41 expressly recognises that such measures operate within the limits of the State's economic capacity and development.

💡 Example

Social-security and public-assistance programmes for elderly, unemployed or persons with disabilities can reflect this constitutional objective.

👩‍⚕️ Article 42 — Humane Conditions of Work & Maternity Relief

Article 42
Constitutional Provision

“The State shall make provision for securing just and humane conditions of work and for maternity relief.”

Explanation

Workers should be provided with working conditions that respect their health, safety, dignity and basic human needs.

The provision also specifically directs the State to provide maternity relief, recognising the need for support and protection related to maternity.

💡 Example

Workplace protections and maternity-related benefits are examples of measures that can advance the objectives of Article 42.

💰 Article 43 — Living Wage, etc., for Workers

Article 43
Constitutional Provision

“The State shall endeavour to secure, by suitable legislation or economic organisation or in any other way, to all workers, agricultural, industrial or otherwise, work, a living wage, conditions of work ensuring a decent standard of life and full enjoyment of leisure and social and cultural opportunities and, in particular, the State shall endeavour to promote cottage industries on an individual or co-operative basis in rural areas.”

Explanation

Article 43 aims at more than simply providing employment. It directs the State to work towards conditions in which workers can earn a living wage and enjoy a decent standard of life.

Workers should also have opportunities for leisure and participation in social and cultural activities.

The Article specifically encourages the promotion of cottage industries in rural areas, either on an individual or cooperative basis.

💡 Example

Policies supporting rural handicrafts, village industries and better working conditions can contribute towards the objectives of Article 43.

🏭 Article 43A — Participation of Workers in Management

Article 43A
Constitutional Provision

“The State shall take steps, by suitable legislation or in any other way, to secure the participation of workers in the management of undertakings, establishments or other organisations engaged in any industry.”

Explanation

Workers should have an opportunity to participate in the management of industrial organisations rather than being completely excluded from decisions affecting their workplace.

The principle seeks to encourage greater participation of workers in industrial management.

📌 Constitutional Amendment:
Article 43A was inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976.

🤝 Article 43B — Promotion of Co-operative Societies

Article 43B
Constitutional Provision

“The State shall endeavour to promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management of Co-operative societies.”

Explanation

The State should encourage cooperative societies that are formed voluntarily and function with autonomy, democratic control and professional management.

The provision aims to strengthen cooperatives as democratic and efficient institutions rather than making them completely dependent on government control.

📌 Constitutional Amendment:
Article 43B was inserted by the 97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011.

⚖️ Article 44 — Uniform Civil Code

Article 44
Constitutional Provision

“The State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a uniform civil code throughout the territory of India.”

Explanation

Article 44 directs the State to endeavour towards a Uniform Civil Code (UCC) for citizens throughout India.

In general terms, a uniform civil code refers to a common set of civil rules governing matters such as marriage, divorce, inheritance and related personal civil matters, rather than different rules based on different personal-law systems.

Article 44 is a Directive Principle; it does not itself create an immediately enforceable fundamental right to a UCC.

👶 Article 45 — Early Childhood Care & Education

Article 45
Constitutional Provision

“The State shall endeavour to provide early childhood care and education for all children until they complete the age of six years.”

Explanation

The State should work towards providing care and education during the early years of childhood, up to the completion of six years of age.

The focus is on early childhood development, when proper care, nutrition, learning and a supportive environment can have a major impact on a child's development.

📌 Important:
Article 45 should not be confused with Article 21A, which deals with the right to free and compulsory education for children in the specified age group.

🛡️ Article 46 — Promotion of Educational and Economic Interests

Article 46
Constitutional Provision

“The State shall promote with special care the educational and economic interests of the weaker sections of the people, and, in particular, of the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes, and shall protect them from social injustice and all forms of exploitation.”

Explanation

The State has a responsibility to give special attention to the educational and economic advancement of weaker sections.

The Constitution specifically mentions the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes and directs the State to protect them from social injustice and exploitation.

💡 Example

Educational support, scholarships and measures aimed at improving economic opportunities for disadvantaged sections can advance the objective of Article 46.

🏥 Article 47 — Nutrition, Standard of Living & Public Health

Article 47
Constitutional Provision

“The State shall regard the raising of the level of nutrition and the standard of living of its people and the improvement of public health as among its primary duties and, in particular, the State shall endeavour to bring about prohibition of the consumption except for medicinal purposes of intoxicating drinks and of drugs which are injurious to health.”

Explanation

Article 47 places improvement of nutrition, standard of living and public health among the primary duties of the State.

It also directs the State to endeavour towards prohibition of the consumption of intoxicating drinks and harmful drugs, while making an exception for medicinal purposes.

💡 Example

Public-health programmes, nutrition schemes and measures aimed at improving living conditions reflect the broader objectives of Article 47.
