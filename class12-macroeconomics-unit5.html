International economic transactions involve payments between residents of different countries. Foreign exchange helps in making these payments, while the Balance of Payments records the economic transactions of a country with the rest of the world.

1. FOREIGN EXCHANGE

💱 Meaning of Foreign Exchange

Definition 1:
Foreign exchange refers to foreign currencies and claims payable in foreign currencies that are used for making international payments.
Definition 2:
Foreign exchange means the system through which one country's currency is exchanged for the currency of another country.
Simple Meaning:
When Indian residents need US dollars to make payments to people or businesses in the USA, they need foreign exchange.
Example:
An Indian company imports machinery from the USA and needs to make payment in US dollars. The company therefore needs foreign exchange.
2. FOREIGN EXCHANGE MARKET

🌍 Foreign Exchange Market

Definition 1:
Foreign exchange market is a market where currencies of different countries are bought and sold.
Definition 2:
It is a market in which foreign currencies are exchanged for domestic currency and vice versa.

Major Participants

  • Commercial Banks
  • Central Bank
  • Importers and Exporters
  • Foreign Investors
  • Travellers
  • Financial Institutions
3. DEMAND FOR FOREIGN EXCHANGE

📥 Demand for Foreign Exchange

Definition 1:
Demand for foreign exchange refers to the desire and ability of residents of a country to purchase foreign currency.
Definition 2:
It is the quantity of foreign currency that domestic residents are willing and able to buy at a particular exchange rate.

Major Sources of Demand

  • Import of goods and services
  • Foreign travel
  • Education abroad
  • Investment in foreign countries
  • Payment of foreign debts
  • Gifts and transfers to foreign countries
Relationship with Exchange Rate:
Other things remaining constant, a higher price of foreign currency generally reduces the quantity demanded of foreign exchange.
4. SUPPLY OF FOREIGN EXCHANGE

📤 Supply of Foreign Exchange

Definition 1:
Supply of foreign exchange refers to the amount of foreign currency available in the foreign exchange market.
Definition 2:
It is the quantity of foreign currency that foreign currency holders are willing and able to sell at a particular exchange rate.

Major Sources of Supply

  • Exports of goods and services
  • Foreign tourists spending in India
  • Foreign investment in India
  • Remittances from abroad
  • Borrowings from foreign countries
General Relationship:
Other things remaining constant, a higher domestic-currency price of foreign currency can increase the incentive to supply foreign currency to the market.
5. EXCHANGE RATE

💱 Exchange Rate

Definition 1:
Exchange rate is the rate at which one country's currency is exchanged for another country's currency.
Definition 2:
It is the price of one currency expressed in terms of another currency.
Example:
If $1 = ₹85, it means one US dollar can be exchanged for ₹85.
6. TYPES OF EXCHANGE RATE

📊 Types of Exchange Rate Systems

1. Fixed Exchange Rate

Definition:
A fixed exchange rate system is one in which the exchange rate is maintained at a particular level by the monetary authority.

2. Flexible Exchange Rate

Definition:
A flexible exchange rate is determined mainly by the demand and supply of foreign exchange in the market.

3. Managed Floating Exchange Rate

Definition:
A managed floating system is one in which the exchange rate is mainly market-determined but the central bank may intervene to influence exchange-rate conditions.
System Main Feature
Fixed Rate maintained by monetary authority
Flexible Determined mainly by demand and supply
Managed Floating Market forces with central bank intervention
7. DETERMINATION

⚖️ Determination of Exchange Rate

Definition 1:
Under a flexible exchange rate system, the equilibrium exchange rate is determined by the interaction of demand and supply of foreign exchange.
Definition 2:
The equilibrium exchange rate is the rate at which the quantity of foreign exchange demanded equals the quantity supplied.
Equilibrium Exchange Rate:

Demand for Foreign Exchange = Supply of Foreign Exchange
Example:
If demand for dollars is equal to supply of dollars at ₹85 per dollar, ₹85 per dollar represents the equilibrium exchange rate.
8. DEPRECIATION

📉 Depreciation of Currency

Definition 1:
Depreciation refers to a fall in the value of a domestic currency relative to foreign currency under a flexible exchange rate system.
Definition 2:
Currency depreciation means that more units of domestic currency are required to purchase one unit of foreign currency.
Example:
If $1 = ₹80 and later $1 = ₹85, the Indian rupee has depreciated against the US dollar.
General Effect:
Depreciation can make exports relatively cheaper for foreigners and imports relatively more expensive for domestic residents, other things remaining constant.
9. APPRECIATION

📈 Appreciation of Currency

Definition 1:
Appreciation refers to a rise in the value of a domestic currency relative to foreign currency under a flexible exchange rate system.
Definition 2:
Currency appreciation means that fewer units of domestic currency are required to purchase one unit of foreign currency.
Example:
If $1 = ₹85 and later $1 = ₹80, the Indian rupee has appreciated against the US dollar.
10. DEVALUATION

📉 Devaluation of Currency

Definition 1:
Devaluation is an official reduction in the value of a country's currency under a fixed exchange rate system.
Definition 2:
Devaluation occurs when the monetary authority officially lowers the external value of the domestic currency.
11. REVALUATION

📈 Revaluation of Currency

Definition 1:
Revaluation is an official increase in the value of a country's currency under a fixed exchange rate system.
Definition 2:
It occurs when the monetary authority officially raises the external value of the domestic currency.
Concept Meaning Exchange Rate System
Depreciation Market-driven fall in currency value Flexible
Appreciation Market-driven rise in currency value Flexible
Devaluation Official fall in currency value Fixed
Revaluation Official rise in currency value Fixed
Board Exam Trick:

Depreciation & Appreciation → generally associated with market forces.

Devaluation & Revaluation → generally associated with official action under a fixed exchange-rate system.
12. BALANCE OF PAYMENTS

🌍 Balance of Payments

Definition 1:
Balance of Payments is a systematic record of all economic transactions between the residents of a country and the rest of the world during a given period of time.
Definition 2:
BOP is a statement that records the transactions relating to exports, imports, income, transfers and capital flows between a country and the rest of the world.
Important:
BOP is a flow concept because it records transactions during a particular period of time.
13. COMPONENTS OF BOP

📋 Components of Balance of Payments

Main Accounts:

1. Current Account
2. Capital Account
14. CURRENT ACCOUNT

💳 Current Account

Definition 1:
Current account records transactions relating to goods, services, income and current transfers between residents of a country and the rest of the world.
Definition 2:
It records transactions that do not directly create or reduce financial assets and liabilities of the country in the same way as capital transactions.

Major Components

  • Export and import of goods
  • Export and import of services
  • Income receipts and payments
  • Current transfers
15. CAPITAL ACCOUNT

💰 Capital Account

Definition 1:
Capital account records transactions that involve changes in financial assets and liabilities between residents of a country and the rest of the world.
Definition 2:
It records capital transactions such as foreign investment, borrowings and lending that affect the international financial position of a country.

Major Components

  • Foreign Direct Investment
  • Portfolio Investment
  • External Borrowings
  • Loans
  • Banking Capital
  • Other capital transactions
16. BALANCE OF TRADE

🚢 Balance of Trade

Definition 1:
Balance of Trade is the difference between the value of exports and imports of visible goods during a given period.
Definition 2:
BOT measures the difference between merchandise exports and merchandise imports of a country.
Balance of Trade = Export of Goods − Import of Goods
Situation Meaning
Exports > Imports Trade Surplus
Exports < Imports Trade Deficit
Exports = Imports Balanced Trade

⚖️ Balance of Trade vs Balance of Payments

Basis BOT BOP
Meaning Difference between exports and imports of goods Record of international economic transactions
Scope Narrow Broad
Goods Included Included
Services Not included in BOT Included
Capital transactions Not included Included
17. BOP SURPLUS

📈 Balance of Payments Surplus

Definition:
A BOP surplus situation occurs when, after considering the relevant transactions and the accounting framework, there is an excess of foreign exchange receipts over payments for the period.
Possible effects:
• Increase in foreign exchange reserves
• Greater availability of foreign exchange
• Possible appreciation pressure on domestic currency under flexible exchange rates
18. BOP DEFICIT

📉 Balance of Payments Deficit

Definition:
A BOP deficit situation occurs when foreign exchange payments exceed foreign exchange receipts in the relevant transactions for the period.
Possible effects:
• Pressure on foreign exchange reserves
• Pressure for currency depreciation under a flexible exchange-rate system
• Need for external financing or adjustment measures

🔍 Causes of BOP Deficit

  • High imports
  • Low exports
  • Increase in international prices of essential imports
  • Large payments for foreign services
  • Large income payments to the rest of the world
  • Capital outflows
  • Slowdown in foreign exchange earnings

🛠️ Measures to Correct BOP Deficit

1. Export Promotion

Increasing exports can raise foreign exchange earnings.

2. Import Control

Reducing unnecessary imports can lower foreign exchange payments.

3. Exchange Rate Adjustment

Depreciation of domestic currency can make exports relatively cheaper and imports relatively expensive, other things remaining constant.

4. Encouraging Foreign Investment

Foreign investment can provide foreign exchange inflows.

🔄 Foreign Exchange — Quick Relationships

Change General Effect
Exports increase Supply of foreign exchange increases
Imports increase Demand for foreign exchange increases
Foreign investment in India increases Supply of foreign exchange increases
Foreign travel by Indians increases Demand for foreign exchange increases

🧮 Important Formula Sheet

Balance of Trade = Export of Goods − Import of Goods
Equilibrium Exchange Rate:

Demand for Foreign Exchange = Supply of Foreign Exchange
Current Account Balance = Receipts − Payments on Current Account
Remember:
BOT covers mainly goods, while BOP has a much broader coverage including goods, services, income, transfers and capital transactions.

⭐ Most Important Board Points

1. Foreign exchange means foreign currencies and claims payable in foreign currencies used for international payments.
2. Exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another currency.
3. Flexible exchange rate is mainly determined by demand and supply of foreign exchange.
4. Depreciation is a market-driven fall in currency value under a flexible exchange-rate system.
5. Appreciation is a market-driven rise in currency value under a flexible exchange-rate system.
6. Devaluation is an official reduction in currency value under a fixed exchange-rate system.
7. Revaluation is an official increase in currency value under a fixed exchange-rate system.
8. BOP records international economic transactions.
9. Current Account records goods, services, income and current transfers.
10. Capital Account records capital and financial transactions.
11. BOT deals mainly with merchandise exports and imports.
