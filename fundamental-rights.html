🇮🇳 Fundamental Rights — Overview

Fundamental Rights are basic rights guaranteed by the Constitution of India for the protection of individual liberty, equality and dignity. They are mainly contained in Part III of the Constitution, Articles 12 to 35.

Part III: Articles 12–35
Purpose: Protection of individual rights and constitutional freedoms
Enforcement: Courts can enforce Fundamental Rights

📘 Basic Constitutional Framework

Article 12

Definition of State

Article 12 defines the term "State" for the purpose of Fundamental Rights. It includes the Government and Parliament of India, Government and Legislature of States, local authorities and other authorities within the territory of India or under the control of the Government of India.

Article 13

Laws Inconsistent with Fundamental Rights

Article 13 provides that laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights are void to the extent of their inconsistency.

⚖️ Fundamental Rights

Articles 14–18

⚖️ Right to Equality

Equality before law, prohibition of discrimination, equality of opportunity, abolition of untouchability and abolition of titles.

Articles 19–22

🕊️ Right to Freedom

Six freedoms under Article 19, reasonable restrictions, protection in criminal cases, life and personal liberty, right to education and protection against arrest and detention.

Articles 23–24

🚫 Right Against Exploitation

These Articles protect individuals against trafficking, forced labour and child labour in hazardous employment.

Article 23(1):
Prohibits trafficking in human beings, begar and other similar forms of forced labour.

Article 23(2):
The State may impose compulsory service for public purposes, but there shall be no discrimination only on the grounds of religion, race, caste or class.

Article 24:
No child below the age of 14 years shall be employed in any factory, mine or other hazardous employment.
Articles 25–28

🛕 Right to Freedom of Religion

Protects freedom of conscience and the right to profess, practise and propagate religion, subject to constitutional restrictions.

Articles 29–30

🎓 Cultural & Educational Rights

These Articles protect cultural interests and provide special rights to religious and linguistic minorities regarding educational institutions.

Article 29(1):
Any section of citizens having a distinct language, script or culture has the right to conserve it.

Article 29(2):
No citizen shall be denied admission to an educational institution maintained by or receiving aid from the State on grounds only of religion, race, caste or language.

Article 30(1):
Religious and linguistic minorities have the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice.

Article 30(2):
The State shall not discriminate in granting aid to an educational institution on the ground that it is under the management of a minority.
Article 32

🏛️ Right to Constitutional Remedies

Article 32 provides the right to approach the Supreme Court for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

📜 Articles 31A, 31B & 31C

Article 31A

Saving of Certain Laws

Article 31A protects certain categories of laws, especially laws relating to acquisition of estates and similar matters, from challenge under specified Fundamental Rights.

Article 31B

Ninth Schedule

Article 31B protects certain Acts and Regulations placed in the Ninth Schedule from being declared void on specified grounds. However, such laws are subject to the Basic Structure doctrine.

Article 31C

Protection of Certain Laws

Article 31C provides protection to certain laws made to give effect to specified Directive Principles, subject to the interpretation of the Supreme Court.

Important:
Article 31, which originally dealt with the Right to Property as a Fundamental Right, was omitted by the 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978. The Right to Property is now a constitutional right under Article 300A.

📌 Articles 33–35

Article 33: Parliament may modify the application of Fundamental Rights to members of the Armed Forces and certain other forces to ensure proper discharge of their duties and maintenance of discipline.

Article 34: Deals with restrictions on Fundamental Rights while martial law is in force in any area.

Article 35: Parliament has exclusive power to make laws on certain matters relating to Fundamental Rights, including matters specified in certain provisions of Part III.

📚 Important Amendments Related to Fundamental Rights

42nd Amendment, 1976: Made important changes affecting the relationship between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles.

44th Amendment, 1978: Removed the Right to Property from the list of Fundamental Rights.

86th Amendment, 2002: Inserted Article 21A, providing the Fundamental Right to Education for children between 6 and 14 years.
