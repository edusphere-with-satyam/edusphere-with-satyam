📘 DPSP & Constitutional Amendments

The Directive Principles of State Policy are contained in Part IV of the Constitution, Articles 36 to 51. Several Constitutional Amendments have changed, added or strengthened provisions connected with the DPSPs.

The most important amendments relating directly or closely to DPSPs are discussed below.

⭐ Important:

Among these, the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 is especially important because it inserted three new Directive Principles and made other significant changes.

1️⃣ 25th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971

Article 31C & Economic Justice

📜 Constitutional Provision

The 25th Amendment inserted Article 31C into the Constitution.

It originally provided protection to laws made to give effect to the principles contained in Article 39(b) and Article 39(c).

🔄 What Changed?

Laws made to implement Article 39(b) and 39(c) were given special constitutional protection against challenges based on certain Fundamental Rights.

🎯 Why was it important?

Article 39(b) deals with distribution of material resources for the common good.

Article 39(c) seeks to prevent concentration of wealth and means of production to the common detriment.

The amendment therefore attempted to give greater importance to these economic justice objectives.

⚖️ Important Result

The validity and scope of Article 31C were later examined by the Supreme Court in Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973).

🧠 Remember:

25th Amendment → Article 31C → Article 39(b) & 39(c)

2️⃣ 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976

⭐ Most Important Amendment for DPSPs

📜 Major DPSP Changes

The 42nd Amendment inserted:

Article 39A — Equal justice and free legal aid.

Article 43A — Workers' participation in management of industries.

Article 48A — Protection and improvement of environment and safeguarding forests and wildlife.

🔄 Change in Article 39(f)

The amendment substituted Article 39(f), strengthening the constitutional protection of children by emphasising their healthy development, freedom and dignity and protection against exploitation and abandonment.

⚖️ Expansion of Article 31C

The 42nd Amendment attempted to extend the protection of Article 31C from laws implementing only Article 39(b) and 39(c) to laws implementing any of the principles contained in Part IV.

🏛️ Supreme Court Result

In Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980), the Supreme Court struck down the expansion of Article 31C to all DPSPs.

The Court emphasised that the Constitution requires a balance between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles.

🎯 Why was it important?

The 42nd Amendment significantly expanded the constitutional importance of DPSPs by adding provisions relating to legal aid, workers' participation and environmental protection.

🧠 Remember:

42nd → 39A + 43A + 48A + Changed 39(f)

Justice → Workers → Environment

3️⃣ 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978

Article 38(2) — Reduction of Inequalities

📜 Constitutional Provision

The 44th Amendment inserted Article 38(2).

🔄 What Changed?

Article 38(2) directs the State to minimise inequalities in income and endeavour to eliminate inequalities in status, facilities and opportunities.

It specifically refers to inequalities not only among individuals but also among groups of people residing in different areas or engaged in different vocations.

🎯 Why was it important?

The amendment strengthened the constitutional objective of reducing social and economic inequalities.

🧠 Remember:

44th Amendment → Article 38(2) → Reduce Inequalities

4️⃣ 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992

🏘️ Panchayati Raj & Article 40

📜 Existing DPSP

Article 40 directs the State to organise village panchayats and give them the powers necessary to function as units of self-government.

🔄 What did the 73rd Amendment do?

The amendment inserted Part IX of the Constitution dealing with The Panchayats.

It created a constitutional framework for Panchayati Raj Institutions.

It also added the Eleventh Schedule, containing subjects that may be entrusted to Panchayats.

🎯 Why is it connected with DPSP?

Article 40 originally provided the constitutional direction for village self-government.

The 73rd Amendment gave Panchayats a detailed constitutional and institutional framework.

✅ Result

Panchayati Raj Institutions received constitutional recognition and a structured system for local self-government.

🧠 Remember:

Article 40 → Village Panchayats

73rd Amendment → Constitutional Panchayati Raj

5️⃣ 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002

📚 Education & Article 45

📜 Earlier Position

Before the amendment, Article 45 directed the State to endeavour to provide free and compulsory education for all children until they completed the age of fourteen years.

🔄 What Changed?

The 86th Amendment changed Article 45.

The present Article 45 directs the State to endeavour to provide early childhood care and education for all children until they complete the age of six years.

📚 Article 21A Added

The amendment inserted Article 21A as a Fundamental Right.

It provides for free and compulsory education for children in the age group of 6 to 14 years, in the manner determined by law.

🎯 Why was it important?

Education for children aged 6–14 was moved from the framework of a Directive Principle to an enforceable Fundamental Right under Article 21A.

At the same time, Article 45 was given a new focus on early childhood care and education below six years.

✅ Result

The amendment significantly strengthened the constitutional protection of children's education.

🧠 Remember:

86th → Article 21A + New Article 45

6–14 → Fundamental Right

Below 6 → Early Childhood Care & Education

6️⃣ 97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011

🤝 Article 43B — Cooperative Societies

📜 Constitutional Provision

The 97th Amendment inserted Article 43B into Part IV.

It directs the State to endeavour to promote the voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management of cooperative societies.

🔄 What Changed?

Cooperative societies received a specific place within the Directive Principles through Article 43B.

🎯 Why was it important?

The provision recognises cooperative societies as an important part of economic and social organisation.

⚖️ Important Constitutional Development

The 97th Amendment also introduced constitutional provisions concerning cooperative societies beyond Article 43B. However, the Supreme Court later examined the amendment's application to State cooperative societies in Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah (2021).

🧠 Remember:

97th Amendment → Article 43B → Cooperative Societies
