Section 1: Assertion–Reason Questions

Questions 1 to 10

Options:
(a) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(b) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, but Reason (R) is NOT the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(c) Assertion (A) is true, but Reason (R) is false.
(d) Assertion (A) is false, but Reason (R) is true.

Question 1

Assertion (A): Unexpected obsolescence of fixed capital assets is excluded from the estimation of Depreciation.
Reason (R): Depreciation accounts only for normal wear and tear and foreseen obsolescence during routine production.
Correct Answer: (a)
Unexpected obsolescence due to events such as natural disasters or fires is treated as Capital Loss. Depreciation measures normal wear and tear and foreseen obsolescence.

Question 2

Assertion (A): Goods purchased by a commercial firm for resale in the same year are treated as final goods.
Reason (R): Final goods are those that cross the boundary line of production and are ready for final use by end-consumers.
Correct Answer: (d)
Goods purchased for resale during the same accounting year are treated as intermediate goods because they are still part of the production/resale process.

Question 3

Assertion (A): Real GDP can increase even when the physical output of goods and services in an economy remains constant.
Reason (R): Real GDP is evaluated at constant base-year prices and eliminates the impact of inflation or price changes.
Correct Answer: (d)
Real GDP changes only when physical output changes because it is measured using constant base-year prices. Nominal GDP may rise because of price changes.

Question 4

Assertion (A): Interest paid by a commercial bank on deposits to a household is included in the estimation of National Income.
Reason (R): Households provide factor services (capital) to commercial banks, which use these funds for productive lending.
Correct Answer: (a)
Interest paid by commercial enterprises or financial intermediaries for productive use of funds is treated as factor payment and included under Operating Surplus.

Question 5

Assertion (A): Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) can be negative for an economy.
Reason (R): NFIA is negative when factor income earned by residents from the rest of the world is less than factor income paid to non-residents within the domestic territory.
Correct Answer: (a)
NFIA = Factor Income Received from Abroad − Factor Income Paid Abroad. If payments to non-residents exceed income received from abroad, NFIA becomes negative.

Question 6

Assertion (A): GDP is not an accurate indicator of economic welfare in the presence of positive or negative externalities.
Reason (R): Externalities refer to uncompensated benefits or harms caused by economic activities for which no market price is paid.
Correct Answer: (a)
GDP records market transactions but does not fully capture uncompensated benefits or harms such as pollution or public benefits. Therefore, GDP may not accurately represent economic welfare.

Question 7

Assertion (A): National Income is a flow concept.
Reason (R): Flow variables are measured over a period of time, such as an accounting year.
Correct Answer: (a)
National Income is measured over a period of time, generally one accounting year. Hence, it is a flow concept.

Question 8

Assertion (A): Dividend received by an Indian resident from a foreign company located in USA is included in India's Domestic Income.
Reason (R): Domestic Income includes all factor incomes generated strictly within the domestic territory of the country.
Correct Answer: (d)
Income earned outside India's domestic territory is not part of Domestic Income. It is factor income from abroad and is relevant for calculating National Income.

Question 9

Assertion (A): Compensation of Employees paid in kind, such as free rent-free accommodation, is included in National Income.
Reason (R): Factor income includes payments made to employees in cash, kind, or social security contributions for rendering productive labour.
Correct Answer: (a)
Compensation of Employees includes wages and salaries in cash, wages in kind or perquisites, and employers' contributions to social security schemes.

Question 10

Assertion (A): Old-age pensions disbursed by the government are excluded from National Income calculations.
Reason (R): Old-age pensions are transfer payments made without any rendering of productive factor services in return.
Correct Answer: (a)
Old-age pensions are transfer payments. They are not made in return for current productive factor services and therefore are excluded from National Income.

Section 2: Two-Statement Questions

Questions 11 to 20

Options:
(a) Both Statement I and Statement II are true.
(b) Both Statement I and Statement II are false.
(c) Statement I is true, but Statement II is false.
(d) Statement I is false, but Statement II is true.

Question 11

Statement I: The problem of double counting in National Income estimation can be avoided by taking the sum total of Gross Value Added at factor cost by all producing units.
Statement II: Intermediate consumption includes expenditure incurred on fixed capital assets like machinery and buildings.
Correct Answer: (c)
The Value Added Method avoids double counting. Fixed capital assets are capital expenditure/final investment and are not intermediate consumption.

Question 12

Statement I: Gross National Product at Market Price (GNPMP) is always greater than Gross Domestic Product at Market Price (GDPMP).
Statement II: Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) can be positive, negative, or zero.
Correct Answer: (d)
GNPMP = GDPMP + NFIA. If NFIA is negative, GNP can be less than GDP. NFIA may be positive, negative or zero.

Question 13

Statement I: Imputed rent of self-occupied houses is included in the estimation of National Income.
Statement II: Self-occupied houses provide housing services similar to rented houses, and their market value can be estimated reliably.
Correct Answer: (a)
Self-occupied houses provide housing services. Their imputed rent is included to account for the value of these services.

Question 14

Statement I: Sale of second-hand cars is excluded from National Income, but brokerage paid on such sales is included.
Statement II: Brokerage paid on second-hand items represents payment for a fresh productive service rendered in the current period.
Correct Answer: (a)
The second-hand car was already included when originally produced. However, brokerage represents a current productive service and is included.

Question 15

Statement I: Corporate tax, dividend, and undistributed profits are the three components of Operating Surplus.
Statement II: Profits earned by a firm are either paid out as corporate tax, distributed as dividends to shareholders, or retained as savings.
Correct Answer: (d)
Profit is only one component of Operating Surplus. Operating Surplus includes rent, royalty, interest and profit. Profit may be divided into corporate tax, dividends and undistributed profits.

Question 16

Statement I: Net Indirect Taxes (NIT) is calculated as Subsidies minus Indirect Taxes.
Statement II: If Subsidies exceed Indirect Taxes, Net Indirect Taxes will be negative, making Factor Cost greater than Market Price.
Correct Answer: (d)
NIT = Indirect Taxes − Subsidies. If subsidies exceed indirect taxes, NIT becomes negative and Factor Cost becomes greater than Market Price.

Question 17

Statement I: Capital Gain arising from the rise in market price of financial shares is counted in National Income.
Statement II: Capital gains reflect financial wealth appreciation without adding to the physical flow of current goods and services.
Correct Answer: (d)
Capital gains represent changes in the value of existing financial assets. They do not represent current production of goods and services and are therefore excluded from National Income.

Question 18

Statement I: If GDP Deflator is equal to 100, Nominal GDP and Real GDP of the economy are equal.
Statement II: GDP Deflator measures the ratio of Real GDP to Nominal GDP expressed as a percentage.
Correct Answer: (c)
GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100. When the deflator is 100, Nominal GDP equals Real GDP.

Question 19

Statement I: Non-monetary exchanges like barter transactions and kitchen gardening are included in National Income estimation due to their high production value.
Statement II: Non-monetary exchanges are left out of GDP calculations primarily due to the lack of non-market valuation data.
Correct Answer: (d)
Many non-monetary activities are difficult to value reliably because they do not have observable market prices. Therefore, they are generally excluded from official GDP estimates.

Question 20

Statement I: Interest on national debt (public debt) paid by the government to households is included in National Income.
Statement II: Government borrows funds for both productive and non-productive expenditures, so interest on national debt is treated as a transfer payment.
Correct Answer: (d)
According to the CBSE/NCERT treatment used for National Income questions, interest on public debt is treated as a transfer payment and is excluded from National Income.
