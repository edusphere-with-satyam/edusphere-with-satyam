Article 12 — Definition of “State”

Fundamental Rights | Constitution of India

← Back to Home

Part 1 — Quick Summary

Article 12 is the opening provision of Part III of the Constitution, which deals with Fundamental Rights. It defines the expression “State” for the purposes of Part III.

Under Article 12, “State” includes:

  • Government and Parliament of India
  • Government and Legislature of each State
  • Local Authorities within the territory of India
  • Other Authorities under the control of the Government of India
Key Idea:
Article 12 tells us which governmental authorities and certain government-controlled bodies can be treated as “State” for the purpose of Fundamental Rights.

The expression “State” is wider than merely the State Government. Through judicial interpretation, certain bodies functioning as an instrumentality or agency of the Government may also fall within Article 12.

Important cases include Rajasthan State Electricity Board v. Mohan Lal (1967), Ramana Dayaram Shetty v. International Airport Authority of India (1979), Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib Sehravardi (1981), Chander Mohan Khanna v. NCERT (1991) and Pradeep Kumar Biswas v. Union of India (2002).

One-line Revision:
Article 12 defines “State” for Part III and identifies the authorities against whom many Fundamental Rights can be enforced.

Part 2 — Detailed Explanation

1. Constitutional Provision

Article 12 provides:

“In this Part, unless the context otherwise requires, ‘the State’ includes the Government and Parliament of India and the Government and the Legislature of each of the States and all local or other authorities within the territory of India or under the control of the Government of India.”

The definition applies specifically to Part III — Fundamental Rights.

2. Components of “State”

A. Government and Parliament of India

This includes the Union Government and Parliament of India, including the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha.

B. Government and Legislature of each State

This includes:

  • State Governments
  • Legislative Assemblies
  • Legislative Councils, wherever they exist

C. Local Authorities

Local authorities are legally constituted bodies that perform functions within a particular local area.

Examples include:

  • Municipal Corporations
  • Municipalities
  • Panchayats
  • Other legally constituted local bodies

D. Other Authorities

This is the most important and judicially developed part of Article 12. The Constitution does not provide an exhaustive list of such authorities.

The Supreme Court has examined whether certain bodies are functioning as an instrumentality or agency of the Government.

Government connection alone is not sufficient. The Court examines the overall relationship between the body and the Government.

3. Meaning of “Other Authorities”

The expression “other authorities” has been interpreted broadly by the Supreme Court. Certain statutory corporations and government-controlled bodies may come within Article 12 depending upon the facts of the case.

The central question is whether the body is so closely controlled or dominated by the Government that it can be regarded as an instrumentality or agency of the State.

4. Judicial Tests

In Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib Sehravardi (1981), the Supreme Court identified several factors that may indicate that a body is an instrumentality or agency of the Government.

  • Whether the entire share capital is held by the Government.
  • Whether the Government provides substantial financial assistance.
  • Whether the body enjoys a State-conferred or State-protected monopoly.
  • Whether there is deep and pervasive Government control.
  • Whether the functions performed are of public importance and closely related to governmental functions.
  • Whether a Government department has been transferred to the body.
Important: These factors are indicative and are not rigid or conclusive tests. The overall circumstances must be considered.

5. Landmark Judgments

Rajasthan State Electricity Board v. Mohan Lal (1967)

The Supreme Court adopted a broad approach towards the expression “other authorities” and recognised that statutory authorities exercising powers conferred by law can fall within Article 12.

Sukhdev Singh v. Bhagatram (1975)

The Court considered statutory corporations and their relationship with the Government, contributing to the development of the concept of State instrumentalities.

Ramana Dayaram Shetty v. International Airport Authority of India (1979)

The Supreme Court developed the important concept of an instrumentality or agency of Government.

Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib Sehravardi (1981)

The Court developed several indicative factors for determining whether a body is an instrumentality or agency of the Government. This is one of the most important cases on Article 12.

Chander Mohan Khanna v. NCERT (1991)

The Court cautioned against treating every body receiving Government assistance or having some Government connection as “State”. Government assistance or regulation alone is not necessarily sufficient.

Pradeep Kumar Biswas v. Union of India (2002)

The Constitution Bench clarified that the factors identified in Ajay Hasia should not be applied mechanically. The Court must consider the cumulative effect of the relevant factors.

Important considerations include whether the body is financially, functionally and administratively dominated by the Government, with such control being deep and pervasive.

6. Article 12 and Private Bodies

A purely private body is not automatically treated as “State” merely because it performs a public activity or receives some Government assistance.

The nature and extent of Government control and the overall circumstances must be examined.

7. Is Article 12 itself a Fundamental Right?

No.

Article 12 does not itself grant a Fundamental Right. It defines the expression “State” for the purposes of Part III.

Remember:
Article 12 → Who is “State”?
Articles 13 onwards → Fundamental Rights and their protection.

8. Amendment History

Article 12 has not been substantially amended by a Constitutional Amendment. Its practical scope has mainly developed through judicial interpretation.

Exam Point:
No major constitutional amendment has expanded Article 12. Its scope has mainly evolved through Supreme Court judgments.

10. Exam-Oriented Key Points

  • Article 12 is contained in Part III.
  • It defines the expression “State” for Fundamental Rights.
  • It is an inclusive definition.
  • It includes the Union Government and Parliament.
  • It includes State Governments and State Legislatures.
  • It includes Local Authorities.
  • It includes certain “Other Authorities”.
  • “Other Authorities” has been substantially developed through judicial interpretation.
  • Ajay Hasia (1981) — important indicative tests.
  • Pradeep Kumar Biswas (2002) — cumulative effect and deep & pervasive control.
  • Mere regulatory control is generally not sufficient.
  • Article 12 itself does not confer a Fundamental Right.
