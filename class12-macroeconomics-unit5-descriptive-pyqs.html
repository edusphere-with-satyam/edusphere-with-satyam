🟦 Short Answer Questions — 3–4 Marks

3–4 Marks

Q1. Demand for Foreign Exchange

Why does the demand for foreign exchange rise when its price falls? Explain. (CBSE 2011, 2015, 2019)

Topic: Demand for Foreign Exchange
View Answer
Answer:

The demand for foreign exchange has an inverse relationship with its price.

  • When the price of foreign exchange falls, foreign goods and services become relatively cheaper for domestic residents.
  • This encourages people to increase imports and other foreign payments.
  • Therefore, the demand for foreign exchange increases.

Hence, price of foreign exchange ↓ → demand for foreign exchange ↑.

3–4 Marks

Q2. Supply of Foreign Exchange

Why does the supply of foreign exchange rise when its price rises? Explain. (CBSE 2011, 2014, 2018)

Topic: Supply of Foreign Exchange
View Answer
Answer:

The supply of foreign exchange generally has a direct relationship with its price.

  • When the price of foreign exchange rises, foreign currency receipts become more valuable in terms of domestic currency.
  • This encourages greater supply of foreign exchange through exports, foreign investment and other receipts.
  • Therefore, the supply of foreign exchange increases.

Hence, price of foreign exchange ↑ → supply of foreign exchange ↑.

3–4 Marks

Q5. Managed Floating Exchange Rate

Explain the concept of the Managed Floating Exchange Rate system. Why is it also known as "dirty floating"? (CBSE 2014, 2019, 2023)

Topic: Managed Floating Exchange Rate
View Answer
Answer:

A managed floating exchange rate is a system in which the exchange rate is mainly determined by market forces of demand and supply, but the Central Bank intervenes when necessary.

It is called "dirty floating" because the exchange rate is not allowed to move completely freely. The monetary authority may buy or sell foreign exchange to influence the rate.

3–4 Marks

Q6. Flexible Exchange Rate

State any two merits and two demerits of a Flexible Exchange Rate system. (CBSE 2011, 2016, 2018)

Topic: Flexible Exchange Rate
View Answer
Answer:

Merits:

  • It automatically adjusts according to changes in demand and supply of foreign exchange.
  • It reduces the need for continuous intervention by the Central Bank.

Demerits:

  • It may create uncertainty in international trade and investment.
  • Large fluctuations in exchange rates can affect exporters and importers.

6 Marks

Q7. Equilibrium Exchange Rate

Illustrate the determination of the equilibrium exchange rate with the help of a suitable diagram. What happens if the current exchange rate is higher than the equilibrium rate? (CBSE 2013, 2015, 2020)

Topic: Equilibrium Exchange Rate
View Answer
Answer:

The equilibrium exchange rate is determined at the point where the demand for foreign exchange equals its supply.

  • Demand for foreign exchange is generally downward sloping.
  • Supply of foreign exchange is generally upward sloping.
  • Their intersection determines the equilibrium exchange rate.

If the current exchange rate is higher than the equilibrium rate, there will be excess supply of foreign exchange. This creates pressure for the exchange rate to fall towards the equilibrium level.

Q8. Sources of Demand and Supply

Identify and explain three main sources of demand and three main sources of supply for foreign exchange. (CBSE 2012, 2014, 2017, 2022)

Topic: Demand and Supply of Foreign Exchange
View Answer
Answer:

Main Sources of Demand:

  • Imports: Payment for imports creates demand for foreign currency.
  • Foreign Travel: Residents need foreign currency for travelling abroad.
  • Foreign Investment: Domestic residents need foreign currency to invest abroad.

Main Sources of Supply:

  • Exports: Foreign buyers make payments for domestic exports.
  • Foreign Investment: Foreign investors bring foreign currency into the domestic economy.
  • Remittances: Money sent by residents working abroad increases the supply of foreign exchange.

Q9. Changes in Exchange Rate

Explain the impact of the following on the domestic exchange rate of a country:

(a) A sharp increase in imports from a foreign nation.
(b) A massive surge in Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) inflows. (CBSE 2016, 2019, 2024)

Topic: Demand & Supply of Foreign Exchange
View Answer
Answer:

(a) Increase in Imports:

Higher imports increase the demand for foreign exchange. Consequently, the domestic currency faces a tendency to depreciate.

Imports ↑ → Demand for Foreign Exchange ↑ → Depreciation of Domestic Currency

(b) Increase in FDI Inflows:

FDI inflows bring foreign currency into the domestic economy. Therefore, the supply of foreign exchange increases, creating pressure for the domestic currency to appreciate.

FDI Inflows ↑ → Supply of Foreign Exchange ↑ → Appreciation of Domestic Currency

6 Marks

Q10. Official Reserve Transactions

Discuss how the Central Bank uses official reserve transactions to stabilize a depreciating domestic currency under a managed float system. (CBSE 2018, 2020, 2023)

Topic: Managed Floating Exchange Rate
View Answer
Answer:

When the domestic currency is under depreciation pressure, the Central Bank can intervene in the foreign exchange market.

  • The Central Bank can sell foreign currency from its official reserves.
  • This increases the supply of foreign exchange in the market.
  • The increased supply reduces pressure on the price of foreign exchange.
  • As a result, the depreciation pressure on the domestic currency can be reduced.

Thus, official reserve transactions help the Central Bank stabilise the exchange rate under a managed floating system.

Q11. Appreciation and Revaluation

Differentiate between Appreciation and Revaluation of currency. How does currency appreciation affect a country's net exports? (CBSE 2015, 2017, 2022)

Topic: Appreciation, Revaluation & Net Exports
View Answer
Answer:
Appreciation Revaluation
Rise in the value of domestic currency due to market forces under a flexible exchange rate system. Official increase in the value of domestic currency by the monetary authority under a fixed exchange rate system.

When the domestic currency appreciates, domestic goods become relatively more expensive for foreigners while foreign goods become relatively cheaper for domestic consumers.

Therefore: Exports ↓ → Imports ↑ → Net Exports ↓

Q12. Spot Market and Forward Market

Explain the role of the Forward Market and Spot Market in foreign exchange transactions. How do they differ? (CBSE 2013, 2016)

Topic: Foreign Exchange Markets
View Answer
Answer:

Spot Market: It is a market where foreign exchange transactions are carried out for immediate delivery or settlement within the normal settlement period.

Forward Market: It is a market where a foreign exchange transaction is agreed upon today for delivery at a specified future date at a predetermined exchange rate.

Spot Market Forward Market
Exchange is for immediate/near-immediate settlement. Delivery takes place on a future agreed date.
Current exchange rate is used. Exchange rate is agreed in advance for future delivery.
