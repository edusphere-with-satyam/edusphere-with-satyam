🟦 Short Answer Questions — 3–4 Marks

3–4 Marks

Q1. Demand for Foreign Exchange

Why does the demand for foreign exchange rise when its price falls? Explain. (CBSE 2011, 2015, 2019)

Topic: Demand for Foreign Exchange
View Answer
Answer:

The demand for foreign exchange has an inverse relationship with its price.

  • When the price of foreign exchange falls, foreign goods and services become relatively cheaper for domestic residents.
  • This encourages people to increase imports and other foreign payments.
  • Therefore, the demand for foreign exchange increases.

Hence, price of foreign exchange ↓ → demand for foreign exchange ↑.

3–4 Marks

Q2. Supply of Foreign Exchange

Why does the supply of foreign exchange rise when its price rises? Explain. (CBSE 2011, 2014, 2018)

Topic: Supply of Foreign Exchange
View Answer
Answer:

The supply of foreign exchange generally has a direct relationship with its price.

  • When the price of foreign exchange rises, foreign currency receipts become more valuable in terms of domestic currency.
  • This encourages greater supply of foreign exchange through exports, foreign investment and other receipts.
  • Therefore, the supply of foreign exchange increases.

Hence, price of foreign exchange ↑ → supply of foreign exchange ↑.

Q5. Managed Floating Exchange Rate

Explain the concept of the Managed Floating Exchange Rate system. Why is it also known as "dirty floating"? (CBSE 2014, 2019, 2023)

Topic: Managed Floating Exchange Rate
View Answer
Answer:

A managed floating exchange rate is a system in which the exchange rate is mainly determined by market forces of demand and supply, but the Central Bank intervenes when necessary.

It is called "dirty floating" because the exchange rate is not allowed to move completely freely. The monetary authority may buy or sell foreign exchange to influence the rate.

Q7. Balance of Payments

What is meant by Balance of Payments? State its main components. (CBSE PYQ-Based)

Topic: Meaning and Components of BOP
View Answer
Answer:

Balance of Payments (BOP) is a systematic record of all economic transactions between the residents of a country and the rest of the world during a given period.

Its main components are:

  • Current Account: Records transactions relating to goods, services, income and transfers.
  • Capital Account: Records capital transactions such as borrowings, lending and other capital flows.
3–4 Marks

Q8. Balance of Trade

What is Balance of Trade? When is the Balance of Trade said to be in surplus and when is it said to be in deficit? (CBSE PYQ-Based)

Topic: Balance of Trade
View Answer
Answer:

Balance of Trade refers to the difference between the value of exports and imports of goods of a country during a given period.

  • BOT Surplus: When exports of goods are greater than imports of goods.
  • BOT Deficit: When imports of goods are greater than exports of goods.

BOT = Exports of Goods − Imports of Goods

View Answer

  • It may reduce the competitiveness of domestic exporters.

Q12. Devaluation of Currency

What is meant by devaluation of domestic currency? How can devaluation help in correcting a Balance of Payments deficit? (CBSE PYQ-Based)

Topic: Devaluation and BOP
View Answer
Answer:

Devaluation refers to an official reduction in the value of the domestic currency by the monetary authority under a fixed exchange rate system.

Devaluation makes domestic goods relatively cheaper for foreigners and foreign goods relatively expensive for domestic consumers.

Therefore, exports may increase and imports may decrease, which can help improve the Balance of Payments position.

Q7. Equilibrium Exchange Rate

Illustrate the determination of the equilibrium exchange rate with the help of a suitable diagram. What happens if the current exchange rate is higher than the equilibrium rate? (CBSE 2013, 2015, 2020)

Topic: Equilibrium Exchange Rate
View Answer
Answer:

The equilibrium exchange rate is determined at the point where the demand for foreign exchange equals its supply.

  • Demand for foreign exchange is generally downward sloping.
  • Supply of foreign exchange is generally upward sloping.
  • Their intersection determines the equilibrium exchange rate.

If the current exchange rate is higher than the equilibrium rate, there will be excess supply of foreign exchange. This creates pressure for the exchange rate to fall towards the equilibrium level.

Q8. Sources of Demand and Supply

Topic: Demand and Supply of Foreign Exchange
View Answer
Answer:

Main Sources of Demand:

  • Imports: Payment for imports creates demand for foreign currency.
  • Foreign Travel: Residents need foreign currency for travelling abroad.
  • Foreign Investment: Domestic residents need foreign currency to invest abroad.

Main Sources of Supply:

  • Exports: Foreign buyers make payments for domestic exports.
  • Foreign Investment: Foreign investors bring foreign currency into the domestic economy.
  • Remittances: Money sent by residents working abroad increases the supply of foreign exchange.

Q9. Changes in Exchange Rate

Explain the impact of the following on the domestic exchange rate of a country:

(a) A sharp increase in imports from a foreign nation.
(b) A massive surge in Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) inflows. (CBSE 2016, 2019, 2024)

Topic: Demand & Supply of Foreign Exchange
View Answer
Answer:

(a) Increase in Imports:

Higher imports increase the demand for foreign exchange. Consequently, the domestic currency faces a tendency to depreciate.

Imports ↑ → Demand for Foreign Exchange ↑ → Depreciation of Domestic Currency

(b) Increase in FDI Inflows:

FDI inflows bring foreign currency into the domestic economy. Therefore, the supply of foreign exchange increases, creating pressure for the domestic currency to appreciate.

FDI Inflows ↑ → Supply of Foreign Exchange ↑ → Appreciation of Domestic Currency

Q10. Official Reserve Transactions

Discuss how the Central Bank uses official reserve transactions to stabilize a depreciating domestic currency under a managed float system. (CBSE 2018, 2020, 2023)

Topic: Managed Floating Exchange Rate
View Answer
Answer:

When the domestic currency is under depreciation pressure, the Central Bank can intervene in the foreign exchange market.

  • The Central Bank can sell foreign currency from its official reserves.
  • This increases the supply of foreign exchange in the market.
  • The increased supply reduces pressure on the price of foreign exchange.
  • As a result, the depreciation pressure on the domestic currency can be reduced.

Thus, official reserve transactions help the Central Bank stabilise the exchange rate under a managed floating system.

Q13. Balance of Payments Deficit

Explain the meaning of a Balance of Payments deficit. Discuss any three measures to correct it. (CBSE PYQ-Based)

Topic: BOP Deficit and Corrective Measures
View Answer
Answer:

A Balance of Payments deficit occurs when the country's payments to the rest of the world exceed its receipts, creating an external financing requirement.

Measures to correct the deficit include:

  • Promoting Exports: Increasing exports can generate additional foreign exchange earnings.
  • Reducing Imports: Reducing unnecessary or excessive imports can lower the demand for foreign exchange.
  • Encouraging Foreign Investment: Greater inflows of foreign capital can increase the availability of foreign exchange.
  • Promoting Tourism: Foreign tourists spending in the country generate foreign exchange receipts.

Q14. Current Account Deficit

What is meant by Current Account Deficit? Explain the major causes of a Current Account Deficit. (CBSE PYQ-Based)

Topic: Current Account Deficit
View Answer
Answer:

A Current Account Deficit occurs when the payments recorded on the current account exceed the receipts during a given period.

Major causes include:

  • High Imports: A large increase in imports of goods can increase payments to foreign countries.
  • Low Exports: Weak export performance reduces foreign exchange receipts.
  • Higher Income Payments: Large payments of interest, profits or other income to foreigners can worsen the current account balance.
  • Increase in Transfer Payments Abroad: Higher outward transfers can also put pressure on the current account.

Q16. Depreciation and Net Exports

Explain how depreciation of domestic currency affects exports, imports and net exports of a country. (CBSE PYQ-Based)

Topic: Depreciation and Net Exports
View Answer
Answer:

Depreciation means a fall in the value of domestic currency under a flexible exchange rate system.

When domestic currency depreciates, domestic goods become relatively cheaper for foreign buyers, while foreign goods become relatively more expensive for domestic consumers.

  • Exports: May increase because domestic goods become cheaper for foreigners.
  • Imports: May decrease because imported goods become more expensive.
  • Net Exports: May increase because exports may rise while imports may fall.

Thus, depreciation can improve net exports, although the actual effect depends on the responsiveness of exports and imports to changes in relative prices.

Q17. Central Bank and Foreign Exchange Market

Explain how the Central Bank can influence the exchange rate by buying and selling foreign exchange in the market. (CBSE PYQ-Based)

Topic: Central Bank Intervention
View Answer
Answer:

Under a managed floating or fixed exchange rate system, the Central Bank may intervene in the foreign exchange market.

To reduce depreciation pressure:

The Central Bank can sell foreign currency from its reserves. This increases the supply of foreign exchange and can reduce pressure on the domestic currency.

To reduce appreciation pressure:

The Central Bank can purchase foreign currency from the market. This increases demand for foreign exchange and can reduce upward pressure on the domestic currency.

Therefore, buying and selling foreign exchange allows the Central Bank to influence exchange-rate movements.

Q18. Foreign Exchange Rate Systems

Explain the main features of Fixed, Flexible and Managed Floating Exchange Rate systems. (CBSE PYQ-Based)

Topic: Exchange Rate Systems
View Answer
Answer:

1. Fixed Exchange Rate:
The exchange rate is fixed or maintained by the monetary authority. The Central Bank intervenes in the foreign exchange market when required.

2. Flexible Exchange Rate:
The exchange rate is mainly determined by the forces of demand and supply of foreign exchange.

3. Managed Floating Exchange Rate:
The exchange rate is mainly determined by market forces, but the Central Bank intervenes when necessary to influence excessive fluctuations.

Quick Comparison:

Fixed → Central Bank maintains the rate

Flexible → Market forces determine the rate

Managed Float → Market forces + Central Bank intervention
