TOPIC 2

Income Determination and Multiplier

Academic Definition:
Income determination refers to the process through which the equilibrium level of national income and output is determined by the interaction between Aggregate Demand and Aggregate Supply in an economy.
In Simple Words:
An economy produces goods and services and generates income. People and firms spend this income on consumption and investment. The level of income at which planned expenditure becomes equal to the value of output supplied is called the equilibrium level of income.

The central idea of this topic is that the economy does not automatically operate at full employment. The equilibrium level of income depends mainly on the level of Aggregate Demand.

Core Idea for Exams:
In a simple two-sector economy, equilibrium income is determined where: Aggregate Demand = Aggregate Supply or equivalently, Saving = Investment
8.1

Meaning of Equilibrium Level of Income

Academic Definition:
The equilibrium level of income is the level of national income at which planned Aggregate Demand is equal to Aggregate Supply, so that producers have no tendency to increase or decrease the level of output.
Simple Explanation:
Think of equilibrium as a point of balance. Firms produce a certain amount of goods and services, while households and firms together plan to spend a certain amount. When planned spending exactly equals planned output, the economy is in equilibrium.

Why is it called equilibrium?

At equilibrium, producers are satisfied with their current level of production because the goods they plan to sell are equal to the goods demanded through planned expenditure.

Therefore, there is no reason for firms collectively to change their level of production merely because of an unintended accumulation or depletion of inventories.

Equilibrium Condition: AD = AS

Alternative Equilibrium Condition

In a two-sector economy, Aggregate Demand is:

AD = C + I

Aggregate Supply is:

AS = Y = C + S

Therefore, at equilibrium:

C + I = C + S

I = S
Important: The equality between saving and investment is an equilibrium condition in the simple two-sector model when we are considering planned/ex-ante saving and investment.
8.2

Determination of Equilibrium Income

In a simple two-sector economy, there are two major sectors:

🏠 Households

Households earn income by supplying factors of production. They use their income for consumption and saving.

Y = C + S

🏭 Firms

Firms produce goods and services and undertake investment expenditure on capital goods and inventories.

AD = C + I

Two Approaches to Determine Equilibrium Income

  1. Aggregate Demand – Aggregate Supply Approach
  2. Saving – Investment Approach

Both approaches lead to the same equilibrium level of income.

8.3

Equilibrium Income through AD–AS Approach

Meaning:
According to the AD–AS approach, equilibrium income is determined at the level where Aggregate Demand is equal to Aggregate Supply.
AD = AS

Step-by-Step Understanding

  1. Households receive income from production.
  2. A part of this income is spent on consumption.
  3. The remaining part is saved.
  4. Firms undertake investment expenditure.
  5. Consumption plus investment determines Aggregate Demand.
  6. Firms compare planned expenditure with the level of output.
  7. When AD becomes equal to AS, equilibrium income is established.

Example

Suppose an economy has the following Aggregate Demand schedule:

Income (Y) Consumption (C) Investment (I) AD = C + I AS = Y
100 120 40 160 100
200 180 40 220 200
300 240 40 280 300
400 300 40 340 400

The exact equilibrium level depends on the complete schedules and the point at which AD becomes equal to AS. The table above illustrates the comparison between planned expenditure and output.

Remember: If AD > AS, planned expenditure is greater than output. If AD < AS, planned expenditure is less than output. At AD = AS, the economy is in equilibrium.

8.4

Equilibrium Income through Saving–Investment Approach

Academic Definition:
Under the saving–investment approach, equilibrium level of income is determined at the level where planned saving is equal to planned investment.
S = I

Why does S = I determine equilibrium?

In a two-sector economy, households divide their income between consumption and saving. Firms undertake investment expenditure.

If planned saving is greater than planned investment, aggregate demand tends to be insufficient relative to output. If planned investment is greater than planned saving, aggregate demand tends to exceed output.

Therefore, the point where planned saving equals planned investment represents equilibrium.

Example

Income Saving (S) Investment (I) Situation
100 10 40 I > S
200 20 40 I > S
300 40 40 S = I
400 60 40 S > I

At an income level of ₹300 crore, planned saving equals planned investment. Hence, ₹300 crore is the equilibrium level of income in this illustration.

Exam Point:
The saving–investment approach and AD–AS approach are two different ways of explaining the same equilibrium condition in a simple two-sector model.
8.5

Under-employment Equilibrium

Academic Definition:
Under-employment equilibrium is a situation in which an economy reaches equilibrium at a level of output and employment lower than the full-employment level.
Simple Explanation:
An economy may be in balance even when some workers and productive resources are not fully employed. This means equilibrium does not necessarily mean full employment.

Key Idea

According to Keynesian analysis, Aggregate Demand may be insufficient to purchase the output that could be produced at full employment.

Firms therefore may not find it profitable to employ all available workers and resources. They reduce or maintain production at a lower level, and the economy may settle at an under-employment equilibrium.

Equilibrium ≠ Always Full Employment

Graphical Explanation

Under-employment Equilibrium
Important: The existence of equilibrium only means that planned expenditure equals output. It does not by itself guarantee that all available labour and resources are employed.
8.6

Full Employment Equilibrium

Meaning:
Full employment refers to a situation in which all those who are willing and able to work at the prevailing conditions can find employment, apart from normal frictional or structural changes associated with a dynamic economy.

When Aggregate Demand is sufficient to support output corresponding to full employment, the economy can reach equilibrium at the full-employment level.

Under-employment vs Full-employment Equilibrium

Basis Under-employment Equilibrium Full-employment Equilibrium
Output Below potential/full-employment output At full-employment output
Employment Some resources remain unemployed Available labour is employed subject to normal unemployment
Aggregate Demand Insufficient to support full-employment output Sufficient to support full-employment output
Key Idea Equilibrium with unemployment Equilibrium at full employment
8.7

Investment Multiplier

Academic Definition:
The investment multiplier measures the ratio of the change in equilibrium income to the change in investment that causes it.
Simple Explanation:
A small increase in investment can cause a much larger increase in national income because one person's expenditure becomes another person's income, a part of which is again spent on consumption.
K = ΔY / ΔI

Where:

  • K = Investment Multiplier
  • ΔY = Change in income
  • ΔI = Change in investment

Multiplier in Terms of MPC

In a simple two-sector model:

K = 1 / (1 − MPC)

Since:

MPS = 1 − MPC

Therefore:

K = 1 / MPS
Three important multiplier formulas:

K = ΔY / ΔI

K = 1 / (1 − MPC)

K = 1 / MPS
8.8

Working or Mechanism of the Multiplier

The multiplier operates because an initial increase in investment creates income for others. Those people spend a part of their additional income on consumption, creating income for another group. This process continues through successive rounds.

Step-by-Step Mechanism

  1. An increase in investment creates additional expenditure.
  2. The expenditure becomes income for producers and workers.
  3. Recipients spend a part of their additional income on consumption.
  4. This consumption expenditure becomes income for other producers and workers.
  5. The new recipients again spend a part of their additional income.
  6. The process continues, but each successive round becomes smaller because part of every additional income is saved.
  7. The total increase in income becomes a multiple of the initial increase in investment.
Initial Investment ↑ → Income ↑ → Consumption ↑ → Income ↑ → Further Consumption ↑

Numerical Illustration

Suppose the initial investment increases by ₹100 crore and MPC is 0.8.

K = 1 / (1 − 0.8)

K = 1 / 0.2

K = 5

Therefore:

ΔY = K × ΔI

ΔY = 5 × ₹100 crore

ΔY = ₹500 crore

Thus, an initial investment increase of ₹100 crore generates a total increase in equilibrium income of ₹500 crore, under the assumptions of the simple multiplier model.

Multiplier Process: Round-by-Round Explanation

Let the initial investment be ₹100 crore and MPC = 0.8.

Round Income Created Consumption (80%) Saving (20%)
Initial investment ₹100 ₹80 ₹20
Second round ₹80 ₹64 ₹16
Third round ₹64 ₹51.2 ₹12.8
Fourth round ₹51.2 ₹40.96 ₹10.24
Further rounds Continue Continue Continue

The successive rounds become smaller because each recipient saves a portion of additional income.

The sum of all these successive increases in income approaches the final multiplier effect.

Concept: The multiplier does not mean that every round creates the same amount of income. The rounds become progressively smaller.
8.9

Relationship Between Multiplier, MPC and MPS

K = 1 / (1 − MPC)

This shows that the multiplier has a direct relationship with MPC.

When MPC increases

A larger MPC means people spend a larger proportion of every additional unit of income. Therefore, more consumption expenditure is generated in every round, causing a larger multiplier.

When MPS increases

A larger MPS means people save a larger proportion of additional income. Therefore, less money is passed on to the next round of consumption, reducing the multiplier.

MPC MPS Multiplier
0.5 0.5 2
0.6 0.4 2.5
0.75 0.25 4
0.8 0.2 5
0.9 0.1 10
Conclusion:
Higher MPC → Higher Multiplier
Higher MPS → Lower Multiplier
8.10

Multiplier and Change in Equilibrium Income

The multiplier establishes a relationship between the change in investment and the resulting change in equilibrium income.

ΔY = K × ΔI

Example 1

If the investment increases by ₹50 crore and the multiplier is 4:

ΔY = 4 × ₹50 crore

ΔY = ₹200 crore

Therefore, equilibrium income increases by ₹200 crore.

Example 2

If MPC = 0.75 and investment increases by ₹100 crore:

K = 1 / (1 − 0.75)

K = 1 / 0.25

K = 4

Therefore:

ΔY = 4 × ₹100 crore

ΔY = ₹400 crore

Finding MPC from Multiplier

Starting with:

K = 1 / (1 − MPC)

We can rearrange the equation:

1 − MPC = 1 / K

MPC = 1 − 1/K

Example

If K = 5:

MPC = 1 − 1/5

MPC = 1 − 0.2

MPC = 0.8

Finding MPS from Multiplier

Since:

K = 1 / MPS

Therefore:

MPS = 1 / K

Example

If the multiplier is 4:

MPS = 1 / 4 = 0.25

Therefore:

MPS = 0.25 or 25%
8.11

Investment and Equilibrium Income

Autonomous investment is independent of the current level of national income in the simplified model. A change in autonomous investment shifts Aggregate Demand and changes the equilibrium level of income.

Increase in Investment

  1. Investment increases.
  2. Aggregate Demand increases.
  3. Firms receive higher demand for their output.
  4. Production and income increase.
  5. Higher income causes higher consumption.
  6. Higher consumption generates further income.
  7. The final increase in equilibrium income is greater than the initial increase in investment when the multiplier is greater than one.
Investment ↑ → AD ↑ → Income ↑ → Consumption ↑ → AD ↑ → Income ↑

Decrease in Investment

The multiplier process also works in the opposite direction. A fall in investment can lead to a multiplied decrease in equilibrium income.

Equilibrium: Important Situations

Situation Meaning Result
AD > AS Planned expenditure exceeds output Firms tend to increase production
AD < AS Planned expenditure is less than output Firms tend to reduce production
AD = AS Planned expenditure equals output Equilibrium
S > I Planned saving exceeds planned investment Aggregate demand tends to be insufficient
S < I Planned investment exceeds planned saving Aggregate demand tends to be greater than output
S = I Planned saving equals planned investment Equilibrium

BOARD QUESTIONS

Topic-wise Important Questions

Question 1. What is meant by equilibrium level of income?
▶ View Answer
The equilibrium level of income is the level of national income at which planned Aggregate Demand is equal to Aggregate Supply. In a simple two-sector economy:

AD = AS

At this level, producers have no tendency to change the level of output because planned expenditure is equal to the value of output supplied.
Question 2. Explain the condition of equilibrium income using the AD–AS approach.
▶ View Answer
In a simple two-sector economy:

AD = C + I

and:
AS = Y = C + S

At equilibrium:
AD = AS

Therefore:
C + I = C + S
I = S

Thus, equilibrium income is determined at the level where planned Aggregate Demand is equal to Aggregate Supply. The same equilibrium can also be expressed through the equality of planned saving and planned investment.
Question 3. Explain the saving-investment approach to determination of equilibrium income.
▶ View Answer
According to the saving-investment approach, equilibrium income is determined at the level where planned saving is equal to planned investment.

S = I

If planned investment is greater than planned saving, Aggregate Demand tends to exceed output and firms have an incentive to increase production. If planned saving is greater than planned investment, Aggregate Demand tends to be insufficient relative to output and firms have an incentive to reduce production. At the point where planned saving equals planned investment, the economy is in equilibrium.
Question 4. What is under-employment equilibrium? Explain.
▶ View Answer
Under-employment equilibrium is a situation in which the economy reaches equilibrium at a level of output below the full-employment level. According to Keynesian analysis, Aggregate Demand may be insufficient to purchase the level of output that could be produced at full employment. As a result, firms may not employ all available productive resources. Nevertheless, the economy can settle at an equilibrium level because planned Aggregate Demand becomes equal to Aggregate Supply at that lower level of output. Thus, equilibrium does not necessarily imply full employment.
Question 5. What is investment multiplier? State its formula.
▶ View Answer
The investment multiplier measures the ratio of the change in equilibrium income to the change in investment that causes it.

K = ΔY / ΔI

In a simple two-sector economy:
K = 1 / (1 − MPC)

Since MPS = 1 − MPC:
K = 1 / MPS
Question 6. Explain the working of investment multiplier.
▶ View Answer
The multiplier works through successive rounds of income and consumption. First, an increase in investment creates additional expenditure and income. The recipients of this additional income spend a part of it on consumption. This consumption expenditure becomes income for other producers and workers. The new recipients again spend a part of their additional income, creating further income. This process continues in successive rounds. Since a part of every additional income is saved, each successive round is smaller than the previous one. As a result, the total increase in income is several times the initial increase in investment.
Question 7. Explain the relationship between MPC and investment multiplier.
▶ View Answer
There is a direct relationship between MPC and the investment multiplier.

K = 1 / (1 − MPC)

When MPC increases, people spend a larger proportion of additional income on consumption. Therefore, more income is generated in successive rounds and the multiplier becomes larger. When MPC decreases, people spend a smaller proportion of additional income and save a larger proportion. Therefore, the multiplier becomes smaller. Hence:

Higher MPC → Higher Multiplier
Lower MPC → Lower Multiplier
Question 8. Explain the relationship between MPS and investment multiplier.
▶ View Answer
There is an inverse relationship between MPS and the investment multiplier.

K = 1 / MPS

When MPS increases, a larger part of additional income is saved. Consequently, less income is spent in subsequent rounds and the multiplier decreases. When MPS decreases, a smaller part is saved and a larger part is spent on consumption. This causes a larger multiplier. Therefore:

Higher MPS → Lower Multiplier
Lower MPS → Higher Multiplier
Question 9. Why is the multiplier greater than one?
▶ View Answer
The multiplier is generally greater than one in the simple Keynesian model because an initial increase in investment creates additional income, and a part of this additional income is repeatedly spent on consumption. Therefore, the final increase in income is greater than the initial increase in investment. For example, if MPC = 0.8:

K = 1 / (1 − 0.8) = 5

Thus, an initial increase in investment of ₹100 crore can generate a total increase in equilibrium income of ₹500 crore, subject to the assumptions of the model.
Question 10. Distinguish between under-employment equilibrium and full-employment equilibrium.
▶ View Answer
Under-employment equilibrium: The economy is in equilibrium at an output level below the full-employment level. Some productive resources may remain unemployed.

Full-employment equilibrium: The economy is in equilibrium at a level of output consistent with full employment.

The important point is that equilibrium itself does not automatically mean full employment.
Question 11. If MPC is 0.8, calculate the investment multiplier.
▶ View Answer
K = 1 / (1 − MPC)
K = 1 / (1 − 0.8)
K = 1 / 0.2
K = 5
Question 12. If the investment multiplier is 5, calculate MPC and MPS.
▶ View Answer

5 = 1 / MPS
MPS = 0.2

Since:
MPC + MPS = 1
MPC + 0.2 = 1

Therefore:
MPC = 0.8 and MPS = 0.2

EXAM ALERT

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Do not confuse equilibrium income with full-employment income.
  • Remember that equilibrium condition in the AD–AS approach is AD = AS.
  • In the saving-investment approach, the equilibrium condition is S = I.
  • Do not write K = MPC. The multiplier is related to MPC through: K = 1 / (1 − MPC).
  • Do not confuse MPC with MPS.
  • Remember: MPC + MPS = 1.
  • A high MPC produces a high multiplier.
  • A high MPS produces a low multiplier.
  • The successive rounds of the multiplier become smaller because a part of additional income is saved.
