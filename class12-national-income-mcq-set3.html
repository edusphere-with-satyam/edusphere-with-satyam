Part A – Assertion & Reason

Questions 1 to 10

Q1. Unexpected obsolescence of fixed capital assets is excluded from the estimation of Depreciation.

Assertion (A): Unexpected obsolescence of fixed capital assets is excluded from the estimation of Depreciation.
Reason (R): Depreciation accounts only for normal wear and tear and expected/foreseen obsolescence during normal production.
Correct Answer: (a)
Unexpected obsolescence caused by events such as natural disasters or fires is treated as a Capital Loss. Depreciation measures normal wear and tear and foreseen obsolescence.

Q2. Goods purchased by a commercial firm for resale in the same year are treated as final goods.

Assertion (A): Goods purchased by a commercial firm for resale in the same year are treated as final goods.
Reason (R): Final goods are those that cross the boundary line of production and are ready for final use by end-consumers.
Correct Answer: (d)
Goods purchased by a commercial firm for resale during the same accounting year are treated as intermediate goods because value is yet to be added to them.

Q3. Real GDP can increase even when the physical output of goods and services in an economy remains constant.

Assertion (A): Real GDP can increase even when the physical output of goods and services in an economy remains constant.
Reason (R): Real GDP is evaluated at constant base-year prices and eliminates the impact of inflation or price changes.
Correct Answer: (d)
Real GDP changes only when physical output changes because it is calculated at constant base-year prices. If physical output remains constant, Real GDP remains constant.

Q4. Interest paid by a commercial bank on deposits to a household is included in the estimation of National Income.

Assertion (A): Interest paid by a commercial bank on deposits to a household is included in the estimation of National Income.
Reason (R): Households provide factor services (capital) to commercial banks, which use these funds for productive lending.
Correct Answer: (a)
Interest paid by commercial enterprises or financial intermediaries on funds used for productive purposes is treated as factor payment and is included under Operating Surplus.

Q5. Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) can be negative for an economy.

Assertion (A): Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) can be negative for an economy.
Reason (R): NFIA is negative when factor income earned by residents from the rest of the world is less than factor income paid to non-residents within the domestic territory.
Correct Answer: (a)
NFIA = Factor Income Received from Abroad − Factor Income Paid to Abroad. If outgoing factor payments exceed incoming factor income, NFIA becomes negative.

Q6. GDP is not an accurate indicator of economic welfare in the presence of positive or negative externalities.

Assertion (A): GDP is not an accurate indicator of economic welfare in the presence of positive or negative externalities.
Reason (R): Externalities refer to the uncompensated benefits or harms caused by economic activities for which no market price is paid.
Correct Answer: (a)
GDP mainly measures market transactions. It does not fully account for uncompensated benefits or harms caused by externalities, so it may not accurately represent social welfare.

Q7. National Income is a flow concept.

Assertion (A): National Income is a flow concept.
Reason (R): Flow variables are measured over a period of time, such as an accounting year.
Correct Answer: (a)
National Income is measured over a period of time, generally one accounting year. Therefore, it is a flow concept.

Q8. Dividend received by an Indian resident from a foreign company located in USA is included in India's Domestic Income.

Assertion (A): Dividend received by an Indian resident from a foreign company located in USA is included in India's Domestic Income.
Reason (R): Domestic Income includes all factor incomes generated strictly within the political/economic boundary of the country.
Correct Answer: (d)
Income earned outside India's domestic territory is not part of India's Domestic Income. It is Factor Income from Abroad and is considered while calculating National Income.

Q9. Compensation of Employees paid in kind is included in National Income.

Assertion (A): Compensation of Employees paid in kind, such as rent-free accommodation, is included in National Income.
Reason (R): Factor income includes payments made to employees in cash, kind, or social security contributions for rendering productive labour.
Correct Answer: (a)
Compensation of Employees includes wages and salaries in cash, wages and salaries in kind such as perquisites, and employers' contribution to social security schemes.

Q10. Old-age pensions disbursed by the government are excluded from National Income calculations.

Assertion (A): Old-age pensions disbursed by the government are excluded from National Income calculations.
Reason (R): Old-age pensions are transfer payments made without any rendering of productive factor services in return.
Correct Answer: (a)
Old-age pensions are transfer payments. They are not made in exchange for current productive services, so they are excluded from National Income.

Part B – Two Statement Type

Questions 11 to 20

Directions: Choose the correct option.

(a) Both Statement I and Statement II are true.
(b) Both Statement I and Statement II are false.
(c) Statement I is true, but Statement II is false.
(d) Statement I is false, but Statement II is true.

Q11. Consider the following statements:

Statement I: The problem of double counting in National Income estimation can be avoided by taking the sum total of Gross Value Added at factor cost by all producing units.
Statement II: Intermediate consumption includes expenditure incurred on fixed capital assets like machinery and buildings.
Correct Answer: (c)
The Value Added Method avoids double counting. Fixed capital assets such as machinery and buildings are capital goods, not intermediate consumption.

Q12. Consider the following statements:

Statement I: Gross National Product at Market Price (GNPMP) is always greater than Gross Domestic Product at Market Price (GDPMP).
Statement II: Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) can be positive, negative, or zero.
Correct Answer: (d)
GNPMP = GDPMP + NFIA. If NFIA is negative, GNP can be less than GDP. NFIA can be positive, negative, or zero.

Q13. Consider the following statements:

Statement I: Imputed rent of self-occupied houses is included in the estimation of National Income.
Statement II: Self-occupied houses provide housing services similar to rented houses, and their market value can be estimated reliably.
Correct Answer: (a)
Self-occupied houses provide housing services to their owners. The estimated/imputed rent of these services is included in National Income.

Q14. Consider the following statements:

Statement I: Sale of second-hand cars is excluded from National Income, but brokerage paid on such sales is included.
Statement II: Brokerage paid on second-hand items represents payment for a fresh productive service rendered in the current period.
Correct Answer: (a)
The second-hand car was already counted when it was originally produced. However, brokerage is payment for a fresh service rendered during the current period and is included.

Q15. Consider the following statements:

Statement I: Corporate tax, dividend, and undistributed profits are the three components of Operating Surplus.
Statement II: Profits earned by a firm are either paid out as corporate tax, distributed as dividends to shareholders, or retained as savings.
Correct Answer: (d)
Corporate tax, dividends and undistributed profits together form the components of profit. Operating Surplus also includes rent, royalty and interest. Therefore, Statement I is incomplete/false, while Statement II is true.

Q16. Consider the following statements:

Statement I: Net Indirect Taxes (NIT) is calculated as Subsidies minus Indirect Taxes.
Statement II: If Subsidies exceed Indirect Taxes, Net Indirect Taxes will be negative, making Factor Cost greater than Market Price.
Correct Answer: (d)
NIT = Indirect Taxes − Subsidies. If subsidies exceed indirect taxes, NIT becomes negative. Since Factor Cost = Market Price − NIT, Factor Cost becomes greater than Market Price.

Q17. Consider the following statements:

Statement I: Capital Gain arising from the rise in market price of financial shares is counted in National Income.
Statement II: Capital gains reflect financial wealth appreciation without adding to the physical flow of current goods and services.
Correct Answer: (d)
Capital gains represent an increase in financial wealth but do not result from current production of goods or services. Therefore, they are excluded from National Income.

Q18. Consider the following statements:

Statement I: If GDP Deflator is equal to 100, Nominal GDP and Real GDP of the economy are equal.
Statement II: GDP Deflator measures the ratio of Real GDP to Nominal GDP expressed as a percentage.
Correct Answer: (c)
GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100. When the deflator is 100, Nominal GDP and Real GDP are equal. Therefore, Statement I is true and Statement II is false.

Q19. Consider the following statements:

Statement I: Non-monetary exchanges like barter transactions and kitchen gardening are included in National Income estimation due to their high production value.
Statement II: Non-monetary exchanges are left out of GDP calculations primarily due to the lack of non-market valuation data.
Correct Answer: (d)
Many non-monetary activities are excluded from official GDP estimates because their market value is difficult to measure reliably. Hence, Statement I is false and Statement II is true.

Q20. Consider the following statements:

Statement I: Interest on national debt (public debt) paid by the government to households is included in National Income.
Statement II: Government borrows funds for both productive and non-productive expenditures, so interest on national debt is treated as a transfer payment.
Correct Answer: (d)
According to the CBSE/NCERT treatment used for National Income, interest on public debt is treated as a transfer payment and is excluded from National Income because it is not considered payment for current productive factor services.
