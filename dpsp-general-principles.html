📘 Articles 36–39

Articles 36 to 39 form the basic foundation of the Directive Principles of State Policy. They explain the meaning of the State, the constitutional nature of DPSPs, the welfare of the people, reduction of inequalities and important principles of social and economic justice.

Covered in this section:
Article 36 → Definition of State
Article 37 → Application of DPSP
Article 38 → Welfare and reduction of inequalities
Article 39 → Principles of economic and social justice

🏛️ Article 36 — Definition

Article 36
Constitutional Provision

“In this Part, unless the context otherwise requires, ‘the State’ has the same meaning as in Part III.”

Explanation

For the purpose of the Directive Principles, the expression “the State” carries the same meaning as it does under Part III of the Constitution, particularly Article 12.

Therefore, the expression generally covers the Union Government, Parliament, State Governments, State Legislatures, local authorities and other authorities falling within the constitutional meaning of “State”.

🎯 Important Point: Article 36 connects the meaning of “State” in DPSP with its meaning under Part III.

⚖️ Article 37 — Application of the Principles

Article 37
Constitutional Provision

“The provisions contained in this Part shall not be enforceable by any court, but the principles therein laid down are nevertheless fundamental in the governance of the country and it shall be the duty of the State to apply these principles in making laws.”

Explanation

DPSPs are not enforceable by courts. This means that a person cannot normally ask a court to directly enforce a Directive Principle.

However, the Constitution still considers these principles fundamental in the governance of the country. The State has a constitutional duty to keep them in mind while making laws and policies.

💡 Example

The Constitution directs the State to promote social and economic welfare. A citizen cannot ordinarily demand direct judicial enforcement of such a directive, but governments are expected to consider it while framing laws and policies.

🎯 Key Point:
Not enforceable by courts + Fundamental in governance + Duty of the State to apply them.

⚖️ Article 38 — State to Secure a Social Order for the Promotion of Welfare of the People

Article 38(1)

Promotion of Welfare and Justice

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall strive to promote the welfare of the people by securing and protecting as effectively as it may a social order in which justice, social, economic and political, shall inform all the institutions of the national life.”

Explanation

The State should work for the welfare of the people and create a social order based on three forms of justice: social justice, economic justice and political justice.

These principles should guide the institutions and functioning of national life.

💡 Example

Policies aimed at improving access to education, healthcare, employment and social welfare can contribute towards the welfare and justice contemplated by Article 38.

Article 38(2)

Reduction of Inequalities

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall, in particular, strive to minimise the inequalities in income, and endeavour to eliminate inequalities in status, facilities and opportunities, not only amongst individuals but also amongst groups of people residing in different areas or engaged in different vocations.”

Explanation

The State should work to reduce inequalities in income, status, facilities and opportunities.

This objective applies not only to individuals but also to groups living in different regions or working in different occupations.

💡 Example

Providing better educational, healthcare and infrastructure facilities in relatively underdeveloped regions can help reduce regional inequalities.

🎯 Key Point:
Article 38 → Welfare + Social Justice + Economic Justice + Political Justice + Reduction of Inequalities.

📋 Article 39 — Certain Principles of Policy to be Followed by the State

Article 39 contains six important clauses, from (a) to (f). These principles mainly deal with livelihood, distribution of resources, economic power, workers and children.

Article 39(a)

👥 Adequate Means of Livelihood

Constitutional Provision

“That the citizens, men and women equally, have the right to an adequate means of livelihood;”

Explanation

The State should create conditions in which both men and women have adequate opportunities to earn their livelihood.

💡 Example:

Employment and livelihood programmes designed to provide people with opportunities to earn a decent living reflect this principle.

Article 39(b)

🌍 Distribution of Material Resources

Constitutional Provision

“That the ownership and control of the material resources of the community are so distributed as best to subserve the common good;”

Explanation

Material resources important to the community should be managed and distributed in a manner that serves the common good.

💡 Example:

The use and management of important natural resources should take into account the wider public interest.

Article 39(c)

💰 Prevention of Concentration of Wealth

Constitutional Provision

“That the operation of the economic system does not result in the concentration of wealth and means of production to the common detriment;”

Explanation

The economic system should not allow wealth and control over the means of production to become concentrated in a way that harms the common interest.

🎯 Key Point:
39(b) → Distribution of resources for common good
39(c) → Prevention of harmful concentration of wealth and economic power
Article 39(d)

⚖️ Equal Pay for Equal Work

Constitutional Provision

“That there is equal pay for equal work for both men and women;”

Explanation

Men and women performing equal work should receive equal remuneration. The principle seeks to prevent discrimination in pay on the ground of sex.

💡 Example:

If male and female employees perform substantially equal work under comparable conditions, they should not be paid differently merely because of their sex.

Article 39(e)

🛡️ Protection of Workers and Children

Constitutional Provision

“That the health and strength of workers, men and women, and the tender age of children are not abused and that citizens are not forced by economic necessity to enter avocations unsuited to their age or strength;”

Explanation

The State should protect the health and strength of workers and children. Poverty or economic necessity should not force a person to undertake work unsuitable for their age or physical capacity.

💡 Example:

Labour laws and child-protection measures that prevent exploitation and unsafe employment reflect this constitutional principle.

Article 39(f)

👶 Protection and Development of Children

Constitutional Provision

“That children are given opportunities and facilities to develop in a healthy manner and in conditions of freedom and dignity and that childhood and youth are protected against exploitation and against moral and material abandonment.”

Explanation

Children should receive proper opportunities and facilities for healthy development. They should grow in conditions of freedom and dignity and be protected from exploitation and abandonment.

💡 Example:

Measures for child protection, education, nutrition and prevention of child exploitation support the objective of Article 39(f).

🎯 Key Point:
39(f) → Healthy development + Freedom + Dignity + Protection from exploitation and abandonment.
