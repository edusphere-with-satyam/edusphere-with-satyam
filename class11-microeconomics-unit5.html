📘 Unit 5 — Market Equilibrium

What you will learn:
Market, demand, supply, equilibrium price, equilibrium quantity, excess demand, excess supply, determination of equilibrium, changes in demand, changes in supply, simultaneous changes in demand and supply, price ceiling, price floor and numerical applications.

Market equilibrium is an important concept in microeconomics. It explains how the forces of demand and supply interact to determine the market price and quantity of a commodity.

1. Market

🏪 Meaning of Market

Definition 1:
A market is an arrangement through which buyers and sellers interact to exchange goods and services.
Definition 2:
A market refers to a system where buyers and sellers determine the price and quantity of a commodity through their interaction.
Simple meaning:
A market does not necessarily mean a physical place. Buyers and sellers can interact through shops, online platforms, telephones or other means.
Example:
The market for mobile phones includes consumers who want to buy phones and producers or sellers who offer phones for sale.
2. Demand

📉 Demand

Definition 1:
Demand refers to the quantity of a commodity that a consumer is willing and able to purchase at different prices during a given period.
Definition 2:
Demand is the quantity of a good that consumers are prepared to buy at a particular price, during a particular period of time.
Remember:
Demand requires both willingness and ability to pay.
3. Law of Demand

📉 Law of Demand

Definition 1:
Other things remaining constant, quantity demanded of a commodity falls when its price rises and rises when its price falls.
Definition 2:
There is generally an inverse relationship between the price of a commodity and its quantity demanded, ceteris paribus.
Price ↑ → Quantity Demanded ↓

Price ↓ → Quantity Demanded ↑
Example:
If the price of a notebook decreases from ₹50 to ₹40, consumers may purchase more notebooks, assuming other factors remain unchanged.
4. Supply

📈 Supply

Definition 1:
Supply refers to the quantity of a commodity that producers are willing and able to sell at different prices during a given period.
Definition 2:
Supply is the quantity of a commodity that sellers are prepared to offer for sale at a particular price during a particular period.
5. Law of Supply

📈 Law of Supply

Definition 1:
Other things remaining constant, quantity supplied of a commodity increases when its price increases and decreases when its price falls.
Definition 2:
There is generally a direct relationship between price and quantity supplied, ceteris paribus.
Price ↑ → Quantity Supplied ↑

Price ↓ → Quantity Supplied ↓
6. Market Demand and Supply

👥 Market Demand and Market Supply

Market Demand:
Market demand is the total quantity demanded by all consumers in the market at different prices.
Market Supply:
Market supply is the total quantity supplied by all producers or sellers in the market at different prices.
Market Demand = Sum of individual demands

Market Supply = Sum of individual supplies
7. Market Equilibrium

⚖️ Market Equilibrium

Definition 1:
Market equilibrium is a situation where quantity demanded is equal to quantity supplied.
Definition 2:
Market equilibrium is the state in which the plans of buyers and sellers are mutually consistent at a particular price.
Equilibrium Condition:

Qd = Qs
At equilibrium, there is neither excess demand nor excess supply.
8. Equilibrium Price

💰 Equilibrium Price

Definition 1:
Equilibrium price is the price at which quantity demanded is equal to quantity supplied.
Definition 2:
The equilibrium price is the market price at which the demand and supply plans of buyers and sellers are balanced.
At equilibrium price:

Quantity Demanded = Quantity Supplied
9. Equilibrium Quantity

📦 Equilibrium Quantity

Definition 1:
Equilibrium quantity is the quantity bought and sold at the equilibrium price.
Definition 2:
It is the quantity of a commodity corresponding to the price at which market demand equals market supply.
10. Equilibrium Table

📊 Determination of Market Equilibrium

Equilibrium:
At ₹30, Demand = 60 and Supply = 60. Therefore:

Equilibrium Price = ₹30

11. Excess Demand

🔴 Excess Demand

Definition 1:
Excess demand occurs when quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied at a particular price.
Definition 2:
Excess demand is the situation in which buyers want to purchase more than sellers are willing to supply at the existing price.
Excess Demand = Qd − Qs
If demand = 100 units and supply = 60 units:

Excess Demand = 100 − 60 = 40 units
Excess demand generally creates upward pressure on price.
12. Excess Supply

🟠 Excess Supply

Definition 1:
Excess supply occurs when quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded at a particular price.
Definition 2:
Excess supply is the situation in which sellers want to sell more than buyers are willing to purchase at the existing price.
Excess Supply = Qs − Qd
If supply = 100 units and demand = 60 units:

Excess Supply = 100 − 60 = 40 units
Excess supply generally creates downward pressure on price.
13. Price Adjustment

🔄 Adjustment Towards Equilibrium

Situation Condition Pressure on Price
Excess Demand Qd > Qs Price tends to rise
Equilibrium Qd = Qs No pressure for change
Excess Supply Qs > Qd Price tends to fall
The market tends to move towards equilibrium through changes in price.
14. Change in Demand

📉 Changes in Demand and Equilibrium

Increase in Demand

An increase in demand means that consumers demand more at every given price.
Effect, other things remaining constant:
Demand ↑ → Equilibrium Price ↑
Demand ↑ → Equilibrium Quantity ↑

Decrease in Demand

A decrease in demand means that consumers demand less at every given price.
Effect, other things remaining constant:
Demand ↓ → Equilibrium Price ↓
Demand ↓ → Equilibrium Quantity ↓
15. Change in Supply

📈 Changes in Supply and Equilibrium

Increase in Supply

An increase in supply means that producers offer more at every given price.
Effect, other things remaining constant:
Supply ↑ → Equilibrium Price ↓
Supply ↑ → Equilibrium Quantity ↑

Decrease in Supply

A decrease in supply means that producers offer less at every given price.
Effect, other things remaining constant:
Supply ↓ → Equilibrium Price ↑
Supply ↓ → Equilibrium Quantity ↓
16. Simultaneous Changes

🔄 Simultaneous Changes in Demand and Supply

Change Effect on Price Effect on Quantity
Demand ↑ + Supply ↑ Uncertain Quantity increases
Demand ↓ + Supply ↓ Uncertain Quantity decreases
Demand ↑ + Supply ↓ Price increases Uncertain
Demand ↓ + Supply ↑ Price decreases Uncertain
When demand and supply change simultaneously, the final effect depends on the relative magnitude of the changes.
17. Price Ceiling

🔒 Price Ceiling

Definition 1:
Price ceiling is the maximum price legally permitted to be charged for a commodity.
Definition 2:
A price ceiling is a government-imposed upper limit on the price of a good or service.
A price ceiling becomes effective when it is fixed below the equilibrium price.
Example:
Suppose the equilibrium price of a commodity is ₹100 and the government fixes a maximum price of ₹80. Consumers may demand more while producers may supply less, creating a shortage.
18. Shortage

⚠️ Shortage Under Price Ceiling

Definition 1:
Shortage occurs when quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied.
Definition 2:
A shortage is a situation in which available supply is insufficient to satisfy demand at the existing price.
Shortage = Quantity Demanded − Quantity Supplied
Binding price ceiling → Excess Demand → Shortage
19. Price Floor

🛡️ Price Floor

Definition 1:
Price floor is the minimum price legally permitted to be charged for a commodity.
Definition 2:
A price floor is a government-imposed lower limit below which the price of a good or service cannot legally fall.
A price floor becomes effective when it is fixed above the equilibrium price.
Example:
Minimum support price for agricultural products is an example of government price intervention.
20. Surplus

📦 Surplus Under Price Floor

Definition 1:
Surplus occurs when quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded.
Definition 2:
A surplus is a situation in which sellers offer more of a commodity than buyers are willing to purchase at the existing price.
Surplus = Quantity Supplied − Quantity Demanded
Binding price floor → Excess Supply → Surplus

📋 Price Ceiling vs Price Floor

Basis Price Ceiling Price Floor
Meaning Maximum legal price Minimum legal price
Effective when Below equilibrium price Above equilibrium price
Main effect Excess demand Excess supply
Result Shortage Surplus
22. Numerical Application

🧮 Finding Equilibrium Price and Quantity

Suppose:

Qd = 100 − 2P
Qs = 20 + 2P
At equilibrium:

Qd = Qs

100 − 2P = 20 + 2P

100 − 20 = 2P + 2P

80 = 4P

P = 20
Equilibrium Price = ₹20
Now put P = 20 in Qd:

Qd = 100 − 2(20)
Qd = 100 − 40
Qd = 60
Equilibrium Quantity = 60 units

⭐ Most Important Relationships

1. Equilibrium occurs when Qd = Qs.
2. Excess demand occurs when Qd > Qs.
3. Excess supply occurs when Qs > Qd.
4. Excess demand creates upward pressure on price.
5. Excess supply creates downward pressure on price.
6. Increase in demand generally raises equilibrium price and quantity.
7. Decrease in demand generally lowers equilibrium price and quantity.
8. Increase in supply generally lowers equilibrium price and raises quantity.
9. Decrease in supply generally raises equilibrium price and lowers quantity.
10. Price ceiling below equilibrium creates shortage.
11. Price floor above equilibrium creates surplus.

🎯 Important Exam Points

  • Market equilibrium is determined by the interaction of demand and supply.
  • Equilibrium price is the price at which Qd = Qs.
  • Equilibrium quantity is the quantity exchanged at equilibrium price.
  • Excess demand means Qd > Qs.
  • Excess supply means Qs > Qd.
  • Price tends to rise in case of excess demand.
  • Price tends to fall in case of excess supply.
  • A binding price ceiling creates shortage.
  • A binding price floor creates surplus.
  • Price ceiling is a maximum legal price.
  • Price floor is a minimum legal price.
  • Market equilibrium can change when demand or supply changes.
