Unit 5 — Green Skills–I

Green Skills–I introduces students to environmental awareness, sustainable development, conservation of natural resources, waste management, energy conservation, green economy and responsible environmental practices.

Unit: Green Skills–I
Part: Part A — Employability Skills
Class: IX

1. Introduction to Green Skills

Definition

Definition 1: Green skills are the knowledge, abilities, values and attitudes that help individuals perform activities in an environmentally responsible manner and contribute towards the protection of natural resources and the environment.

Definition 2: Green skills are the skills required to understand environmental problems, use resources efficiently, reduce waste and pollution, and support sustainable development.

In simple words: Green skills mean knowing how to use natural resources carefully, reduce harm to the environment and adopt practices that are better for the present and future generations.

Importance of Green Skills

  • Help protect the environment.
  • Encourage responsible use of natural resources.
  • Help reduce pollution and waste.
  • Promote energy and water conservation.
  • Support sustainable development.
  • Prepare students for future green jobs.
Example: Switching off lights and computers when they are not required is a simple green practice.

2. Environment and Sustainable Development

Meaning of Environment

Definition 1: The environment is the surrounding in which living organisms live and interact with one another and with physical, biological and social factors.

Definition 2: Environment includes all the natural and human-made conditions around us, such as air, water, soil, plants, animals and human activities.

Components of Environment

  • Air
  • Water
  • Soil
  • Plants
  • Animals
  • Human beings
  • Other living and non-living elements

Sustainable Development

Definition 1: Sustainable development means development that meets the needs of the present generation without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

Definition 2: Sustainable development is a balanced approach to development in which economic growth, social well-being and environmental protection are considered together.

Need for Sustainable Development

  • Natural resources are limited.
  • Pollution is increasing.
  • Population and consumption are increasing.
  • Future generations also need natural resources.
  • Environmental balance must be maintained.

3. Natural Resources

Definition

Natural resources are materials and sources obtained from nature that are useful for human beings and other living organisms.

Examples of Natural Resources

  • Water
  • Air
  • Soil
  • Forests
  • Minerals
  • Sunlight
  • Fuels

Renewable Resources

Renewable resources are resources that can naturally replenish themselves within a reasonable period when they are used responsibly.

Examples: Sunlight, wind and water.

Non-Renewable Resources

Non-renewable resources are resources that exist in limited quantities and take a very long time to form again.

Examples: Coal, petroleum and natural gas.

Conservation of Natural Resources

  • Save water.
  • Reduce unnecessary electricity consumption.
  • Plant and protect trees.
  • Use resources efficiently.
  • Reduce unnecessary consumption.
  • Reuse materials whenever possible.

4. Environmental Problems

Environmental problems are harmful changes in the natural surroundings that affect human beings, animals, plants and natural ecosystems.

Major Environmental Problems

  • Air pollution
  • Water pollution
  • Soil pollution
  • Noise pollution
  • Deforestation
  • Waste accumulation

Air Pollution

Air pollution occurs when harmful substances such as smoke, dust and harmful gases enter the atmosphere in quantities that can affect living organisms and the environment.

Water Pollution

Water pollution occurs when harmful substances such as waste, chemicals and pollutants contaminate rivers, lakes, groundwater or other water sources.

Soil Pollution

Soil pollution occurs when harmful chemicals, waste or other pollutants reduce the quality and usefulness of soil.

Noise Pollution

Noise pollution refers to excessive or unwanted sound that may disturb people, animals and the surrounding environment.

Basic Preventive Measures

  • Use public transport whenever possible.
  • Avoid unnecessary burning of waste.
  • Dispose of waste properly.
  • Reduce plastic use.
  • Plant and protect trees.
  • Keep water sources clean.

5. Waste Management

Definition

Definition 1: Waste management is the systematic process of collecting, separating, treating, reusing, recycling and safely disposing of waste.

Definition 2: Waste management means handling waste in a responsible manner so that its harmful effects on human health and the environment are reduced.

Types of Waste

  • Biodegradable waste
  • Non-biodegradable waste
  • Electronic waste
  • Household waste
  • Industrial waste

Biodegradable Waste

Biodegradable waste can be broken down naturally by microorganisms over time.

Examples: Vegetable peels, food waste and leaves.

Non-Biodegradable Waste

Non-biodegradable waste does not break down easily through natural biological processes and may remain in the environment for a long time.

Examples: Many plastics, glass and certain synthetic materials.

The 3Rs

  • Reduce: Use fewer resources and avoid unnecessary consumption.
  • Reuse: Use an item again instead of throwing it away.
  • Recycle: Process waste materials so that they can be used to make new products.

E-Waste

E-waste means discarded electrical and electronic equipment, such as old computers, keyboards, mobile phones, printers and other electronic devices.

E-waste should be handled responsibly and sent to appropriate collection or recycling systems instead of being disposed of carelessly.

6. Energy Conservation

Definition

Energy conservation means reducing unnecessary energy use and using available energy efficiently without affecting essential activities.

Renewable Energy Sources

  • Solar energy
  • Wind energy
  • Hydropower
  • Biomass energy

Ways to Save Energy

  • Switch off lights when not required.
  • Turn off computers after use.
  • Use energy-efficient appliances.
  • Avoid unnecessary use of electricity.
  • Use natural light whenever possible.
  • Use fuel efficiently.

7. Green Economy and Green Jobs

Green Economy

Definition 1: A green economy is an economy that aims to improve human well-being and economic development while reducing environmental risks and ecological damage.

Definition 2: A green economy promotes efficient use of resources, reduction of pollution and development of economic activities that support environmental sustainability.

Green Jobs

Definition: Green jobs are jobs that contribute to preserving or restoring the environment and improving the efficient use of natural resources.

Examples of Green Jobs

  • Solar energy technician
  • Environmental consultant
  • Waste management worker
  • Recycling professional
  • Energy auditor
  • Environmental scientist

Importance of Green Jobs

  • Create employment opportunities.
  • Support environmental protection.
  • Promote clean technologies.
  • Encourage efficient use of resources.
  • Support sustainable economic development.

8. Role of Individuals in Environmental Protection

Environmental protection is not only the responsibility of governments and organisations. Every individual can contribute by adopting responsible habits in daily life.

  • Save water.
  • Save electricity.
  • Reduce plastic consumption.
  • Use public transport when practical.
  • Plant and protect trees.
  • Keep surroundings clean.
  • Separate waste properly.
  • Use electronic devices responsibly.
  • Follow the principles of Reduce, Reuse and Recycle.

9. Green Practices in School and Workplace

  • Use paper carefully and avoid unnecessary printing.
  • Use digital documents whenever appropriate.
  • Switch off lights and fans when not required.
  • Shut down computers properly after work.
  • Keep separate bins for different types of waste.
  • Reduce unnecessary use of plastic.
  • Reuse materials whenever possible.
  • Maintain clean and healthy surroundings.
  • Dispose of electronic waste responsibly.
Example: A school can reduce paper consumption by sharing notices digitally whenever appropriate and by printing only necessary documents.

10. Quick Revision

  • Green Skills → Skills that support environmental responsibility and sustainability.
  • Sustainable Development → Development that considers present and future needs.
  • Natural Resources → Useful materials and sources obtained from nature.
  • Renewable Resources → Resources that can naturally replenish.
  • Non-Renewable Resources → Limited resources that take a very long time to form.
  • Waste Management → Responsible handling and disposal of waste.
  • 3Rs → Reduce, Reuse and Recycle.
  • E-Waste → Discarded electrical and electronic equipment.
  • Energy Conservation → Avoiding unnecessary energy use and improving efficiency.
  • Green Economy → Economic activity that supports environmental sustainability.
  • Green Jobs → Jobs that contribute to environmental protection or resource efficiency.
