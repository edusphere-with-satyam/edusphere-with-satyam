7.1 Meaning and Components of Aggregate Demand

Definition 1 – Academic:
Aggregate Demand (AD) refers to the total monetary value of all final goods and services that all sectors of an economy are planning to buy at a given level of income during a period of one accounting year.
Definition 2 – Simple:
Aggregate Demand means the total amount that all sectors of an economy plan to spend on final goods and services during a given period.

Meaning of Aggregate Demand

In microeconomics, demand generally refers to the quantity of a particular commodity that an individual consumer is willing and able to buy at a given price. Macroeconomics, however, studies the economy as a whole. Therefore, Aggregate Demand represents total planned expenditure in the economy.

Key Characteristics of AD

  • Flow Concept: AD is measured over a particular period, generally an accounting year.
  • Planned Expenditure: AD represents desired or planned expenditure rather than actual expenditure.
  • Monetary Valuation: AD is expressed in monetary terms.
  • Economy-wide Concept: It considers spending by different sectors of the economy.

Components of Aggregate Demand

In a four-sector open economy:

AD = C + I + G + (X − M)
Component Meaning Examples
C Private Final Consumption Expenditure Food, clothing, medical care, rent
I Private Gross Investment Expenditure Machinery, buildings, inventories
G Government Final Consumption & Investment Expenditure Education, defence, roads, railways
X − M Net Exports Exports minus imports

1. Private Final Consumption Expenditure (C)

It includes expenditure incurred by households and private non-profit institutions serving households on final goods and services to satisfy immediate wants.

  • Food
  • Clothing
  • Medical care
  • Rent
  • Recreation

A major determinant of consumption is disposable income. Generally, when disposable income increases, consumption expenditure also increases.

2. Private Gross Investment Expenditure (I)

Investment refers to planned expenditure by private firms on acquiring new capital assets, creating capital stock and adding to inventories.

Fixed Investment

Expenditure on plant, equipment, machinery, buildings and similar productive assets.

Inventory Investment

The net change in the stock of raw materials, semi-finished goods and finished goods during a period.

3. Government Final Consumption & Investment Expenditure (G)

It represents government expenditure undertaken to satisfy collective needs of society.

  • Defence
  • Law and order
  • Public administration
  • Education
  • Healthcare
  • Roads and highways
  • Railways
  • Dams and public utilities

4. Net Exports (X − M)

Net exports represent the difference between exports and imports.

Net Exports = Exports − Imports

Exports (X): Spending by foreigners on domestically produced goods and services.

Imports (M): Domestic spending on foreign-produced goods and services.

Additional Concept Builder:
The four-sector formula is useful for understanding an open economy. However, the basic Keynesian income-determination model at this level often begins with a simple two-sector economy consisting of households and firms.

Two-Sector Economy

Households Firms

There is no government sector and no foreign trade in this simplified model. Therefore:

AD = C + I

Here, C represents household consumption expenditure and I represents private investment expenditure.

📚 Questions Related to Aggregate Demand

Q1. Define Aggregate Demand.

View Answer
Aggregate Demand is the total planned expenditure on final goods and services in an economy during a given period at a given level of income. In a two-sector economy, AD is represented as AD = C + I.

Q2. State the components of Aggregate Demand in a four-sector economy.

View Answer
The four components are:
1. Private Final Consumption Expenditure (C)
2. Private Gross Investment Expenditure (I)
3. Government Final Consumption and Investment Expenditure (G)
4. Net Exports (X − M)

Therefore: AD = C + I + G + (X − M)

Q3. Explain why Aggregate Demand is called planned expenditure.

View Answer
Aggregate Demand represents the expenditure that economic agents plan or intend to make on final goods and services. Therefore, it is an ex-ante or planned concept rather than a measure of actual expenditure.

7.2 Aggregate Supply

Definition 1 – Academic:
Aggregate Supply refers to the total monetary value of all final goods and services that producers are willing and planned to supply in an economy during a given accounting year.
Definition 2 – Simple:
Aggregate Supply means the total value of final goods and services supplied by producers in an economy during a given period.

Aggregate Supply and National Income

When goods and services are produced, income is generated for the factors of production in the form of wages, rent, interest and profit. That income is either consumed or saved.

AS = Y = C + S

Therefore:

  • AS = Aggregate Supply
  • Y = National Income
  • C = Consumption
  • S = Saving

The 45° Line / Line of Equality

The 45° line represents equality between Aggregate Supply and National Income/expenditure.

📈 45° Line: AS = Y
Exam Point:
Every point on the 45° line shows equality between Aggregate Supply and National Income. Thus, AS = Y at every point on this line.

📚 Questions Related to Aggregate Supply

Q1. What is Aggregate Supply?

View Answer
Aggregate Supply is the total monetary value of final goods and services that producers plan to supply during a given period. In the simple Keynesian framework, AS is equal to national income: AS = Y = C + S.

Q2. Why is AS represented by the 45° line?

View Answer
The 45° line represents equality between the horizontal-axis variable, national income, and the vertical-axis variable, aggregate supply or expenditure. Therefore, every point on this line represents AS = Y.

7.3 Consumption Function (Propensity to Consume)

Definition 1 – Academic:
The Consumption Function expresses the functional relationship between total household consumption expenditure and total national income.
Definition 2 – Simple:
The consumption function shows how consumption expenditure changes when national income changes.
C = f(Y)

Algebraic Form

C = C̄ + bY
Symbol Meaning
C̄ Autonomous Consumption
b Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC)
Y National Income

Keynesian Psychological Law of Consumption

  1. Income Drive: When aggregate income increases, aggregate consumption also increases.
  2. Less than Proportionate Increase: Consumption increases by less than the increase in income: ΔC < ΔY.
  3. Savings Creation: The part of additional income that is not consumed is saved: ΔY = ΔC + ΔS.
  4. Autonomous Consumption: Some minimum consumption exists even when current income is zero.

Autonomous Consumption

Autonomous consumption is consumption that does not depend on current income. In the consumption equation, it is represented by C̄.

Induced Consumption

Induced consumption changes with income. When income rises, induced consumption generally rises.

Break-Even Point

Definition:
The Break-Even Point is the level of income at which consumption expenditure is exactly equal to national income.
C = Y
S = 0

Using C = 40 + 0.8Y:

Income (Y) Consumption (C) Saving (S = Y − C) Economic State
0 40 −40 Dis-saving
100 120 −20 Dis-saving
200 200 0 Break-Even
300 280 20 Positive Saving
400 360 40 Positive Saving
Remember:
Before the break-even point, C > Y and saving is negative. After the break-even point, C < Y and saving is positive.
📈 Consumption Function & Break-Even Point

📚 Questions Related to Consumption Function

Q1. Explain the consumption function.

View Answer
The consumption function shows the functional relationship between consumption expenditure and national income. It is represented as C = f(Y). Its linear form is C = C̄ + bY, where C̄ is autonomous consumption and b is MPC.

Q2. What is the break-even point?

View Answer
The break-even point is the level of income where consumption is equal to income. At this point saving is zero.

C = Y and S = 0.

7.4 Types of Propensities to Consume

1. Average Propensity to Consume (APC)

Definition 1:
APC is the ratio of aggregate consumption expenditure to total national income.
Definition 2 – Simple:
APC tells us what proportion of total income is being consumed.
APC = C / Y

Important Properties of APC

  • APC can be greater than 1 when C > Y.
  • APC equals 1 at the break-even point.
  • APC is less than 1 when C < Y.
  • APC generally falls as income rises when autonomous consumption is positive.
  • APC cannot normally be zero when positive autonomous consumption exists.

2. Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC)

Definition 1:
MPC is the ratio of change in consumption expenditure to change in national income.
Definition 2 – Simple:
MPC tells us how much additional consumption occurs when income increases by one unit.
MPC = ΔC / ΔY

In the standard Keynesian framework:

0 ≤ MPC ≤ 1

APC vs MPC

Basis APC MPC
Meaning Proportion of total income consumed Proportion of additional income consumed
Formula C / Y ΔC / ΔY
Related to Total values Changes in values
Exam Point:
Poorer households generally tend to have a higher MPC because a larger share of additional income may be spent on basic necessities.

📚 Questions Related to APC & MPC

Q1. Distinguish between APC and MPC.

View Answer
APC is the ratio of total consumption to total income: APC = C/Y. MPC is the ratio of change in consumption to change in income: MPC = ΔC/ΔY. Thus, APC deals with total values whereas MPC deals with changes.

Q2. If income is ₹1,000 and consumption is ₹800, calculate APC.

View Answer
Given:
Y = ₹1,000
C = ₹800

APC = C/Y
= 800/1000
= 0.8

Therefore, APC = 0.8 or 80%.

7.5 Saving Function (Propensity to Save)

Definition 1 – Academic:
The saving function represents the functional relationship between aggregate saving and national income.
Definition 2 – Simple:
The saving function shows how saving changes when national income changes.
S = f(Y)
S = Y − C

Derivation of Saving Function

We know:

S = Y − C

and:

C = C̄ + bY

Therefore:

S = Y − (C̄ + bY)
S = −C̄ + (1 − b)Y

Here:

  • −C̄ represents dissaving at zero income.
  • (1 − b) represents MPS.

Dissaving

When consumption exceeds income, saving becomes negative. This negative saving is called dissaving.

C > Y → S < 0 → Dissaving

Positive Saving

C < Y → S > 0 → Positive Saving
📈 Saving Function

📚 Questions Related to Saving Function

Q1. Derive the saving function from the consumption function.

View Answer
Given:
C = C̄ + bY

We know:
S = Y − C

Therefore:
S = Y − (C̄ + bY)
S = −C̄ + (1 − b)Y

Hence, the saving function is: S = −C̄ + (1 − b)Y.

Q2. What is dissaving?

View Answer
Dissaving occurs when consumption expenditure exceeds income. Therefore saving becomes negative.

C > Y → S < 0

7.6 Types of Propensities to Save

1. Average Propensity to Save (APS)

Definition 1:
APS is the ratio of total saving to total national income.
Definition 2 – Simple:
APS tells us what proportion of total income is being saved.
APS = S / Y
  • APS can be negative during dissaving.
  • APS is zero at the break-even point.
  • APS rises as income rises under the standard consumption function.

2. Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS)

Definition 1:
MPS is the ratio of change in saving to change in national income.
Definition 2 – Simple:
MPS tells us how much additional saving occurs when income increases by one unit.
MPS = ΔS / ΔY
MPC + MPS = 1

Fundamental Mathematical Relationships

Relationship Formula
Average Propensities APC + APS = 1
Marginal Propensities MPC + MPS = 1
Break-Even Condition:
At break-even:

C = Y
S = 0
APC = 1
APS = 0

📚 Questions Related to APS & MPS

Q1. If MPC is 0.75, find MPS.

View Answer
We know:
MPC + MPS = 1

Therefore:
MPS = 1 − MPC
= 1 − 0.75
= 0.25

Q2. State the relationship between APC and APS.

View Answer
Since income is either consumed or saved:
Y = C + S

Dividing by Y:
C/Y + S/Y = 1

Therefore: APC + APS = 1.

7.7 Investment Function

Definition 1 – Academic:
Investment refers to expenditure on the creation or acquisition of capital assets and addition to inventories.
Definition 2 – Simple:
Investment means spending on productive assets such as machinery, buildings, equipment and inventories.

Investment Expenditure

Investment expenditure includes spending by firms on productive capital and additions to inventories.

Autonomous Investment

Autonomous investment is investment that is independent of the current level of income.

Income changes → Autonomous Investment remains constant

In a diagram, autonomous investment is represented by a horizontal straight line.

Induced Investment

Induced investment depends on factors such as income, demand and expected profitability. It generally increases when firms expect higher demand and profits.

Income/Demand ↑ → Induced Investment ↑
Basis Autonomous Investment Induced Investment
Dependence on income Independent Dependent
Graph Horizontal Generally upward sloping
Main idea Independent of current income Changes with income/demand and expectations

📚 Questions Related to Investment

Q1. Distinguish between autonomous and induced investment.

View Answer
Autonomous investment is independent of the current level of income, whereas induced investment changes with income, demand and expected profitability.

Q2. What is investment in macroeconomics?

View Answer
Investment refers to expenditure on capital assets such as machinery, buildings and equipment and on changes in inventories. It contributes to the capital stock and productive capacity of the economy.

7.8 Determinants of Investment

Definition 1:
The determinants of investment are the economic factors that influence the decision of firms to undertake investment expenditure.
Definition 2 – Simple:
These are the factors that influence whether firms decide to invest more or less.

Major Determinants

1. Expected Rate of Return

Firms are more likely to invest when they expect a higher return from the investment.

2. Rate of Interest

The rate of interest is an important cost of borrowed funds. Generally:

Rate of Interest ↑ → Investment ↓
Rate of Interest ↓ → Investment ↑

3. Expected Demand

If firms expect demand for their products to increase, they may expand their production capacity and investment.

4. Level of Income

Higher income can increase demand for goods and services, encouraging firms to expand production and investment.

5. Business Expectations

Optimistic expectations about future sales and profits encourage investment, while uncertainty may discourage it.

6. Availability of Credit

Easy availability of credit can make it easier for firms to finance investment projects.

7. Government Policy

Tax policies, infrastructure, regulatory conditions and other government measures can affect investment decisions.

8. Technology

Technological developments may create new opportunities for investment and increase the productivity of capital.

9. Cost of Capital Goods

Higher prices of machinery and capital goods may make investment projects more expensive, while lower costs may encourage investment.

📚 Questions Related to Determinants of Investment

Q1. Explain any three determinants of investment.

View Answer
Three important determinants are:

1. Rate of Interest: A lower interest rate generally reduces the cost of borrowing and may encourage investment.

2. Expected Rate of Return: Higher expected profitability makes investment more attractive.

3. Expected Demand: If firms expect demand to rise, they may increase investment to expand productive capacity.

7.9 Ex-Ante and Ex-Post Saving and Investment

Ex-Ante: Refers to planned, intended or desired values before the economic activity takes place.
Simple Meaning: Ex-ante means planned.
Ex-Post: Refers to actual or realised values after the economic activity has taken place.
Simple Meaning: Ex-post means actual.
Basis Ex-Ante Ex-Post
Meaning Planned or desired Actual or realised
Nature Forward-looking Backward-looking
Shows Intentions/plans Actual accounting results
Saving & Investment May not be equal Are equal in accounting identity
Very Important:
Ex-ante saving and ex-ante investment need not be equal because they are planned independently. Ex-post saving and ex-post investment are equal as an accounting identity.

📚 Questions Related to Ex-Ante & Ex-Post

Q1. Distinguish between ex-ante and ex-post values.

View Answer
Ex-ante values are planned or intended values for a period, whereas ex-post values are actual or realised values recorded after the period. Thus, ex-ante is forward-looking while ex-post is backward-looking.

Q2. Can ex-ante saving and investment be unequal?

View Answer
Yes. Ex-ante saving and ex-ante investment may differ because saving and investment plans are made independently by different economic units. However, ex-post saving and ex-post investment are equal as an accounting identity.

7.10 Full Employment and Involuntary Unemployment

Full Employment

Definition 1 – Academic:
Full employment refers to a situation in which all those who are willing and able to work at the prevailing wage rate can obtain employment, apart from normal forms of unemployment.
Definition 2 – Simple:
Full employment does not mean that every person has a job. It means that people who are willing and able to work can generally obtain employment.
Remember:
Full Employment ≠ Zero Unemployment.

Some normal or voluntary unemployment may still exist.

Involuntary Unemployment

Definition 1:
Involuntary unemployment exists when people are willing and able to work at the prevailing wage rate but cannot obtain employment.
Definition 2 – Simple:
A person wants to work and is ready to work at the prevailing wage, but no employment is available.

Full Employment vs Involuntary Unemployment

Full Employment Involuntary Unemployment
Willing workers can obtain employment. Willing workers cannot obtain employment.
Involuntary unemployment is absent. Involuntary unemployment exists.
Labour resources are utilised relatively fully. Some willing labour remains unemployed.
Additional Concept Builder:
In Keynesian analysis, an economy may settle at an equilibrium level of output below the full-employment level. Such a situation can involve involuntary unemployment.

📚 Questions Related to Full Employment

Q1. Does full employment mean zero unemployment?

View Answer
No. Full employment does not mean zero unemployment. Some normal, frictional or voluntary unemployment may exist. Full employment means that people who are willing and able to work at the prevailing wage can obtain employment.

Q2. What is involuntary unemployment?

View Answer
Involuntary unemployment is a situation in which people are willing and able to work at the prevailing wage rate but cannot find employment.

7.11 Solved Practicals

Practical 1 — Consumption and Saving Functions

Question:
Autonomous Consumption (C̄) = ₹100 crore
MPC = 0.75
Investment (I) = ₹200 crore

Find:
1. Consumption and Saving functions
2. Break-even level of income
3. Aggregate Demand at Y = ₹1,500 crore
View Complete Solution
Step 1: Consumption Function Formula:
C = C̄ + bY

C = 100 + 0.75Y

Therefore: C = 100 + 0.75Y
Step 2: Saving Function Formula:
S = −C̄ + (1 − b)Y

S = −100 + (1 − 0.75)Y
S = −100 + 0.25Y

Therefore: S = −100 + 0.25Y
Step 3: Break-Even Income At break-even:
C = Y

Y = 100 + 0.75Y
Y − 0.75Y = 100
0.25Y = 100
Y = ₹400 crore
Step 4: Aggregate Demand at Y = ₹1,500 crore C = 100 + 0.75(1500)
C = 100 + 1125
C = ₹1,225 crore

AD = C + I
AD = 1225 + 200
AD = ₹1,425 crore

Final Answers:
Consumption Function = C = 100 + 0.75Y
Saving Function = S = −100 + 0.25Y
Break-Even Income = ₹400 crore
AD at Y = ₹1,500 crore = ₹1,425 crore

Saving: S = Y − C

For Y = 100:
S = 100 − 120 = −20

For Y = 200:
S = 200 − 190 = 10

At Y = 100:
APC = 120/100 = 1.20

At Y = 200:
APC = 190/200 = 0.95

At Y = 300:
APC = 260/300 ≈ 0.867

From Y = 0 to Y = 100:
MPC = (120 − 50)/(100 − 0)
= 70/100
= 0.70 Similarly, the MPC between successive income levels remains: 0.70.

📊 Diagrammatic Derivation of Saving Curve from Consumption Curve

The saving curve can be derived from the consumption curve using the 45° line and the break-even point.

Step 1 — Draw the 45° Line

Draw the AS = Y line originating from the origin at an angle of 45°.

Step 2 — Draw the Consumption Function

Draw the consumption function:

C = C̄ + bY

It begins from the autonomous consumption level C̄ on the vertical axis.

Step 3 — Identify Break-Even Point

The consumption curve intersects the 45° line at point E. At this point:

C = Y
S = 0

Step 4 — Project the Break-Even Point

Project point E downward to the income axis of the saving graph. This gives point E′ where saving is zero.

Step 5 — Mark −C̄

On the saving graph, mark −C̄ on the negative vertical axis.

Step 6 — Draw the Saving Curve

Join −C̄ through E′ and extend the line upward. The resulting curve is the saving function:

S = −C̄ + (1 − b)Y
Board Exam Sequence:
45° line → Consumption curve → Break-even point E → Projection to E′ → −C̄ → Saving curve.
