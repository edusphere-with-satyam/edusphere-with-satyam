📘 Classification of DPSPs

The Directive Principles of State Policy are contained in Part IV of the Constitution, Articles 36 to 51. They provide directions to the State for establishing social, economic and political conditions necessary for a welfare-oriented society.

The Constitution itself does not formally divide DPSPs into three categories. However, constitutional scholars and textbooks traditionally classify them according to their objectives and underlying ideas.

📌 Three Traditional Categories

1. Socialist Principles
2. Gandhian Principles
3. Liberal-Intellectual Principles

🔎 At a Glance

Classification Main Focus Core Idea
🟩 Gandhian Village, rural development and weaker sections Gram Swaraj & social upliftment
🟦 Socialist Equality, economic justice and workers' welfare Welfare State
🟨 Liberal-Intellectual Governance, law, environment and international relations Modern constitutional State

🟩 1. Gandhian Principles

The Gandhian Principles reflect ideas associated with Mahatma Gandhi, especially village self-government, rural development, cottage industries, protection of weaker sections, public health and animal welfare.

Article 40

🏘️ Organisation of Village Panchayats

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall take steps to organise village panchayats and endow them with such powers and authority as may be necessary to enable them to function as units of self-government.”

Explanation

The State should establish and strengthen village panchayats and give them sufficient powers and authority to function as genuine units of local self-government.

This reflects the Gandhian idea of Gram Swaraj, where villages play an important role in governing their local affairs.

💡 Example

Giving Gram Panchayats responsibilities for local development, sanitation, roads and community facilities reflects the objective of Article 40.

Article 43

🏭 Cottage Industries

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall endeavour to promote cottage industries on an individual or co-operative basis in rural areas.”

Explanation

The State should encourage small-scale and cottage industries, particularly in rural areas.

The objective is to create rural employment and strengthen local economic activity.

💡 Example

Supporting village-based handicrafts, handloom work and other traditional cottage industries can help create employment in rural areas.

Article 43B

🤝 Cooperative Societies

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall endeavour to promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management of co-operative societies.”

Explanation

The State should encourage cooperative societies while respecting their voluntary nature, democratic functioning and autonomy.

Article 46

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Interests of Weaker Sections

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall promote with special care the educational and economic interests of the weaker sections of the people, and, in particular, of the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes, and shall protect them from social injustice and all forms of exploitation.”

Explanation

The State should give special attention to the educational and economic advancement of weaker sections, particularly SCs and STs.

It should also protect them from social injustice and exploitation.

Article 47

🥗 Nutrition, Public Health & Prohibition

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall regard the raising of the level of nutrition and the standard of living of its people and the improvement of public health as among its primary duties...”

The Article also directs the State to endeavour to bring about prohibition of intoxicating drinks and drugs injurious to health, except for medicinal purposes.

Explanation

The State should work to improve nutrition, living standards and public health.

It also contains a constitutional direction concerning prohibition of harmful intoxicating drinks and drugs, subject to the medicinal exception mentioned in the Article.

Article 48

🐄 Agriculture & Animal Husbandry

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall endeavour to organise agriculture and animal husbandry on modern and scientific lines...”

It also directs the State to take steps for preserving and improving breeds and prohibiting the slaughter of cows and calves and other milch and draught cattle.

Explanation

The Article combines modern scientific agriculture with preservation and improvement of animal breeds and protection of specified cattle.

🧠 Remember Gandhian Principles:

Village → Cottage → Cooperative → Weaker Sections → Health → Cattle

Articles: 40, 43, 43B, 46, 47, 48

🟦 2. Socialist Principles

The Socialist Principles focus mainly on social and economic justice, reduction of inequality, adequate livelihood and welfare of workers.

Article 38

⚖️ Social Order Based on Justice

Constitutional Provision

The State shall strive to promote the welfare of the people by securing and protecting a social order in which justice—social, economic and political—shall inform all the institutions of the national life.

It also directs the State to minimise inequalities in income and endeavour to eliminate inequalities in status, facilities and opportunities.

Explanation

The State should create conditions where social, economic and political justice influence public life and where excessive inequalities are reduced.

Article 39

💰 Principles of Economic Justice

Article 39(a)

Citizens, men and women equally, have the right to an adequate means of livelihood.

Article 39(b)

The ownership and control of the material resources of the community are to be distributed so as best to subserve the common good.

Article 39(c)

The operation of the economic system should not result in the concentration of wealth and means of production to the common detriment.

Article 39(d)

There should be equal pay for equal work for both men and women.

Article 39(e)

The health and strength of workers, men and women, and the tender age of children should not be abused and citizens should not be forced by economic necessity to enter unsuitable employment.

Article 39(f)

Children should be given opportunities and facilities to develop in a healthy manner and in conditions of freedom and dignity, and childhood and youth should be protected against exploitation and against moral and material abandonment.

Explanation

Article 39 seeks to create a fairer economic and social order. It addresses livelihood, distribution of resources, concentration of wealth, equal pay, worker protection and child development.

Article 39A

⚖️ Equal Justice & Free Legal Aid

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall secure that the operation of the legal system promotes justice, on a basis of equal opportunity, and shall, in particular, provide free legal aid...”

Explanation

Economic or other disadvantages should not prevent a person from accessing justice.

The State should therefore provide legal aid to ensure equal opportunity in the justice system.

Article 41

👷 Work, Education & Public Assistance

Constitutional Provision

The State shall, within the limits of its economic capacity and development, make effective provision for securing the right to work, to education and to public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness and disablement, and in other cases of undeserved want.

Explanation

The Article directs the State to provide support in specified situations, but expressly links this obligation with the State's economic capacity and development.

Article 42

👩‍💼 Humane Working Conditions & Maternity Relief

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall make provision for securing just and humane conditions of work and for maternity relief.”

Explanation

Workers should have fair and humane working conditions, and women should receive appropriate maternity protection.

Article 43

💼 Living Wage & Decent Standard of Life

Constitutional Provision

The State shall endeavour to secure, by suitable legislation or economic organisation or in any other way, to all workers, agricultural, industrial or otherwise, work, a living wage, conditions of work ensuring a decent standard of life and full enjoyment of leisure and social and cultural opportunities.

Explanation

The objective is not merely employment but a living wage and decent standard of life for workers.

Article 43A

🏢 Workers' Participation in Management

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall take steps, by suitable legislation or in any other way, to secure the participation of workers in the management of undertakings, establishments or other organisations engaged in any industry.”

Explanation

Workers should have an opportunity to participate in the management of industrial organisations.

🧠 Remember Socialist Principles:

Equality → Livelihood → Resources → Legal Aid → Work → Workers' Welfare

Articles: 38, 39, 39A, 41, 42, 43, 43A

🟨 3. Liberal-Intellectual Principles

These principles are mainly concerned with modern governance, uniformity in civil matters, education, environmental protection, preservation of heritage, judicial independence and international peace.

Article 44

⚖️ Uniform Civil Code

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a uniform civil code throughout the territory of India.”

Explanation

The Article directs the State to endeavour to establish a common set of civil laws for citizens across India.

Article 45

👶 Early Childhood Care & Education

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall endeavour to provide early childhood care and education for all children until they complete the age of six years.”

Explanation

The State should provide care and educational support during the early childhood period up to six years of age.

Article 48A

🌳 Environment, Forests & Wildlife

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wild life of the country.”

Explanation

The State should protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife.

Article 49

🏛️ Protection of National Monuments

Constitutional Provision

It shall be the obligation of the State to protect every monument or place or object of artistic or historic interest, declared by or under law made by Parliament to be of national importance, from spoliation, disfigurement, destruction, removal, disposal or export, as the case may be.

Explanation

The State must protect historically or artistically important monuments and objects that have been declared to be of national importance.

Article 50

⚖️ Separation of Judiciary from Executive

Constitutional Provision

“The State shall take steps to separate the judiciary from the executive in the public services of the State.”

Explanation

The Article seeks to keep judicial functions separate from executive functions in State public services, supporting an independent and impartial justice system.

Article 51

🌍 International Peace & Security

Article 51(a)

Promote international peace and security.

Article 51(b)

Maintain just and honourable relations between nations.

Article 51(c)

Foster respect for international law and treaty obligations.

Article 51(d)

Encourage settlement of international disputes by arbitration.

Explanation

Article 51 directs the State to pursue peaceful international relations, respect international law and encourage peaceful settlement of international disputes.

🧠 Remember Liberal-Intellectual Principles:

UCC → Children → Environment → Heritage → Judiciary → International Peace

Articles: 44, 45, 48A, 49, 50, 51

📌 Important Note About Classification

The terms Socialist, Gandhian and Liberal-Intellectual are not categories expressly mentioned in the Constitution.

They are a scholarly classification used to understand the objectives behind different Directive Principles. Therefore, different books may classify a few Articles differently.

For example, Article 43 is commonly associated with Gandhian principles because of its emphasis on cottage industries, but it also contains an important worker-welfare objective through the living-wage provision.

🎯 Understanding the Three Categories

Category What it mainly tries to achieve
🟩 Gandhian Strong villages, rural economy, weaker sections, public health and animal welfare
🟦 Socialist Social and economic justice, equality and workers' welfare
🟨 Liberal-Intellectual Modern governance, education, environment, judicial independence and international peace

Gandhian → Village & Rural Society
