30 Important MCQs | Utility • Consumer Equilibrium • Demand • Elasticity
Utility

Q1. What does utility mean in economics?

A. Price of a commodity
B. Want-satisfying capacity of a commodity
C. Cost of production
D. Income of a consumer
✅ Answer: B. Want-satisfying capacity of a commodity
Explanation: Utility refers to the capacity of a commodity or service to satisfy human wants.
Utility

Q2. Utility is generally considered to be:

A. Objective
B. Subjective
C. Constant
D. Equal for everyone
✅ Answer: B. Subjective
Explanation: Utility differs from person to person because wants and preferences differ.
Total Utility

Q3. Total Utility means:

A. Utility from the last unit only
B. Price of all units
C. Total satisfaction from all units consumed
D. Income of the consumer
✅ Answer: C. Total satisfaction from all units consumed
Explanation: Total Utility is the sum of utility obtained from all units consumed.
Marginal Utility

Q4. Marginal Utility refers to:

A. Total satisfaction
B. Additional satisfaction from one more unit
C. Price of a commodity
D. Total income
✅ Answer: B. Additional satisfaction from one more unit
Explanation: Marginal Utility is the additional utility obtained from consuming one additional unit of a commodity.
Formula

Q5. Which formula represents Marginal Utility?

A. MU = TU × Q
B. MU = ΔTU ÷ ΔQ
C. MU = Q ÷ TU
D. MU = TU + Q
✅ Answer: B. MU = ΔTU ÷ ΔQ
Explanation: Marginal Utility is the change in Total Utility divided by the change in quantity consumed.
TU and MU

Q6. When Marginal Utility is zero, Total Utility is:

A. Minimum
B. Negative
C. Maximum
D. Constantly decreasing
✅ Answer: C. Maximum
Explanation: Total Utility reaches its maximum level when Marginal Utility becomes zero.
Law of DMU

Q7. The Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility states that Marginal Utility:

A. Always increases
B. Remains constant
C. Tends to decrease with successive consumption
D. Becomes infinite
✅ Answer: C. Tends to decrease with successive consumption
Explanation: As successive units of a commodity are consumed continuously, the additional satisfaction generally decreases.
Law of DMU

Q8. Which of the following is an assumption of the Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility?

A. Units should be identical
B. Price must continuously increase
C. Income must continuously decrease
D. Consumer must be irrational
✅ Answer: A. Units should be identical
Explanation: For the law to operate properly, successive units should be reasonably identical and consumption should be continuous.
Consumer Equilibrium

Q9. Consumer equilibrium refers to a situation where the consumer:

A. Minimises satisfaction
B. Maximises satisfaction subject to constraints
C. Has unlimited income
D. Buys nothing
✅ Answer: B. Maximises satisfaction subject to constraints
Explanation: A rational consumer tries to obtain maximum satisfaction from given income and prices.
Consumer Equilibrium

Q10. In the single commodity approach, consumer equilibrium is achieved when:

A. MU > Price
B. MU < Price
C. MU = Price
D. MU = 0 always
✅ Answer: C. MU = Price
Explanation: When marginal utility measured in monetary terms equals the price, the consumer reaches equilibrium for a single commodity.
Demand

Q11. Which of the following is necessary for demand?

A. Desire only
B. Willingness only
C. Ability to pay only
D. Desire, willingness and ability to pay
✅ Answer: D. Desire, willingness and ability to pay
Explanation: A mere desire is not demand. It must be supported by willingness and ability to pay.
Demand

Q12. Demand is related to:

A. A particular price and time period
B. Any price without time
C. Income only
D. Production only
✅ Answer: A. A particular price and time period
Explanation: Demand refers to the quantity a consumer is willing and able to buy at a particular price during a particular period.
Demand Schedule

Q13. A demand schedule is a:

A. Graphical presentation
B. Tabular presentation
C. Mathematical equation only
D. Written story
✅ Answer: B. Tabular presentation
Explanation: A demand schedule shows different quantities demanded at different prices in a table.
Demand Curve

Q14. A normal demand curve generally slopes:

A. Upward from left to right
B. Downward from left to right
C. Vertically upward only
D. Horizontally only
✅ Answer: B. Downward from left to right
Explanation: The demand curve generally slopes downward because price and quantity demanded have an inverse relationship, other things remaining constant.
Law of Demand

Q15. According to the Law of Demand, when price rises, quantity demanded generally:

A. Rises
B. Falls
C. Remains unchanged
D. Becomes zero always
✅ Answer: B. Falls
Explanation: Other things remaining constant, quantity demanded generally decreases when price increases.
Law of Demand

Q16. The Law of Demand assumes:

A. Other things remain constant
B. Income changes continuously
C. Tastes change continuously
D. Population changes every day
✅ Answer: A. Other things remain constant
Explanation: The phrase ceteris paribus means other relevant factors are held constant.
Exceptions

Q17. Which of the following is commonly discussed as an exception to the Law of Demand?

A. Giffen goods
B. Normal goods
C. Necessities only
D. All goods
✅ Answer: A. Giffen goods
Explanation: Giffen goods are traditionally discussed as a special case where the usual inverse price-demand relationship may not hold.
Determinants

Q18. Which of the following can affect demand?

A. Consumer income
B. Tastes and preferences
C. Prices of related goods
D. All of the above
✅ Answer: D. All of the above
Explanation: Income, tastes, preferences and prices of related goods are important determinants of demand.
Movement

Q19. A change in quantity demanded due to a change in the commodity's own price is called:

A. Shift in demand
B. Movement along the demand curve
C. Change in supply
D. Change in income
✅ Answer: B. Movement along the demand curve
Explanation: A change in the commodity's own price causes movement along the same demand curve.
Movement

Q20. Expansion of demand occurs when:

A. Price rises
B. Price falls
C. Income falls only
D. Tastes change
✅ Answer: B. Price falls
Explanation: Expansion of demand means an increase in quantity demanded caused by a fall in the commodity's own price.
Shift

Q21. A shift in demand curve is caused by:

A. Change in own price only
B. Change in factors other than own price
C. Change in quantity supplied
D. No change at all
✅ Answer: B. Change in factors other than own price
Explanation: Changes in income, tastes, prices of related goods and other non-price factors can shift the demand curve.
Shift

Q22. An increase in demand generally shifts the demand curve:

A. Leftward
B. Rightward
C. Vertically downward
D. Nowhere
✅ Answer: B. Rightward
Explanation: An increase in demand at every given price is represented by a rightward shift of the demand curve.
Elasticity

Q23. Elasticity of demand measures:

A. Total production
B. Responsiveness of demand to a change in its determinants
C. Total income
D. Total cost
✅ Answer: B. Responsiveness of demand to a change in its determinants
Explanation: Elasticity measures how responsive quantity demanded is to changes in price, income or prices of related goods.
Price Elasticity

Q24. Price elasticity of demand measures responsiveness of quantity demanded to:

A. Change in income
B. Change in price
C. Change in population
D. Change in technology
✅ Answer: B. Change in price
Explanation: Price elasticity measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in the price of the commodity.
Formula

Q25. Which is the formula for price elasticity of demand?

A. Ed = % change in price ÷ % change in quantity demanded
B. Ed = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price
C. Ed = Price × Quantity
D. Ed = Income ÷ Price
✅ Answer: B. % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price
Explanation: Price elasticity is calculated as percentage change in quantity demanded divided by percentage change in price.
Elasticity

Q26. If Ed = 0, demand is:

A. Perfectly elastic
B. Unitary elastic
C. Perfectly inelastic
D. Relatively elastic
✅ Answer: C. Perfectly inelastic
Explanation: When elasticity is zero, quantity demanded does not respond to a change in price.
Elasticity

Q27. When Ed = 1, demand is:

A. Perfectly inelastic
B. Unitary elastic
C. Perfectly elastic
D. Inelastic only
✅ Answer: B. Unitary elastic
Explanation: When percentage change in quantity demanded equals percentage change in price, elasticity is equal to one.
Elasticity

Q28. If Ed > 1, demand is:

A. Relatively elastic
B. Relatively inelastic
C. Perfectly inelastic
D. Unitary elastic
✅ Answer: A. Relatively elastic
Explanation: When percentage change in quantity demanded is greater than percentage change in price, demand is relatively elastic.
Factors

Q29. Demand is generally more elastic when:

A. There are many close substitutes
B. There are no substitutes
C. The good is extremely necessary
D. The consumer has no choice
✅ Answer: A. There are many close substitutes
Explanation: The availability of close substitutes makes it easier for consumers to switch when price changes, making demand more responsive.
Factors

Q30. Which factor generally makes demand more elastic?

A. Longer time period
B. No substitutes
C. Strong habit
D. Essential nature of the good
✅ Answer: A. Longer time period
Explanation: Consumers generally get more time to adjust their consumption and find alternatives over a longer period.

🎯 Quick Revision Before Exam

  • Utility → Want-satisfying capacity
  • TU → Total satisfaction
  • MU → Additional satisfaction
  • MU = ΔTU ÷ ΔQ
  • TU is maximum when MU = 0
  • Demand requires desire + willingness + ability to pay
  • Price ↑ → Quantity demanded generally ↓
  • Movement → Change in own price
  • Shift → Change in non-price factors
  • Ed = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price
  • Ed = 0 → Perfectly inelastic
  • Ed = 1 → Unitary elastic
  • Ed > 1 → Relatively elastic
