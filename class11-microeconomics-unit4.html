📘 Unit 4 — The Theory of Firm Under Perfect Competition

What you will learn:
Market, perfect competition, features of perfect competition, firm and industry, price determination, price-taking behaviour, revenue concepts, Total Revenue, Average Revenue, Marginal Revenue, producer's equilibrium, profit maximisation, break-even point, shutdown point and short-run supply.

A firm is an economic unit that organises factors of production to produce goods and services. In this unit, we study how a firm behaves when it operates under perfect competition.

1. Market

🏪 Meaning of Market

Definition 1:
A market is a system or arrangement through which buyers and sellers interact for the exchange of goods and services.
Definition 2:
A market refers to the mechanism through which demand and supply interact to determine the price and quantity of a commodity.
Important: A market does not necessarily mean a physical place. Buyers and sellers can interact through online platforms or other communication systems.
2. Perfect Competition

⚖️ Meaning of Perfect Competition

Definition 1:
Perfect competition is a market structure in which there are a large number of buyers and sellers selling a homogeneous product.
Definition 2:
Perfect competition is a market situation where individual firms have no control over the market price and accept the price determined by market forces.
Simple meaning:
There are many sellers, the product is identical, and an individual firm cannot decide the market price.
3. Features

⭐ Features of Perfect Competition

1. Large Number of Buyers and Sellers

There are many buyers and sellers, and each individual participant is too small to influence the market price.

2. Homogeneous Product

All firms sell identical or homogeneous products. Buyers consider the products of different firms to be perfect substitutes.

3. Free Entry and Exit

Firms are generally free to enter the industry when profits are available and leave when losses persist.

4. Perfect Knowledge

Buyers and sellers are assumed to have complete knowledge about market conditions, including prices.

5. Perfect Mobility of Factors

Factors of production can move freely from one use or place to another.

6. No Individual Price Control

An individual firm cannot influence the market price because its share of total market supply is very small.

Key idea:
A perfectly competitive firm is a price taker, not a price maker.
4. Firm and Industry

🏭 Firm and Industry

Firm:
A firm is an individual production unit that produces goods or services.
Industry:
An industry consists of all firms producing the same or closely related goods and services.
Basis Firm Industry
Meaning Individual production unit Collection of firms
Supply Supply of one firm Supply of all firms
Price influence Cannot determine market price Market forces determine price
5. Price Determination

📊 Price Determination Under Perfect Competition

Under perfect competition, the market price is determined by the interaction of market demand and market supply.

Equilibrium Price:
The price at which quantity demanded is equal to quantity supplied is called the equilibrium price.
Equilibrium Condition:

Market Demand = Market Supply
Example:
Suppose buyers want to purchase 1,000 units at ₹50 and sellers are also willing to supply 1,000 units at ₹50. Then ₹50 represents the equilibrium price under the given conditions.
6. Price-Taking Firm

🎯 Price-Taking Behaviour

Definition:
A price-taking firm is a firm that accepts the market price as given because it cannot influence the price individually.
The market determines the price → Individual firm accepts that price.

Because the product is homogeneous and many firms operate in the market, charging a higher price would cause buyers to purchase from other firms.

7. Revenue

💰 Revenue Concepts

Revenue is the money received by a firm from the sale of its output. The three important revenue concepts are:

  • Total Revenue (TR)
  • Average Revenue (AR)
  • Marginal Revenue (MR)
8. Total Revenue

💵 Total Revenue (TR)

Definition 1:
Total Revenue is the total amount of money received by a firm from selling its output.
Definition 2:
TR is the total sales receipts obtained from the sale of a given quantity of output.
TR = Price × Quantity Sold

TR = P × Q
If price = ₹20 and quantity sold = 100 units:

TR = ₹20 × 100
TR = ₹2,000
9. Average Revenue

📊 Average Revenue (AR)

Definition 1:
Average Revenue is the revenue earned per unit of output sold.
Definition 2:
AR is obtained by dividing Total Revenue by the quantity sold.
AR = TR ÷ Q
Since:

TR = P × Q

Therefore:
AR = P
10. Marginal Revenue

➕ Marginal Revenue (MR)

Definition 1:
Marginal Revenue is the change in Total Revenue resulting from the sale of one additional unit of output.
Definition 2:
MR is the additional revenue obtained by selling an additional unit of the commodity.
MR = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ
11. AR and MR

🔗 Relationship Between AR and MR

Under perfect competition, the market price remains constant for an individual firm.
AR = MR = Price
Quantity Price TR AR MR
1 ₹10 ₹10 ₹10 ₹10
2 ₹10 ₹20 ₹10 ₹10
3 ₹10 ₹30 ₹10 ₹10
4 ₹10 ₹40 ₹10 ₹10
Exam Point:
For a perfectly competitive firm, the AR and MR curves are horizontal and coincide with the price line.
12. Producer's Equilibrium

🎯 Producer's Equilibrium

Definition 1:
Producer's equilibrium is the level of output at which a producer earns maximum possible profit.
Definition 2:
A producer is in equilibrium when there is no incentive for the firm to change its output level because profit is maximised.
The producer compares Marginal Revenue (MR) with Marginal Cost (MC) to determine equilibrium output.
13. MR and MC Approach

⚖️ Producer Equilibrium Using MR-MC

Equilibrium Condition:

MR = MC

The condition alone is not sufficient. The MC curve must be rising at the equilibrium output.

First condition: MR = MC

Second condition: MC must be rising at the point of equilibrium.
Situation Result
MR > MC Firm should increase output
MR < MC Firm should reduce output
MR = MC Equilibrium output
14. Profit

💰 Profit Maximisation

Profit:
Profit is the excess of total revenue over total cost.
Profit = TR − TC
A firm aims to choose that level of output where the difference between Total Revenue and Total Cost is maximum.
15. Profit, Loss and Normal Profit

📈 Profit, Normal Profit and Loss

Situation Relationship Result
Supernormal Profit TR > TC Profit
Normal Profit TR = TC Zero economic profit
Loss TR < TC Firm incurs loss
Normal Profit:
Normal profit is considered a part of the minimum return required to keep the entrepreneur in the existing business.
16. Break-Even Point

🟢 Break-Even Point

Definition 1:
The break-even point is the level of output at which Total Revenue is equal to Total Cost.
Definition 2:
At the break-even point, the firm earns normal profit and has neither supernormal profit nor economic loss.
TR = TC
At break-even:

Profit = 0 in the economic sense.
The firm covers its total economic cost.
17. Shutdown Point

🔴 Shutdown Point

Definition:
The shutdown point is the situation in which the firm's revenue is just sufficient to cover its variable cost.
TR = TVC
In the standard short-run analysis, if price falls below the minimum AVC, the firm may find it better to temporarily shut down and produce zero output rather than continue production.
Important distinction:
Break-even concerns covering total cost, while shutdown analysis in the short run concerns whether variable cost can be covered.
18. Short-Run Supply

📈 Short-Run Supply Curve of a Firm

Definition:
The short-run supply curve of a perfectly competitive firm is the portion of its MC curve that lies above the minimum point of AVC.
Why?
Below the minimum AVC, the firm is better off shutting down in the standard short-run model. Above the minimum AVC, the firm supplies the output where P = MC, subject to the equilibrium conditions.
19. Market Supply

🏭 Market Supply Curve

Definition:
Market supply is the total quantity of a commodity supplied by all firms in the market at different prices.
In a perfectly competitive market, the market supply is obtained by adding the quantities supplied by all individual firms at each price.
20. Market Price and Firm

🔄 Market Price vs Firm Price

Market Individual Firm
Price determined by demand and supply Firm accepts the market price
Market demand and supply interact Firm chooses quantity
Price is given to the firm Firm is a price taker
Remember:
The market determines the price; the individual firm determines its profit-maximising quantity at that price.
21. Revenue Formula Sheet

🧮 Important Revenue Formulas

Concept Formula

Average Revenue AR = TR ÷ Q
Marginal Revenue MR = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ
Profit Profit = TR − TC
Perfect Competition AR = MR = P
Producer Equilibrium MR = MC
22. Numerical Example

🧮 Revenue Calculation

Suppose a firm sells 50 units at a price of ₹20 per unit.

TR = P × Q
= ₹20 × 50
TR = ₹1,000

AR = TR ÷ Q
= ₹1,000 ÷ 50
AR = ₹20

Since the firm is operating under perfect competition:
AR = MR = Price = ₹20

⭐ Important Exam Points

1. Perfect competition has a large number of buyers and sellers.
2. The product is homogeneous.
3. Firms are price takers.
4. Market price is determined by demand and supply.
5. For a competitive firm: AR = MR = Price.
6. TR = P × Q.
7. AR = TR ÷ Q.
8. MR = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ.
9. Profit = TR − TC.
10. Producer equilibrium occurs where MR = MC and MC is rising.
11. Break-even point occurs when TR = TC.
12. In the short run, shutdown is associated with the inability to cover variable cost; the shutdown point corresponds to minimum AVC in the standard model.

  • There is free entry and exit in the standard model.

  • Firm decides its profit-maximising quantity.
  • TR = P × Q.
  • AR = TR ÷ Q.

  • Under perfect competition, AR = MR = Price.
  • Profit = TR − TC.
  • Producer equilibrium: MR = MC.
