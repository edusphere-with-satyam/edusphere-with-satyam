📘 Unit 2 — Theory of Consumer Behaviour

This unit explains how consumers make choices to obtain maximum satisfaction from their limited income. It covers utility, consumer equilibrium, demand, law of demand and elasticity of demand.

What you will learn:

Utility Total Utility Marginal Utility Consumer Equilibrium Demand Law of Demand Demand Curve Elasticity

1. Utility

Meaning of Utility

Utility refers to the want-satisfying capacity of a commodity or service. It represents the satisfaction that a consumer derives or expects to derive from consuming a good or service.

Definition 1: Utility is the capacity of a commodity to satisfy human wants.

Definition 2: Utility is the satisfaction or benefit that a consumer derives from the consumption of a commodity.

Characteristics of Utility

  • Utility is subjective.
  • Utility differs from person to person.
  • Utility depends on the intensity of a want.
  • Utility can change according to time and circumstances.
  • Utility is different from usefulness.
Example: A glass of water has high utility for a thirsty person because it satisfies the person's thirst.

2. Total Utility

Total Utility (TU) is the total satisfaction obtained from consuming all units of a commodity.

Definition 1: Total Utility is the sum of utilities obtained from all units consumed.

Definition 2: Total Utility is the total satisfaction derived from consuming a given quantity of a commodity.

TU = Sum of Utility of All Units Consumed
Example: If the utility from three units is 10, 8 and 6 respectively, then:

TU = 10 + 8 + 6 = 24 units

3. Marginal Utility

Marginal Utility (MU) is the additional satisfaction obtained from consuming one additional unit of a commodity.

Definition 1: Marginal Utility is the change in Total Utility resulting from the consumption of one additional unit.

Definition 2: Marginal Utility is the additional satisfaction obtained from consuming an extra unit of a commodity.

MU = Change in TU ÷ Change in Quantity

MU = ΔTU / ΔQ
Example: If Total Utility increases from 30 to 38 after consuming one additional unit, then Marginal Utility is:

MU = 38 − 30 = 8 units

4. Relationship between TU and MU

Marginal Utility Total Utility
Positive TU increases
Positive but decreasing TU increases at a decreasing rate
Zero TU is maximum
Negative TU starts falling
⭐ Remember: Total Utility is maximum when Marginal Utility becomes zero.

5. Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility

Statement

The Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility states that as a consumer consumes more and more successive units of a commodity continuously, the marginal utility obtained from each additional unit tends to diminish, other things remaining constant.

Definition 1: The law states that marginal utility decreases as successive units of a commodity are consumed.

Definition 2: With continuous consumption of a commodity, the additional satisfaction from each additional unit tends to fall.

Example

Suppose a person is very thirsty. The first glass of water gives very high satisfaction. The second glass gives less additional satisfaction and the third glass gives still less satisfaction.

Assumptions

  • Units consumed should be identical.
  • Consumption should be continuous.
  • Tastes and preferences should remain unchanged.
  • The consumer should behave rationally.
  • The units should be of suitable size.

6. Consumer Equilibrium

Consumer equilibrium refers to a situation in which a consumer obtains maximum satisfaction from a given income and given prices and has no desire to change the level of consumption.

Definition 1: Consumer equilibrium is the position where the consumer maximises satisfaction subject to income and prices.

Definition 2: It is the position where a consumer has no incentive to change the consumption pattern because satisfaction is maximum.

Single Commodity Approach

For a single commodity, consumer equilibrium is achieved when the marginal utility of the commodity is equal to its price, assuming marginal utility is measured in monetary terms.

MU = Price of the Commodity
Important: The consumer should continue purchasing units as long as the marginal benefit is greater than the price and stop when equilibrium is reached.

7. Demand

Demand refers to the quantity of a commodity that a consumer is willing and able to buy at a particular price during a particular period of time.

Definition 1: Demand is the quantity of a commodity that a consumer is willing and able to purchase at a given price.

Definition 2: Demand is the desire for a commodity backed by willingness and ability to pay.

Essential Elements of Demand

  • Desire for the commodity
  • Willingness to buy
  • Ability to pay
  • A specific price
  • A specific period of time
⭐ Remember: Desire alone is not demand. Desire must be supported by willingness and ability to pay.

9. Demand Curve

A demand curve is a graphical representation of the relationship between the price of a commodity and its quantity demanded.

Under the law of demand, the demand curve generally slopes downward from left to right.

Price ↑ → Quantity Demanded ↓

Price ↓ → Quantity Demanded ↑

The downward slope of the demand curve represents the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded, other things remaining constant.

10. Law of Demand

Statement

The Law of Demand states that, other things remaining constant, there is an inverse relationship between the price of a commodity and its quantity demanded.

Definition 1: Other things remaining constant, quantity demanded increases when price falls and decreases when price rises.

Definition 2: There is an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded of a commodity, ceteris paribus.

Price ↑ → Demand ↓

Price ↓ → Demand ↑

Reasons for the Law of Demand

  • Law of diminishing marginal utility
  • Income effect
  • Substitution effect
  • Increase in number of buyers

11. Exceptions to the Law of Demand

In certain special situations, demand may not follow the normal inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

  • Giffen goods
  • Prestige or Veblen goods
  • Expectation of future price changes
  • Emergency situations
  • Ignorance of consumers
Example: If consumers expect the price of a commodity to rise further, they may purchase more of it even when its current price increases.

12. Determinants of Demand

Determinants of demand are the factors that influence the quantity demanded of a commodity.

  • Price of the commodity
  • Income of the consumer
  • Prices of related goods
  • Tastes and preferences
  • Expectations about future prices
  • Number of consumers
  • Distribution of income
  • Season and weather

13. Movement Along the Demand Curve

Movement along the demand curve occurs when quantity demanded changes because of a change in the price of the commodity itself, while other factors remain constant.

Expansion of Demand

Expansion of demand occurs when quantity demanded increases due to a fall in the price of the commodity.

Contraction of Demand

Contraction of demand occurs when quantity demanded decreases due to a rise in the price of the commodity.

Price changes → Movement along the same demand curve.

14. Shift in Demand Curve

A shift in the demand curve occurs when demand changes because of factors other than the price of the commodity itself.

Increase in Demand

An increase in demand shifts the demand curve to the right.

Decrease in Demand

A decrease in demand shifts the demand curve to the left.

Other factors change → Demand curve shifts.

Movement vs Shift

Basis Movement Shift
Cause Change in own price Change in other factors
Curve Same demand curve New demand curve
Types Expansion / Contraction Increase / Decrease

15. Elasticity of Demand

Elasticity of demand measures the degree of responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in one of its determinants.

Definition 1: Elasticity of demand is the measure of responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price or another determinant of demand.

Definition 2: It measures how much quantity demanded changes in response to a change in price, income or the price of related goods.

Main Types

  • Price Elasticity of Demand
  • Income Elasticity of Demand
  • Cross Elasticity of Demand

16. Price Elasticity of Demand

Price elasticity of demand measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in the price of the commodity.

Definition 1: Price elasticity of demand is the degree of responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price.

Definition 2: It measures the percentage change in quantity demanded resulting from a percentage change in price.

Ed = Percentage Change in Quantity Demanded ÷ Percentage Change in Price

Example

If the price of a commodity changes by 10% and quantity demanded changes by 20%, then the price elasticity is:

Ed = 20% ÷ 10% = 2

Therefore, demand is relatively elastic in this example.

17. Types of Price Elasticity of Demand

1. Perfectly Inelastic Demand

Quantity demanded remains unchanged despite a change in price.

2. Relatively Inelastic Demand

Percentage change in quantity demanded is less than the percentage change in price.

3. Unitary Elastic Demand

Percentage change in quantity demanded is equal to the percentage change in price.

4. Relatively Elastic Demand

Percentage change in quantity demanded is greater than the percentage change in price.

5. Perfectly Elastic Demand

A very small change in price can cause a very large change in quantity demanded.

Type Elasticity
Perfectly Inelastic Ed = 0
Relatively Inelastic Ed < 1
Unitary Elastic Ed = 1
Relatively Elastic Ed > 1
Perfectly Elastic Ed = ∞

18. Factors Affecting Price Elasticity of Demand

  • Availability of substitutes: More substitutes generally make demand more elastic.
  • Nature of commodity: Necessities generally have less elastic demand than luxuries.
  • Proportion of income: Goods taking a larger share of income tend to have more elastic demand.
  • Number of uses: Goods having several uses may have relatively elastic demand.
  • Time period: Demand generally becomes more elastic over a longer period.
  • Possibility of postponement: Goods whose consumption can be postponed generally have more elastic demand.
  • Habit: Habit-forming commodities may have relatively inelastic demand.
