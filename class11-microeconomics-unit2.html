Unit 2 — Theory of Consumer Behaviour

What you will learn:
Utility, Total Utility, Marginal Utility, Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility, Consumer Equilibrium, Demand, Law of Demand, Demand Schedule, Demand Curve, Determinants of Demand, Movement and Shift in Demand Curve, and Elasticity of Demand.

1. Utility

Meaning of Utility

Utility refers to the want-satisfying capacity of a commodity or service. It is the satisfaction that a consumer expects to obtain from consuming a good or service.

Definition 1: Utility is the capacity of a commodity to satisfy human wants.

Definition 2: Utility is the satisfaction or benefit that a consumer derives from the consumption of a commodity.

Important Points

  • Utility is subjective.
  • Utility differs from person to person.
  • Utility depends on the intensity of a want.
  • Utility can change with time and circumstances.
  • Utility is different from usefulness.

Example: A glass of water may have very high utility for a thirsty person but comparatively less utility for a person who is not thirsty.

2. Total Utility

Total Utility (TU) is the total satisfaction obtained by a consumer from the consumption of all units of a commodity.

Definition 1: Total Utility is the sum of utilities obtained from consuming all units of a commodity.

Definition 2: Total Utility is the total satisfaction derived by a consumer from the consumption of a given quantity of a commodity.

Formula

TU = Sum of utilities of all units consumed

When consumption increases, Total Utility generally increases initially, but after a point it may start increasing at a decreasing rate and can eventually become maximum.

3. Marginal Utility

Marginal Utility (MU) is the additional utility obtained from consuming one more unit of a commodity.

Definition 1: Marginal Utility is the change in Total Utility resulting from the consumption of one additional unit.

Definition 2: Marginal Utility is the additional satisfaction received from consuming an extra unit of a commodity.

Formula

MU = Change in TU ÷ Change in Quantity

MU = ΔTU / ΔQ

If one additional unit is consumed and Total Utility increases by 10 units, then Marginal Utility is 10 units.

4. Relationship between TU and MU

  • When MU is positive, TU increases.
  • When MU decreases but remains positive, TU increases at a decreasing rate.
  • When MU becomes zero, TU is maximum.
  • When MU becomes negative, TU starts falling.
Key Point: TU is maximum when MU = 0.

5. Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility

Statement

The Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility states that as a consumer consumes more and more units of a commodity continuously, the marginal utility obtained from each successive unit tends to diminish, assuming other things remain constant.

Definition 1: The law states that marginal utility decreases as successive units of a commodity are consumed.

Definition 2: With continuous consumption of a commodity, the additional satisfaction from each additional unit tends to fall.

Example

Suppose a person is very thirsty. The first glass of water gives high satisfaction. The second glass gives less additional satisfaction, and the third may give still less.

Assumptions

  • Units consumed should be identical.
  • Consumption should be continuous.
  • The consumer's tastes and preferences should remain unchanged.
  • The consumer should behave rationally.
  • The units should be of suitable size.

6. Consumer Equilibrium

Consumer equilibrium refers to a situation in which a consumer obtains maximum satisfaction from a given income and given prices of commodities and has no desire to change the level of consumption.

Definition 1: Consumer equilibrium is a situation in which the consumer maximises satisfaction subject to income and prices.

Definition 2: It is the position where a consumer has no incentive to change the consumption pattern because satisfaction is maximum.

Consumer Equilibrium and Marginal Utility

For a single commodity, consumer equilibrium is achieved when:

MU = Price of the commodity

In monetary terms, the consumer compares the marginal utility of the commodity with its price.

7. Demand

Demand refers to the quantity of a commodity that a consumer is willing and able to buy at a particular price during a particular period of time.

Definition 1: Demand is the quantity of a commodity that a consumer is willing and able to purchase at a given price.

Definition 2: Demand is the desire for a commodity backed by willingness and ability to pay for it.

Essential Elements of Demand

  • Desire for the commodity
  • Willingness to buy
  • Ability to pay
  • A specific price
  • A specific period of time
Remember: Desire alone is not demand. Desire must be supported by willingness and ability to pay.

8. Demand Schedule

A demand schedule is a tabular presentation showing different quantities of a commodity demanded at different prices during a particular period.

Types of Demand Schedule

  • Individual Demand Schedule
  • Market Demand Schedule

Example

9. Law of Demand

The Law of Demand states that, other things remaining constant, there is an inverse relationship between the price of a commodity and its quantity demanded.

Definition 1: Other things remaining constant, quantity demanded increases when price falls and decreases when price rises.

Definition 2: There is an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded of a commodity, ceteris paribus.

Price ↑ → Demand ↓
Price ↓ → Demand ↑

Reasons for the Law of Demand

  • Law of diminishing marginal utility
  • Income effect
  • Substitution effect
  • Increase in number of buyers

10. Exceptions to the Law of Demand

In certain special situations, demand may not follow the normal inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

  • Giffen goods
  • Conspicuous or prestige goods
  • Expectation of future price changes
  • Emergency situations
  • Ignorance of consumers

Example: Consumers may buy more of a commodity when they expect its price to rise further in the future.

11. Demand Curve

A demand curve is a graphical representation of the relationship between price of a commodity and its quantity demanded.

Under the law of demand, the demand curve generally slopes downward from left to right.

Important: Movement along the demand curve occurs because of a change in the price of the commodity itself.

12. Determinants of Demand

Determinants of demand are the factors that influence the quantity demanded of a commodity.

  • Price of the commodity
  • Income of the consumer
  • Prices of related goods
  • Tastes and preferences
  • Expectations about future prices
  • Number of consumers
  • Distribution of income
  • Season and weather

13. Movement Along the Demand Curve

Movement along the demand curve occurs when the quantity demanded changes because of a change in the price of the commodity itself, while other factors remain constant.

Types

  • Expansion of Demand: Quantity demanded increases due to a fall in price.
  • Contraction of Demand: Quantity demanded decreases due to a rise in price.
Price changes → Movement along the same demand curve

14. Shift in Demand Curve

A shift in the demand curve occurs when demand changes because of factors other than the price of the commodity itself.

Increase in Demand

An increase in demand shifts the demand curve to the right.

Decrease in Demand

A decrease in demand shifts the demand curve to the left.

Other factors change → Demand curve shifts

15. Elasticity of Demand

Elasticity of demand measures the degree of responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in one of its determinants.

Definition 1: Elasticity of demand is the measure of the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price or other determinants of demand.

Definition 2: It measures how much quantity demanded changes in response to a change in price, income or the price of related goods.

Price Elasticity of Demand

Price elasticity of demand measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in the price of the commodity.

Ed = Percentage Change in Quantity Demanded ÷ Percentage Change in Price

16. Types of Price Elasticity of Demand

1. Perfectly Inelastic Demand

Quantity demanded remains unchanged despite a change in price.

2. Relatively Inelastic Demand

Percentage change in quantity demanded is less than the percentage change in price.

3. Unitary Elastic Demand

Percentage change in quantity demanded is equal to the percentage change in price.

4. Relatively Elastic Demand

Percentage change in quantity demanded is greater than the percentage change in price.

5. Perfectly Elastic Demand

A very small change in price can cause a very large change in quantity demanded.

17. Factors Affecting Price Elasticity of Demand

  • Availability of substitutes
  • Nature of the commodity
  • Number of uses of the commodity
  • Proportion of income spent on the commodity
  • Time period
  • Possibility of postponing consumption
  • Habit-forming nature of the commodity
Key Point: The greater the availability of close substitutes, generally the more elastic the demand.

18. Quick Revision

  • Utility → Want-satisfying capacity of a commodity.
  • Total Utility → Total satisfaction from all units consumed.
  • Marginal Utility → Additional satisfaction from one more unit.
  • DMU → Marginal utility tends to decrease with successive consumption.
  • Consumer Equilibrium → Position of maximum satisfaction.
  • Demand → Desire backed by willingness and ability to pay.
  • Law of Demand → Price and quantity demanded generally have an inverse relationship.
  • Demand Curve → Graphical representation of price-demand relationship.
  • Movement → Caused by change in own price.
  • Shift → Caused by factors other than own price.
  • Elasticity of Demand → Responsiveness of demand to changes in its determinants.
