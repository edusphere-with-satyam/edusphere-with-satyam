Introduction to Workplace Safety

A safe workplace requires employees to recognise hazards and follow appropriate safety procedures. Physical conditions, electrical equipment and fire-related situations can create significant safety concerns if they are not managed properly.

In this session, we will study physical safety, electrical safety and fire safety in detail. We will also understand the precautions that can help prevent common workplace accidents.

Workplace Safety:

Workplace safety refers to the practices, precautions, procedures and conditions designed to protect people from accidents, injuries and other workplace hazards.

In Simple Words:

Workplace safety means taking proper precautions so that people can work safely and avoid preventable accidents.

Physical Safety

Physical safety focuses on preventing accidents caused by physical conditions, objects, equipment, working areas and movement within the workplace.

Definition:

Physical safety is the practice of maintaining the physical workplace in a condition that reduces the possibility of accidents and injuries.

Common Physical Safety Concerns

  • Slippery floors
  • Uneven surfaces
  • Objects blocking passages
  • Unsafe stairs or ladders
  • Poor lighting
  • Improperly arranged workplace materials
  • Objects that may fall
📌 Example – Unsafe Passage

Suppose several boxes are kept in a passage used regularly by employees.

The boxes may obstruct movement and create a tripping hazard. The passage should be kept clear according to workplace safety procedures.

Safety While Working at Heights

Working at an elevated location can create a fall hazard. Appropriate workplace procedures and suitable equipment must be followed whenever work involves height.

Common Causes of Fall Hazards

  • Damaged or unsuitable ladders
  • Unsafe elevated work areas
  • Unstable working surfaces
  • Poor lighting
  • Objects obstructing the work area
  • Failure to follow safety procedures

Preventive Measures

  • Use suitable equipment for the task.
  • Check equipment according to workplace procedures.
  • Keep the work area organised.
  • Maintain suitable lighting.
  • Follow authorised safety instructions.
  • Report unsafe conditions immediately.
📌 Example – Ladder Safety

An employee finds that a ladder is damaged before using it.

The employee should not ignore the unsafe condition. The damaged equipment should be reported and handled according to the workplace's safety procedure.

Preventing Slips and Trips

Slips and trips are common workplace accidents. Maintaining clean, organised and unobstructed working areas can help reduce these hazards.

Slip Trip
Occurs when a person's foot loses proper grip with the walking surface. Occurs when an object or uneven surface interferes with normal movement.
Example: Wet floor. Example: Cable or box in a walking path.

Safety Measures

  • Keep floors and passages clean.
  • Deal with spills promptly according to procedure.
  • Keep cables and materials properly arranged.
  • Keep emergency and walking routes clear.
  • Maintain adequate lighting.
  • Report damaged flooring.
📌 Example – Office Safety

A computer cable is lying across a frequently used walking path.

This can create a tripping hazard. The cable should be managed safely according to workplace procedures so that it does not obstruct movement.

Electrical Safety

Electrical equipment is an essential part of many modern workplaces. However, damaged equipment, faulty wiring and improper use can create electrical hazards.

Formal Definition:

Electrical safety refers to the practices and precautions used to prevent injury, fire, equipment damage and other harm associated with electricity and electrical equipment.

In Simple Words:

Electrical safety means using electrical equipment correctly and following proper precautions to avoid electrical accidents.

Common Electrical Hazards

  • Damaged electrical cables
  • Faulty electrical equipment
  • Improperly maintained wiring
  • Overloaded connections
  • Improper use of electrical devices
  • Ignoring electrical warnings
📌 Example – Damaged Cable

An employee notices that the outer covering of an electrical cable is damaged.

This is an electrical hazard. The condition should be reported and dealt with according to the appropriate workplace safety procedure.

Basic Electrical Safety Rules

Employees should follow workplace electrical safety procedures and use electrical equipment only in the manner for which it is intended.

  • Do not use visibly damaged electrical equipment.
  • Report damaged cables and equipment.
  • Do not ignore electrical warning signs.
  • Keep electrical equipment appropriately maintained.
  • Do not overload electrical connections.
  • Follow the manufacturer's and workplace instructions.
  • Only authorised persons should carry out electrical repair work.
Safety Reminder:

Electrical repair or maintenance should be performed only by appropriately authorised and trained personnel according to workplace procedures.

📌 Example – Unsafe Electrical Practice

An employee notices that an electrical device is malfunctioning but continues using it without reporting the problem.

This is unsafe because a faulty device may create an electrical or fire hazard. The problem should be reported and the workplace procedure should be followed.

Fire Safety

Fire safety involves preventing workplace fires where possible, recognising fire hazards and following established emergency procedures when a fire-related situation occurs.

Formal Definition:

Fire safety refers to the practices, precautions and procedures used to prevent fires, reduce fire-related risks and protect people and property during a fire emergency.

In Simple Words:

Fire safety means taking precautions to prevent fires and knowing how to respond safely when a fire emergency occurs.

Common Fire Hazards

  • Faulty electrical equipment
  • Improperly maintained electrical systems
  • Unsafe storage of combustible materials
  • Blocked emergency routes
  • Careless workplace practices
  • Failure to follow fire-safety procedures
📌 Example – Electrical Fire Hazard

Faulty electrical equipment can become a source of fire risk if it is not properly maintained or reported.

Employees should report faulty equipment and follow the workplace's established safety procedures.

Fire Prevention Measures

Fire prevention begins with identifying possible sources of fire and maintaining the workplace according to established safety procedures.

  • Follow workplace fire-safety policies.
  • Keep emergency routes accessible.
  • Maintain electrical equipment appropriately.
  • Store materials safely.
  • Keep work areas organised.
  • Report fire hazards promptly.
  • Know the workplace emergency procedures.
  • Participate in authorised safety training and drills.
Important:

During an emergency, employees should follow the workplace's established emergency plan and instructions from responsible personnel.

What to Do During a Fire Emergency

A fire emergency requires calm and responsible action. Workplace emergency procedures should always take priority.

  1. Raise or report the alarm according to workplace procedure.
  2. Inform the responsible emergency personnel.
  3. Follow the designated evacuation procedure.
  4. Use the designated emergency exit or route.
  5. Do not block emergency routes.
  6. Follow instructions from authorised personnel.
  7. Move to the designated assembly area if instructed.
Remember:

The priority during a workplace fire emergency is the safety of people. Employees should follow the organisation's emergency plan rather than attempting actions outside their training or authority.

📌 Example – Office Fire Alarm

Suppose a fire alarm is activated in an office.

Employees should follow the organisation's emergency evacuation procedure, use the designated route and follow the instructions of authorised personnel.

Fire Safety Equipment and Arrangements

Workplaces may have different fire-safety equipment and emergency arrangements depending on the type of workplace and applicable safety requirements.

Examples

  • Fire alarms
  • Emergency exits
  • Emergency lighting
  • Fire extinguishers
  • Emergency assembly areas
  • Fire-safety signs
📌 Why Are Fire-Safety Signs Important?

Fire-safety signs help people identify emergency exits, equipment and other important safety information quickly.

Physical, Electrical and Fire Hazards

Type Meaning Example Basic Prevention
Physical Hazard A physical condition, object or activity that may cause injury. Wet floor or obstruction. Keep workplace clean, organised and maintained.
Electrical Hazard An unsafe condition involving electrical equipment, wiring or power. Damaged electrical cable. Report faults and follow electrical safety procedures.
Fire Hazard A condition, material or activity that may cause or contribute to a fire. Faulty electrical equipment. Follow fire-safety procedures and report hazards.

Practical Activity – Identify Safety Hazards

Observe the following workplace situations and identify the hazard and appropriate preventive action.

🔎 Situation 1 – Wet Floor

Water has been spilled on the floor near a workplace entrance.

Hazard: Slipping hazard.

Why dangerous: A person may lose balance while walking over the wet surface.

Appropriate action: Report the condition and follow the workplace procedure for dealing with the spill and warning people in the area.

🔎 Situation 2 – Damaged Electrical Cable

A computer workstation has a visibly damaged electrical cable.

Hazard: Electrical hazard.

Why dangerous: Damaged electrical equipment can create an unsafe condition.

Appropriate action: Report the problem and follow the workplace electrical-safety procedure.

🔎 Situation 3 – Blocked Passage

Several boxes are stored in an emergency or walking passage.

Hazard: Physical and tripping hazard.

Why dangerous: The boxes can obstruct movement and may interfere with safe evacuation.

Appropriate action: Report the obstruction and ensure that the area is managed according to workplace procedures.

🔎 Situation 4 – Faulty Electrical Equipment

An electrical device is not functioning correctly but an employee continues to use it.

Hazard: Electrical and possible fire hazard.

Why dangerous: Faulty equipment may create unsafe electrical conditions.

Appropriate action: Stop using the equipment as appropriate and report the fault according to workplace procedure.

🔎 Situation 5 – Unsafe Ladder

A ladder appears damaged but is being considered for use.

Hazard: Fall-from-height hazard.

Why dangerous: Damaged equipment may not provide safe support.

Appropriate action: Do not ignore the unsafe condition. Report it and follow the workplace procedure.

Important Questions

1. What is physical safety?
View Answer

Physical safety is the practice of maintaining the physical workplace in a condition that reduces the possibility of accidents and injuries.

2. What are the common causes of slips and trips?
View Answer

Common causes include wet floors, spilled liquids, obstructions, uneven surfaces, poor lighting and untidy workplace areas.

3. What is electrical safety?
View Answer

Electrical safety refers to practices and precautions used to prevent injury, fire, equipment damage and other harm associated with electricity and electrical equipment.

4. Mention any four electrical hazards.
View Answer
  • Damaged electrical cables
  • Faulty electrical equipment
  • Improperly maintained wiring
  • Overloaded electrical connections
5. What is fire safety?
View Answer

Fire safety refers to practices, precautions and procedures used to prevent fires, reduce fire-related risks and protect people and property during a fire emergency.

6. Mention any four fire hazards.
View Answer
  • Faulty electrical equipment
  • Improperly maintained electrical systems
  • Unsafe storage of combustible materials
  • Blocked emergency routes
7. Why should emergency routes be kept clear?
View Answer

Emergency routes should be kept clear so that people can move safely and efficiently during an emergency evacuation.

8. Why should damaged electrical equipment be reported?
View Answer

Damaged electrical equipment can create an unsafe condition and may contribute to injury, equipment damage or fire. Reporting it allows the appropriate safety procedure to be followed.

Application-Based Questions

1. A worker notices water spilled near a computer workstation. What should the worker do?
View Answer

The worker should recognise the slipping and possible electrical hazard, keep people away from the unsafe area as appropriate, and report or address the condition according to workplace safety procedures.

2. Why should only authorised persons perform electrical repair work?
View Answer

Electrical repair work can involve significant safety risks. It should therefore be carried out only by appropriately trained and authorised personnel according to workplace procedures.

3. Why can boxes kept in an emergency passage be dangerous?
View Answer

They can obstruct movement and may interfere with safe evacuation during an emergency.

4. An employee sees faulty electrical equipment. Why should it not simply be ignored?
View Answer

Faulty electrical equipment may create an electrical or fire hazard. It should be reported and handled according to the appropriate workplace procedure.

5. What should employees do when a workplace fire alarm is activated?
View Answer

Employees should follow the workplace emergency procedure, use designated evacuation routes and follow instructions from authorised personnel.
