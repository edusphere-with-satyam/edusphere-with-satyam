Introduction to Workplace Health, Safety and Security

Every workplace should provide an environment in which people can work safely, remain healthy and protect themselves, other people, equipment and important resources. A workplace may be an office, school, factory, shop, hospital, laboratory or any other place where people perform work-related activities.

Workplace health, safety and security are closely related concepts, but they do not mean exactly the same thing. Health mainly concerns the well-being of people, safety focuses on preventing accidents and unsafe situations, while security focuses on protecting people, property, information and resources from threats.

Workplace Health – Formal Definition:

Workplace health refers to the physical and general well-being of people at the workplace and the conditions that help protect them from work-related health problems.

In Simple Words:

Workplace health means keeping people healthy and providing working conditions that do not unnecessarily harm their health.

Workplace Health

Workplace health is concerned with maintaining conditions that support the well-being of employees and other people at the workplace. A healthy workplace should be clean, hygienic and reasonably comfortable for the work being performed.

Definition:

Workplace health is the practice of maintaining working conditions that promote the physical and general well-being of workers and reduce work-related health risks.

Important Elements of Workplace Health

  • Clean and hygienic working conditions
  • Proper ventilation
  • Adequate lighting
  • Suitable working conditions
  • Clean drinking water where required
  • Proper workplace cleanliness
  • Awareness of health-related risks
📌 Example – Healthy Workplace

Imagine an office where the rooms are clean, the work area is properly ventilated, lighting is suitable and employees are encouraged to maintain cleanliness.

Such conditions contribute to a healthier working environment because they help reduce unnecessary health-related risks and make the workplace more suitable for work.

Workplace Safety

Workplace safety is concerned with preventing accidents, injuries and other unsafe situations. Employees should understand the safety rules applicable to their workplace and follow them while performing their duties.

Formal Definition:

Workplace safety refers to the practices, rules and measures used to prevent accidents, injuries and unsafe conditions during work.

In Simple Words:

Workplace safety means doing work in a way that reduces the possibility of accidents and keeps people and the workplace safe.

Important Safety Practices

  • Follow workplace safety rules.
  • Use equipment according to instructions.
  • Keep the work area organised.
  • Report unsafe conditions.
  • Follow warning signs and instructions.
  • Follow emergency procedures.
  • Do not misuse workplace equipment.
📌 Example – Reporting an Unsafe Condition

Suppose an employee notices that a piece of workplace equipment is damaged. Instead of continuing to use it, the employee reports the problem to the responsible person.

Reporting the problem helps prevent the unsafe condition from causing an accident and is therefore an important safety practice.

Workplace Security

Security protects people, property, information and other important resources from unauthorised access, misuse, theft, damage or other threats.

Formal Definition:

Workplace security refers to the measures and procedures used to protect people, property, information and resources from unauthorised access, misuse, loss or other security threats.

In Simple Words:

Workplace security means protecting the workplace and its resources from people or situations that may cause loss, misuse or harm.

Examples of Security Measures

  • Controlling access to restricted areas
  • Protecting important documents
  • Using authorised access systems
  • Protecting workplace equipment
  • Following information-security rules
  • Reporting suspicious or unauthorised activities
📌 Example – Restricted Area

A company may have a room containing important equipment or confidential information. Only authorised employees may be allowed to enter that room.

Controlling access helps protect the equipment and information and is therefore a security measure.

Health, Safety and Security – Difference

Health, safety and security are connected, but each has a different primary purpose.

Basis Health Safety Security
Main Concern Well-being and health of people Prevention of accidents and unsafe situations Protection from threats, unauthorised access or misuse
Focus Healthy working conditions Safe working conditions Protection of people, property and resources
Example Clean and properly ventilated workplace Following equipment safety instructions Restricting access to a confidential area

Importance of a Healthy, Safe and Secure Workplace

A healthy, safe and secure workplace benefits both employees and organisations. Safety should not be treated only as a set of rules; it should become part of normal workplace behaviour.

Prevents Accidents

Following safety rules and identifying unsafe conditions can help reduce the possibility of workplace accidents.

Protects Employees

Proper workplace practices help protect employees from avoidable risks and unsafe conditions.

Improves Working Conditions

A clean, organised and safe workplace provides better conditions for employees to perform their duties.

Protects Equipment and Property

Proper safety and security practices can help reduce damage, misuse and loss of workplace resources.

Improves Productivity

When employees can work in an organised and safe environment, unnecessary interruptions caused by accidents and unsafe conditions can be reduced.

Creates a Responsible Workplace Culture

When everyone follows safety procedures and reports hazards, safety becomes a shared responsibility rather than the responsibility of only one person.

📌 Example – Why Workplace Safety Matters

Consider a workplace where employees regularly leave work areas untidy and ignore safety instructions. Such behaviour may create unnecessary risks.

If employees instead keep the workplace organised, follow instructions and report unsafe conditions, the workplace becomes more orderly and safer.

Health, Safety and Security Policies

Organisations establish policies to provide clear guidance about how employees should behave and what standards should be followed at the workplace.

Definition of Policy:

A workplace policy is a formal set of principles, rules or guidelines established by an organisation to guide behaviour and decision-making in a particular area.

In Simple Words:

A policy tells people what rules or standards they are expected to follow.

Purpose of Workplace Safety Policies

  • Provide clear safety guidelines
  • Define expected workplace behaviour
  • Help prevent unsafe practices
  • Promote awareness of workplace risks
  • Support consistent safety practices
  • Help employees understand their responsibilities
📌 Example – Safety Policy

An organisation may establish a policy stating that employees must follow safety instructions while using workplace equipment.

The policy establishes the expected standard of behaviour. The detailed steps for following that policy can be provided through procedures.

Health, Safety and Security Procedures

A policy provides general direction, while a procedure explains the steps or method that should be followed to carry out a task safely and correctly.

Definition of Procedure:

A workplace procedure is a defined sequence of steps or actions used to perform a task or respond to a situation in accordance with organisational rules and requirements.

In Simple Words:

A procedure explains how a rule or policy should be followed in practice.

Why Procedures Are Important

  • Provide clear instructions
  • Reduce confusion
  • Promote consistent behaviour
  • Help employees perform tasks safely
  • Provide guidance during emergencies
📌 Example – Policy and Procedure

Policy: Workplace equipment must be used safely.

Procedure: Before using the equipment, the employee should follow the provided instructions, check for obvious problems and report any unsafe condition according to workplace rules.

Policy vs Procedure

Basis Policy Procedure
Meaning A rule or general guideline A sequence of steps for carrying out a task
Purpose Provides direction and establishes standards Explains how the standard should be implemented
Nature Generally broad Generally more specific
Example Employees must follow workplace safety rules. Employees follow the specified steps for safely performing a task.

Why Health, Safety and Security Programs Are Needed

A workplace safety program is a planned approach used by an organisation to promote safe working practices, identify risks, communicate responsibilities and support the prevention of accidents.

Prevention of Accidents

Safety programs help organisations identify unsafe practices and encourage employees to follow appropriate safety measures.

Protection of Employees

A major purpose of safety programs is to protect workers from unnecessary workplace risks.

Identification of Risks

Organisations need to recognise situations that may cause harm or loss so that appropriate preventive measures can be taken.

Emergency Preparedness

Employees should know how to respond appropriately when an emergency occurs. Safety programs can provide guidance and establish responsibilities.

Protection of Workplace Resources

Proper safety and security practices can also help protect equipment, property, documents and other resources.

Development of Safety Awareness

Regular communication and training can help employees understand the importance of safe behaviour.

📌 Example – Safety Program

An organisation may have a program that includes safety instructions, employee training, reporting of unsafe conditions, emergency procedures and regular checks of the workplace.

Such a program helps make safety an organised and continuous activity rather than something considered only after an accident.

Responsibilities of Employees

Employees play an important role in maintaining a healthy, safe and secure workplace. Safety cannot be maintained only through rules; employees must also follow those rules responsibly.

  • Follow workplace health and safety rules.
  • Use workplace equipment correctly.
  • Follow instructions provided by authorised persons.
  • Keep the work area clean and organised.
  • Report unsafe conditions.
  • Report accidents or incidents according to workplace procedures.
  • Follow emergency instructions.
  • Protect workplace property and resources.
  • Do not misuse workplace equipment.
  • Cooperate with workplace safety programs.
📌 Example – Employee Responsibility

If an employee notices an unsafe condition, simply ignoring it may allow the problem to continue. Reporting it to the appropriate person allows corrective action to be considered.

Responsibilities of Employers

Employers and organisations are responsible for establishing and maintaining appropriate workplace systems that support health, safety and security.

  • Provide a safe working environment.
  • Identify workplace hazards and risks.
  • Establish appropriate safety policies and procedures.
  • Provide safety instructions and awareness.
  • Maintain workplace equipment properly.
  • Provide appropriate safety resources where required.
  • Establish emergency arrangements.
  • Take appropriate action when hazards are reported.
  • Promote responsible safety behaviour.
  • Review and improve workplace safety practices.
📌 Example – Employer Responsibility

If an organisation identifies an unsafe condition in the workplace, it should take appropriate steps to address the problem rather than simply expecting employees to ignore it.

Safety Culture at the Workplace

Safety should not be treated as a responsibility of only the safety officer or manager. A good workplace safety culture develops when everyone understands the importance of safe behaviour and takes appropriate responsibility.

Definition:

Safety culture refers to the shared attitudes, values, behaviours and practices through which an organisation demonstrates its commitment to workplace safety.

In Simple Words:

A safety culture means that people at the workplace regularly think about safety, follow safety rules and take responsibility for preventing unsafe situations.

Features of a Good Safety Culture

  • Employees take safety seriously.
  • Safety rules are clearly communicated.
  • Unsafe conditions are reported.
  • Employees cooperate with one another.
  • Management supports safe practices.
  • Safety awareness is maintained regularly.

Practical Activity – Identify Safe and Unsafe Practices

Read each situation and decide whether it represents a safe or unsafe workplace practice. Then identify the reason and suggest the appropriate action.

🛠️ Situation 1 – Wet Floor

A workplace floor is wet, but there is no warning sign and people continue walking through the area.

Answer: Unsafe practice.

The wet floor may create a risk of slipping. The area should be dealt with according to workplace safety procedures and an appropriate warning should be provided where necessary.

🛠️ Situation 2 – Reporting a Problem

An employee notices a damaged piece of workplace equipment and reports it to the responsible person instead of using it.

Answer: Safe and responsible practice.

Reporting an unsafe condition helps the organisation take appropriate action.

🛠️ Situation 3 – Blocked Emergency Exit

Boxes have been placed in front of an emergency exit.

Answer: Unsafe practice.

An emergency exit should remain accessible according to workplace safety procedures.

🛠️ Situation 4 – Following Instructions

An employee follows the instructions provided for using workplace equipment.

Answer: Safe practice.

Following appropriate instructions reduces the possibility of misuse and unsafe operation.

🛠️ Situation 5 – Ignoring a Safety Rule

An employee knows a workplace safety rule but deliberately ignores it to complete a task more quickly.

Answer: Unsafe practice.

Completing work quickly should not be treated as a reason to ignore applicable safety rules.

Application-Based Questions

1. An employee notices an unsafe condition but does not report it. What safety responsibility is being ignored?
View Answer

The employee is failing to report an unsafe condition. Reporting hazards is an important employee responsibility because it allows appropriate action to be considered.

2. An organisation has safety rules but does not explain how employees should follow them. What additional element is needed?
View Answer

Appropriate procedures are needed. A policy generally establishes the rule or standard, while a procedure explains the steps or method for implementing it.

3. A company restricts access to a room containing confidential information. Is this mainly a health, safety or security measure?
View Answer

It is mainly a security measure because it protects information and resources from unauthorised access.

4. A workplace is clean and well organised, employees follow safety instructions, and hazards are reported. What does this indicate?
View Answer

These practices indicate a positive workplace safety culture because safety is being treated as a shared responsibility and employees are following responsible practices.
