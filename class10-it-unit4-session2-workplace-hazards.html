Introduction to Workplace Hazards

A workplace should be organised in such a way that people can perform their duties without being unnecessarily exposed to unsafe conditions. However, different workplaces may contain situations that can cause accidents, injuries, damage or health problems.

Such potentially harmful conditions or sources are known as workplace hazards. Identifying hazards is an important part of maintaining a safe working environment.

Formal Definition:

A workplace hazard is a condition, activity, object or source that has the potential to cause injury, illness, damage or loss in a workplace.

In Simple Words:

A hazard is something at the workplace that can potentially cause harm or create an unsafe situation.

📌 Example – Workplace Hazard

Suppose a workplace has a damaged electrical cable lying in an area where people work.

The damaged cable is a potential hazard because it can create an unsafe electrical condition. It should be reported and dealt with according to the workplace's safety procedures.

Hazard and Risk

The terms hazard and risk are related but they are not exactly the same.

Hazard:

A source or condition that has the potential to cause harm.

Risk:

The possibility that a hazard may cause harm under particular circumstances.

Hazard Risk
A source or condition that can potentially cause harm. The possibility of harm occurring because of that hazard.
Example: A wet floor. Example: A person may slip and fall because of the wet floor.
📌 Example – Understanding Hazard and Risk

A wet floor is the hazard.

The possibility that someone may slip and fall because of the wet floor represents the associated risk.

Workplace Safety Hazards

Workplace safety hazards can arise from the physical environment, equipment, electrical systems, workplace activities, fire-related conditions and other sources.

Recognising these hazards helps employees and organisations take appropriate preventive measures.

Common Categories Covered in This Unit

  • Physical Hazards
  • Falling from Heights
  • Slipping and Tripping
  • Electrical Hazards
  • Fire Hazards
  • Health Hazards
Important:

Different workplaces may have different hazards. For example, an office, factory, laboratory and construction site may not have the same safety risks.

Physical Hazards

Physical hazards arise from aspects of the physical work environment that may cause injury or harm. These may include floors, stairs, work areas, equipment, machinery and other physical conditions.

Formal Definition:

Physical hazards are hazards arising from physical conditions, objects, equipment or activities in the workplace that may cause injury or harm.

In Simple Words:

Physical hazards are unsafe physical conditions or objects in the workplace that can cause accidents or injuries.

Examples of Physical Hazards

  • Uneven floors
  • Damaged stairs
  • Objects placed in walking areas
  • Unsafe workplace arrangements
  • Improperly maintained equipment
  • Objects that may fall from an unsafe position
📌 Example – Physical Hazard

If boxes are placed in a narrow passage where people regularly walk, they may create an obstruction.

The obstruction is a physical hazard because it can interfere with safe movement through the workplace.

Falling from Heights

Working at a height can create a serious workplace hazard when appropriate precautions are not followed. Falls may occur because of unsafe ladders, faulty structures, poor working conditions or loss of balance.

Definition:

A fall-from-height hazard occurs when a person may fall from an elevated position such as a ladder, platform or other raised work area.

Possible Causes

  • Unsafe or damaged ladders
  • Unsafe elevated work areas
  • Loss of balance
  • Poor visibility or lighting
  • Unsafe working practices
  • Failure to follow applicable safety procedures

Basic Prevention

  • Use appropriate equipment correctly.
  • Follow workplace safety procedures.
  • Keep working areas properly maintained.
  • Ensure suitable lighting where required.
  • Report damaged equipment or unsafe conditions.
📌 Example – Fall Hazard

A damaged ladder is being used in a workplace even though employees know that it is not in proper condition.

The damaged ladder represents a potential fall hazard. Employees should not ignore the problem; it should be reported and handled according to the workplace safety procedure.

Slipping and Tripping

Slips and trips are common types of workplace hazards. They can occur when a person loses balance because of the condition of the floor, an obstruction or another unsafe situation.

Slip:

A slip occurs when a person's foot loses proper contact or grip with a walking surface, causing loss of balance.

Trip:

A trip occurs when a person's foot or movement is obstructed, causing loss of balance.

Common Causes

  • Wet or slippery floors
  • Spilled liquids
  • Objects left in walking areas
  • Uneven surfaces
  • Poor lighting
  • Untidy work areas

Prevention

  • Keep walking areas clear.
  • Clean spills promptly according to workplace procedures.
  • Maintain suitable lighting.
  • Repair damaged surfaces when required.
  • Keep workplace materials properly arranged.
  • Report unsafe conditions.
📌 Example – Slip Hazard

Water has been spilled on a workplace floor and people are walking through the same area.

The wet surface can create a slipping hazard. The situation should be addressed according to workplace safety procedures and appropriate warning measures should be used where required.

📌 Example – Trip Hazard

A cable has been left across a walking path.

The cable can obstruct movement and create a tripping hazard. It should be arranged safely according to workplace procedures.

Electrical Hazards

Electricity is widely used in modern workplaces. Electrical equipment and systems can become hazardous when they are damaged, improperly maintained or used incorrectly.

Formal Definition:

Electrical hazards are unsafe conditions involving electrical equipment, wiring, power sources or electrical systems that may cause injury, fire, equipment damage or other harm.

In Simple Words:

An electrical hazard is an unsafe electrical condition that can potentially cause harm.

Common Causes

  • Damaged wires or cables
  • Faulty electrical equipment
  • Improper use of electrical devices
  • Overloaded electrical connections
  • Improper maintenance
  • Ignoring electrical safety instructions

Basic Prevention

  • Use electrical equipment according to instructions.
  • Report damaged cables or equipment.
  • Do not ignore electrical warning signs.
  • Ensure equipment is maintained appropriately.
  • Follow authorised workplace procedures.
📌 Example – Electrical Hazard

An employee notices that the outer covering of an electrical cable is damaged.

The damaged cable represents a potential electrical hazard. The employee should report it and follow the organisation's electrical safety procedure rather than ignoring the problem.

Fire Hazards

A fire hazard is any condition or source in a workplace that may contribute to the occurrence or spread of fire. Fire hazards may arise from electrical equipment, combustible materials, unsafe practices or other workplace conditions.

Formal Definition:

A fire hazard is a condition, material, equipment or activity that has the potential to cause or contribute to a workplace fire.

Common Sources of Fire Hazards

  • Faulty electrical equipment
  • Improperly maintained electrical systems
  • Unsafe storage of combustible materials
  • Careless workplace practices
  • Blocked or poorly maintained emergency arrangements

Basic Prevention

  • Follow workplace fire-safety procedures.
  • Keep work areas organised.
  • Report faulty equipment.
  • Store materials appropriately.
  • Keep emergency routes accessible.
  • Follow instructions during emergencies.
📌 Example – Fire Hazard

A workplace has faulty electrical equipment that is known to have a problem but continues to be used without following the appropriate safety procedure.

Faulty electrical equipment can contribute to a fire hazard. The problem should be reported and dealt with according to workplace procedures.

Health Hazards

Some workplace conditions may affect a person's health over time rather than causing an immediate accident. Such conditions are considered health hazards.

Formal Definition:

Health hazards are workplace conditions, substances, activities or environmental factors that may adversely affect the health or well-being of workers.

In Simple Words:

A health hazard is something at work that may negatively affect a person's health.

Examples of Workplace Health Hazards

  • Poor workplace cleanliness
  • Inadequate ventilation
  • Unsuitable working conditions
  • Exposure to harmful workplace conditions
  • Poorly maintained work environments

Basic Prevention

  • Maintain workplace cleanliness.
  • Follow health and safety procedures.
  • Report unhealthy workplace conditions.
  • Maintain suitable workplace conditions.
  • Follow appropriate instructions and precautions.
📌 Example – Health Hazard

A workplace has poor ventilation and the air does not circulate properly.

Poor ventilation may contribute to an unhealthy working environment. The condition should be reported and addressed according to appropriate workplace procedures.

Identifying Workplace Hazards

Hazard identification means recognising conditions, objects, activities or situations that may cause harm in a workplace. Identifying hazards is an important step in maintaining workplace safety.

Basic Steps for Hazard Identification

  1. Observe the workplace carefully.
  2. Look for unsafe conditions.
  3. Identify objects or activities that may cause harm.
  4. Consider who may be affected.
  5. Report the identified hazard.
  6. Follow the appropriate workplace procedure.
📌 Example – Hazard Identification

Imagine that a workplace inspection shows that a walking passage contains several boxes.

Hazard: Obstructed walking passage.

Possible risk: A person may trip or have difficulty moving safely through the passage.

Action: Report the condition and arrange the area safely according to workplace procedures.

Case Study – Identifying Hazards

Read the following situation and identify the different hazards.

A small office has a wet floor near the entrance. A damaged electrical cable is lying near a workstation, and several boxes have been placed in a passage. The lighting in one area is also inadequate.

🔍 View Analysis

Hazard 1: Wet floor – possible slipping hazard.

Hazard 2: Damaged electrical cable – potential electrical hazard.

Hazard 3: Boxes in the passage – physical/trip hazard.

Hazard 4: Inadequate lighting – may contribute to unsafe working or movement conditions.

These conditions should be reported and addressed through the appropriate workplace safety procedures.

Important Questions

1. What is a workplace hazard?
View Answer

A workplace hazard is a condition, activity, object or source that has the potential to cause injury, illness, damage or loss in a workplace.

2. Differentiate between a hazard and a risk.
View Answer

A hazard is a source or condition that has the potential to cause harm, whereas risk refers to the possibility that the hazard may actually cause harm under particular circumstances.

3. What are physical hazards?
View Answer

Physical hazards are hazards arising from physical conditions, objects, equipment or activities in the workplace that may cause injury or harm.

4. What causes slips and trips at the workplace?
View Answer

Slips and trips may be caused by wet or slippery floors, spilled liquids, objects left in walking areas, uneven surfaces, poor lighting and untidy work areas.

5. What is an electrical hazard?
View Answer

An electrical hazard is an unsafe condition involving electrical equipment, wiring, power sources or electrical systems that may cause injury, fire, equipment damage or other harm.

6. What is a fire hazard?
View Answer

A fire hazard is a condition, material, equipment or activity that has the potential to cause or contribute to a workplace fire.

7. What are health hazards?
View Answer

Health hazards are workplace conditions, substances, activities or environmental factors that may adversely affect the health or well-being of workers.

8. Why is hazard identification important?
View Answer

Hazard identification helps recognise unsafe conditions before they cause harm and allows appropriate preventive or corrective action to be taken.

Application-Based Questions

1. A student notices water spilled on a workplace floor. What type of hazard may this create?
View Answer

It may create a slipping hazard because a person could lose balance on the wet surface.

2. Boxes are placed in a passage used by employees. What hazard may this create?
View Answer

The boxes may create a physical or tripping hazard because they obstruct the walking area.

3. An employee notices a damaged electrical cable. What should the employee do?
View Answer

The employee should report the damaged cable to the appropriate responsible person and follow the workplace's electrical safety procedure.

4. Why can poor workplace lighting become a safety concern?
View Answer

Poor lighting can make it more difficult to see obstacles, equipment or unsafe conditions and may therefore contribute to accidents or unsafe working conditions.

5. A workplace has poor ventilation. Which type of hazard may this represent?
View Answer

Poor ventilation may represent a health hazard because it can contribute to an unhealthy working environment.

CBSE PYQ-Based Practice

The following questions are provided as CBSE-style/PYQ-based practice. Specific examination years are not assigned unless the question has been verified as an actual CBSE question.

1. Explain any four types of workplace hazards.
View Answer

Four types of workplace hazards are:

  • Physical hazards: Unsafe physical conditions, objects or equipment that may cause injury.
  • Electrical hazards: Unsafe conditions involving electrical equipment, wiring or power sources.
  • Fire hazards: Conditions or materials that may cause or contribute to a fire.
  • Health hazards: Workplace conditions that may adversely affect health or well-being.
2. What precautions can help prevent slips and trips at the workplace?
View Answer

Walking areas should be kept clear, spills should be dealt with promptly according to workplace procedures, lighting should be suitable, damaged surfaces should be reported or repaired as appropriate, and workplace materials should be properly arranged.

3. Why should workplace hazards be identified?
View Answer

Identifying hazards helps recognise unsafe conditions before they cause harm and supports appropriate preventive or corrective action.
