📘 Unit 3 — Production and Costs

What you will learn:
Production, factors of production, production function, short run and long run, Total Product, Average Product, Marginal Product, Law of Variable Proportions, returns to a factor, cost concepts, fixed cost, variable cost, total cost, average cost, marginal cost and relationships among different cost concepts.

Production is one of the most important activities of an economy. A producer combines different inputs such as land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship to produce goods and services.

1. Production

🏭 Meaning of Production

Definition 1:
Production is the process of transforming inputs into goods and services that satisfy human wants.
Definition 2:
Production refers to the creation or addition of utility to goods and services.
Simple meaning: Production means using resources to create goods and services or to increase their usefulness.
Example:
A bakery uses flour, labour, electricity and machines to produce bread. The bread produced is the output, while the resources used are inputs.
2. Factors of Production

🌱 Factors of Production

Factors of production are the resources used in the production of goods and services.

Factor Meaning Reward
Land Natural resources used in production Rent
Labour Human physical and mental effort Wages
Capital Man-made resources used for production Interest
Entrepreneur Organises production and bears risk Profit
Remember:
Land → Rent
Labour → Wages
Capital → Interest
Entrepreneur → Profit
3. Fixed and Variable Factors

⚙️ Fixed Factors and Variable Factors

Fixed Factor — Definition 1:
A fixed factor is a factor whose quantity cannot be changed during a given short period.
Fixed Factor — Definition 2:
A factor whose quantity remains constant even when the level of output changes in the short run is called a fixed factor.
Examples: Factory building, heavy machinery and land may be treated as fixed factors in the short run.
Variable Factor — Definition 1:
A variable factor is a factor whose quantity can be changed according to the level of output.
Variable Factor — Definition 2:
A factor whose quantity changes with changes in output in the short run is called a variable factor.
Example: Labour and raw materials are commonly treated as variable factors in the short run.
4. Production Function

📊 Production Function

Definition 1:
A production function shows the functional relationship between inputs and the maximum output that can be produced from them.
Definition 2:
Production function represents the technical relationship between physical inputs and physical output.
Q = f(L, K)

Here, Q = output, L = labour and K = capital.

Important: A production function is a technical relationship. It does not directly show the prices of inputs or output.
5. Short Run and Long Run

⏳ Short Run and Long Run

Short Run: A period in which at least one factor of production is fixed.
Long Run: A period in which all factors of production can be varied.
Basis Short Run Long Run
Factors At least one factor is fixed All factors can be varied
Capacity Cannot be fully changed Can be changed
Costs Fixed and variable costs exist All costs are variable
6. Total Product

📈 Total Product (TP)

Definition 1:
Total Product is the total quantity of output produced by a firm with given quantities of inputs.
Definition 2:
TP is the total output produced by all units of a variable factor working with given fixed factors.
Example:
If 5 workers produce 100 units of output, Total Product = 100 units.
7. Average Product

📊 Average Product (AP)

Definition 1:
Average Product is output per unit of the variable factor.
Definition 2:
AP measures the average contribution of each unit of the variable factor to total output.
AP = TP ÷ Units of Variable Factor
If 5 workers produce 100 units:

AP = 100 ÷ 5 = 20 units
8. Marginal Product

➕ Marginal Product (MP)

Definition 1:
Marginal Product is the change in Total Product resulting from the employment of one additional unit of the variable factor.
Definition 2:
MP is the additional output produced by using one more unit of the variable input, while other factors remain constant.
MP = ΔTP ÷ ΔL

Where ΔTP means change in Total Product and ΔL means change in labour.

9. TP, AP and MP

🔗 Relationship Between TP, AP and MP

Relationship 1:
When MP is positive, TP increases.
Relationship 2:
When MP becomes zero, TP reaches its maximum.
Relationship 3:
When MP becomes negative, TP starts falling.
Relationship 4:
When MP > AP, AP rises.
Relationship 5:
When MP < AP, AP falls.
Relationship 6:
When MP = AP, AP is maximum.
10. Law of Variable Proportions

⚖️ Law of Variable Proportions

Definition 1:
The Law of Variable Proportions states that when more units of a variable factor are employed with fixed factors, Total Product initially increases at an increasing rate, then at a decreasing rate, and may eventually decrease.
Definition 2:
It studies the changes in output when one factor is varied while other factors remain fixed.
Key assumption:
Technology remains unchanged and at least one factor remains fixed.
11. Three Stages

🔵 Three Stages of Production

Stage TP MP AP
Stage I Increases at increasing rate, then decreasing rate Positive Rises and reaches maximum
Stage II Increases at decreasing rate Positive but falling Falls
Stage III Falls Negative Falls
Rational producer operates in Stage II.
Stage I ends when AP is maximum and MP = AP.

Stage II ends when MP = 0 and TP is maximum.
12. Returns to a Factor

📈 Returns to a Factor

Returns to a factor explain how output changes when additional units of a variable factor are combined with fixed factors.

Increasing Returns:
Output increases at an increasing rate as additional units of the variable factor are employed.
Diminishing Returns:
Output continues to increase but at a decreasing rate.
Negative Returns:
Output decreases when additional units of the variable factor are added.

💰 PART B — THEORY OF COST

Cost refers to the expenditure incurred by a producer in using resources for producing goods and services.
13. Cost

💰 Meaning of Cost

Definition 1:
Cost is the expenditure incurred by a producer in producing a good or service.
Definition 2:
Cost represents the value of resources used in the production process.
Example: Payments for raw materials, wages, electricity, rent and other resources used in production form part of production costs.
14. Fixed Cost

🔒 Fixed Cost (FC)

Definition 1:
Fixed cost is a cost that does not change with the level of output in the short run.
Definition 2:
Fixed cost is the cost incurred on fixed factors of production.
Examples: Rent of factory, insurance and certain fixed administrative expenses.
Important: Fixed cost exists even when output is zero.
15. Variable Cost

🔄 Variable Cost (VC)

Definition 1:
Variable cost changes with the level of output.
Definition 2:
Variable cost is the expenditure incurred on variable factors of production.
Examples: Raw materials, power used in production and wages of variable labour.
Important: Variable cost is generally zero when output is zero.
16. Total Cost

🧮 Total Cost (TC)

Definition 1:
Total Cost is the sum of Total Fixed Cost and Total Variable Cost.
Definition 2:
Total Cost is the total expenditure incurred by a producer to produce a given level of output.
TC = TFC + TVC
17. Average Fixed Cost

📉 Average Fixed Cost (AFC)

Definition 1:
Average Fixed Cost is fixed cost per unit of output.
Definition 2:
AFC shows the average amount of fixed cost borne by each unit of output.
AFC = TFC ÷ Q
Important: AFC continuously falls as output increases because the same fixed cost is spread over more units.
18. Average Variable Cost

📉 Average Variable Cost (AVC)

Definition 1:
Average Variable Cost is variable cost per unit of output.
Definition 2:
AVC represents the variable cost incurred for producing each unit of output on average.
AVC = TVC ÷ Q
19. Average Cost

📊 Average Cost (AC)

Definition 1:
Average Cost is total cost per unit of output.
Definition 2:
AC represents the average expenditure incurred in producing each unit of output.
AC = TC ÷ Q
Since TC = TFC + TVC:

AC = AFC + AVC
20. Marginal Cost

➕ Marginal Cost (MC)

Definition 1:
Marginal Cost is the change in Total Cost resulting from producing one additional unit of output.
Definition 2:
MC is the additional cost incurred due to an additional unit of output.
MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ
Since fixed cost does not change with output:

MC = ΔTVC ÷ ΔQ
21. AC and MC

🔗 Relationship Between AC and MC

When MC < AC:
AC falls.
When MC > AC:
AC rises.
When MC = AC:
AC is minimum.
Easy example:
If your average marks are 70 and you score 80 in the next test, your average rises. If you score 60, your average falls. The same logic applies to MC and AC.
22. Cost Relationships

🔗 Relationship Among TC, TFC and TVC

TC = TFC + TVC
Output TFC TVC TC
0 100 0 100
1 100 50 150
2 100 90 190
3 100 120 220
4 100 160 260
Notice that TFC remains constant while TVC changes with output. Therefore TC changes because of changes in TVC.
23. Important Cost Formulas

🧮 Cost Formula Sheet

Concept Formula
Total Cost TC = TFC + TVC
Average Fixed Cost AFC = TFC ÷ Q
Average Variable Cost AVC = TVC ÷ Q
Average Cost AC = TC ÷ Q
Average Cost AC = AFC + AVC
Marginal Cost MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ
Marginal Cost MC = ΔTVC ÷ ΔQ
24. Short-Run Cost Curves

📉 Short-Run Cost Curves

Fixed Cost Curve

TFC remains constant at all levels of output. Therefore, its curve is horizontal.

Variable Cost Curve

TVC generally increases with output.

Total Cost Curve

TC increases as output increases because TVC increases. The vertical distance between TC and TVC remains equal to TFC.

AFC Curve

AFC continuously falls as output increases.

AVC and AC Curves

AVC and AC are generally U-shaped in the short run because of the operation of the Law of Variable Proportions.

MC Curve

MC is generally U-shaped and intersects AVC and AC at their respective minimum points.

25. Numerical Example

🧮 Simple Cost Calculation

Suppose:

TFC = ₹100
TVC = ₹200
Output = 10 units

TC = TFC + TVC
= ₹100 + ₹200
= ₹300

AFC = TFC ÷ Q
= ₹100 ÷ 10
= ₹10

AVC = TVC ÷ Q
= ₹200 ÷ 10
= ₹20

AC = TC ÷ Q
= ₹300 ÷ 10
= ₹30
Check:
AC = AFC + AVC
₹30 = ₹10 + ₹20 ✔️
