⚖️ Right to Equality — Overview

The Right to Equality is guaranteed under Articles 14 to 18 of the Constitution. It aims to ensure equality before law, equal opportunity and protection against certain forms of discrimination and social inequality.

Articles: 14–18
Theme: Equality before law | Non-discrimination | Equal opportunity | Social equality

📜 Article 14 — Equality Before Law

Article 14 guarantees equality before the law and equal protection of the laws to every person within the territory of India.

Concept 1

Equality Before Law

No person is above the law. Every person is subject to the ordinary law of the land.

Concept 2

Equal Protection of Laws

Persons who are similarly situated should receive similar treatment. Different treatment can be permitted when there is a valid constitutional classification.

Reasonable Classification:
Article 14 does not require identical treatment in every situation. A classification may be valid when it is based on an intelligible differentia and that differentia has a rational relation to the object sought to be achieved.
Important: Article 14 applies to all persons, not merely citizens.

🚫 Article 15 — Prohibition of Discrimination

Article 15 protects citizens against discrimination by the State on specified grounds and permits certain forms of affirmative action.

Article 15(1):
The State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth or any of them.
Article 15(2):
No citizen shall, on the above grounds only, be subjected to any disability, liability, restriction or condition regarding access to:

(a) shops, public restaurants, hotels and places of public entertainment; or

(b) wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads and places of public resort maintained wholly or partly out of State funds or dedicated to the use of the general public.
Article 15(3):
Nothing in Article 15 prevents the State from making special provisions for women and children.
Article 15(4):
The State may make special provisions for the advancement of socially and educationally backward classes and for the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
Article 15(5):
The State may make special provisions for the advancement of socially and educationally backward classes or SCs/STs in relation to their admission to educational institutions, including private educational institutions, whether aided or unaided by the State.

Exception: Minority educational institutions referred to in Article 30(1) are excluded.
Article 15(6):
The State may make special provisions for the advancement of Economically Weaker Sections (EWS), including special provisions relating to admission to educational institutions.

Such provisions may include reservation, subject to the constitutional limit specified in the clause.
Important Amendments:
15(4) → First Constitutional Amendment, 1951
15(5) → 93rd Constitutional Amendment, 2005
15(6) → 103rd Constitutional Amendment, 2019

🏛️ Article 16 — Equality of Opportunity in Public Employment

Article 16 guarantees equality of opportunity for citizens in matters of employment or appointment to offices under the State.

Article 16(1):
There shall be equality of opportunity for all citizens in matters relating to employment or appointment to any office under the State.
Article 16(2):
No citizen shall, on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth, residence or any of them, be ineligible for or discriminated against in respect of employment or office under the State.
Article 16(3):
Parliament may make a law prescribing, in regard to a class or classes of employment or appointment under a State or Union Territory, a requirement as to residence within that State or Union Territory.
Article 16(4):
The State may make provisions for the reservation of appointments or posts in favour of any backward class of citizens which, in the opinion of the State, is not adequately represented in the services under the State.
Article 16(4A):
The State may make provision for reservation in matters of promotion, with consequential seniority, for SCs and STs where they are not adequately represented in services under the State.
Article 16(4B):
Unfilled reserved vacancies of a year may be treated as a separate class of vacancies and may be carried forward to subsequent years. They are considered separately for determining the ceiling on reservation for that year.
Article 16(5):
A law may require that the holder of an office connected with the affairs of a religious or denominational institution, or a member of its governing body, belong to the particular religion or denomination.
Article 16(6):
The State may make provision for reservation of appointments or posts in favour of EWS, in addition to existing reservation and subject to the constitutional limit specified in the clause.
Important:
16(4A) → 77th Constitutional Amendment, 1995
16(4B) → 81st Constitutional Amendment, 2000
16(6) → 103rd Constitutional Amendment, 2019

🚫 Article 17 — Abolition of Untouchability

Article 17 abolishes untouchability and forbids its practice in any form.

Constitutional Rule:
The practice of untouchability is prohibited, and enforcement of any disability arising from it is an offence punishable according to law.
Important Law

Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955

The Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 was enacted to prescribe punishment for the practice of untouchability and related disabilities.

🏅 Article 18 — Abolition of Titles

Article 18(1):
No title, except a military or academic distinction, shall be conferred by the State.
Article 18(2):
No citizen of India shall accept any title from any foreign State.
Article 18(3):
No person who is not a citizen of India shall, while holding any office of profit or trust under the State, accept any title from any foreign State without the consent of the President.
Article 18(4):
No person holding any office of profit or trust under the State shall, without the consent of the President, accept any present, emolument or office of any kind from or under a foreign State.
Important:
Military and academic distinctions are permitted. National awards such as Bharat Ratna and Padma Awards are not treated as "titles" under Article 18 when they are not used as prefixes or suffixes to names.

⚖️ Important Supreme Court Judgments

1. State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar (1952)

Article: 14

Judgment: The Supreme Court examined the validity of differential treatment under a special criminal procedure law and emphasised that classification must satisfy the requirements of Article 14.

Exam Point: Article 14 permits reasonable classification but prohibits arbitrary discrimination.

2. E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu (1974)

Article: 14

Judgment: The Supreme Court expanded the understanding of equality and held that arbitrariness is contrary to equality. Arbitrary State action can therefore violate Article 14.

Exam Point: Article 14 is not merely about formal classification; it also protects against arbitrary State action.

3. Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992)

Article: 16(4)

Judgment: The Supreme Court upheld reservation for Other Backward Classes subject to constitutional principles and recognised the creamy layer principle for OBC reservation. It also held that reservation under Article 16(4) is generally subject to a 50% ceiling, subject to exceptional circumstances.

Exam Point: OBC reservation + creamy layer + general 50% ceiling principle.

4. M. Nagaraj v. Union of India (2006)

Article: 16(4A) and reservation in promotion

Judgment: The Supreme Court upheld the constitutional amendments enabling reservation in promotion, but held that the State must satisfy constitutional conditions before exercising this enabling power, including demonstrating relevant inadequacy of representation and considering administrative efficiency.

Exam Point: Article 16(4A) is an enabling provision; it does not create an automatic fundamental right to reservation in promotion.

5. Ashoka Kumar Thakur v. Union of India (2008)

Article: 15(5)

Judgment: The Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of Article 15(5) and the related reservation framework for socially and educationally backward classes in educational institutions, while excluding minority institutions protected under Article 30(1).

Exam Point: Article 15(5) permits affirmative action in educational admissions, including private non-minority institutions.

6. Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022)

Article: 15(6) and 16(6)

Judgment: The Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of the 103rd Constitutional Amendment, which enabled EWS reservation under Articles 15(6) and 16(6).

Exam Point: EWS reservation under the 103rd Amendment was upheld by the Supreme Court.

🎯 Quick Revision

Article 14: Equality before law + Equal protection of laws
Article 15: Prohibition of discrimination + Special provisions
Article 16: Equality of opportunity in public employment
Article 17: Abolition of untouchability
Article 18: Abolition of titles
Important Cases: Anwar Ali Sarkar → E.P. Royappa → Indra Sawhney → M. Nagaraj → Ashoka Kumar Thakur → Janhit Abhiyan
