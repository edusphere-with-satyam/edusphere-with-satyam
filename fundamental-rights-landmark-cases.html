⚖️ Landmark Supreme Court Judgments

Landmark judgments have played an important role in explaining the meaning and scope of Fundamental Rights. These cases help us understand how the Supreme Court has interpreted the Constitution.

Study Format:
Main Issue → Supreme Court's Judgment → Importance → Exam Point

🟦 Article 14 — Right to Equality

Article 14

1. State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar (1952)

🔹 Main Issue:
The West Bengal Special Courts Act allowed the government to select certain cases for trial by Special Courts. The question was whether this arbitrary selection violated Article 14.
🔹 Supreme Court's Judgment:
The Supreme Court struck down the relevant provision because it gave excessive discretion to the government without adequate guidelines. The classification was held to be inconsistent with the guarantee of equality under Article 14.

Why it matters: It is an important early case on equality and reasonable classification.

🎯 Exam Point:
Article 14 does not permit arbitrary classification.
Article 14

2. Ram Krishna Dalmia v. Justice S.R. Tendolkar (1958)

🔹 Main Issue:
The case challenged a classification made under a law and raised the question of when different treatment of different groups is valid under Article 14.
🔹 Supreme Court's Judgment:
The Supreme Court explained the test of reasonable classification. A valid classification must have:

1. An intelligible differentia separating the group from others; and
2. A rational connection between the differentia and the objective of the law.

Why it matters: It became a leading case for understanding reasonable classification under Article 14.

🎯 Exam Point:
Intelligible Differentia + Rational Nexus = Valid Classification
Article 14

3. E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu (1974)

🔹 Main Issue:
The case concerned the transfer and appointment of a senior government officer and raised questions about arbitrary state action under Article 14.
🔹 Supreme Court's Judgment:
The Supreme Court held that equality is not merely a matter of formal classification. **Arbitrariness is opposed to equality**. An arbitrary state action can therefore violate Article 14.

Why it matters: It expanded the understanding of Article 14 beyond the traditional classification test.

🎯 Exam Point:
Article 14 → Equality and protection against arbitrary State action.
Article 14

4. Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)

🔹 Main Issue:
Maneka Gandhi's passport was impounded by the government. The case raised questions concerning personal liberty and the relationship between Articles 14, 19 and 21.
🔹 Supreme Court's Judgment:
The Supreme Court held that the procedure affecting personal liberty must be **fair, just and reasonable**. It also emphasised that Articles 14, 19 and 21 are closely connected.

Why it matters: It greatly expanded the protection of personal liberty under Article 21.

🎯 Exam Point:
Articles 14 + 19 + 21 → Interconnected Fundamental Rights.
Article 14

5. Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib Sehravardi (1981)

🔹 Main Issue:
The case concerned admission to an engineering college and whether discriminatory admission criteria could violate Article 14.
🔹 Supreme Court's Judgment:
The Court held that **arbitrariness in State action is inconsistent with equality**. The Court also examined whether an institution could fall within the meaning of "State" under Article 12.

Why it matters: Important for understanding Article 14 and the meaning of "State" under Article 12.

🎯 Exam Point:
Arbitrary State action → Violation of Article 14.

🎯 Article 14 — Quick Revision

Anwar Ali Sarkar → Arbitrary classification
Ram Krishna Dalmia → Reasonable Classification
E.P. Royappa → Arbitrariness = inequality
Maneka Gandhi → Articles 14, 19 & 21 interconnected
Ajay Hasia → Article 14 + Meaning of State
