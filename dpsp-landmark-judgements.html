📘 Why are these Judgements Important?

The Directive Principles of State Policy guide the State in making laws and policies for social and economic welfare. Although DPSPs are not directly enforceable by courts, the Supreme Court has frequently used them while interpreting Fundamental Rights and other constitutional provisions.

These cases show how DPSPs have influenced the meaning of equality, livelihood, dignity, education, labour welfare and environmental protection.

Important: The cases below are different from the major Fundamental Rights–DPSP conflict cases discussed separately on the DPSP & Fundamental Rights Relationship page.
Case 1

⚖️ Randhir Singh v. Union of India (1982)

🔴 Main Issue

Whether employees performing substantially similar work could claim equal pay when they were placed in different pay structures.

📜 Relevant Constitutional Provision

Article 39(d) directs the State to secure equal pay for equal work for both men and women.

🏛️ Supreme Court Judgement

The Supreme Court recognised equal pay for equal work as an important constitutional goal. It held that the principle, although contained in the DPSPs, could be read along with the Fundamental Rights relating to equality.

✅ Result / Impact

The judgement strengthened the principle of equal pay and showed that DPSPs can help courts interpret and enforce constitutional equality in appropriate circumstances.

Case 2

⚖️ Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India (1984)

🔴 Main Issue

The case concerned the exploitation and poor working conditions of bonded labourers. The question was whether the State had a constitutional duty to protect workers from such conditions.

📜 Relevant Constitutional Provisions

Article 21 protects life and personal liberty.

DPSPs such as Articles 39(e), 39(f) and 42 direct the State to protect workers and children and secure just and humane conditions of work.

🏛️ Supreme Court Judgement

The Supreme Court gave a broad interpretation to Article 21 and held that the right to live with human dignity includes protection against conditions that deprive people of basic human dignity.

The Court used the DPSPs as important constitutional guidance while understanding the scope of Fundamental Rights.

✅ Result / Impact

The judgement strengthened constitutional protection for bonded labourers and reinforced the idea that Fundamental Rights and DPSPs should work together to promote human dignity.

Case 3

⚖️ Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation (1985)

🔴 Main Issue

The case concerned the eviction of pavement and slum dwellers in Mumbai. The important constitutional question was whether the right to livelihood forms part of the right to life.

📜 Relevant Constitutional Provisions

Article 21 protects the right to life and personal liberty.

Article 39(a) directs the State to secure an adequate means of livelihood for citizens.

🏛️ Supreme Court Judgement

The Supreme Court held that the right to livelihood is an important part of the right to life under Article 21 because a person cannot meaningfully live without the means of livelihood.

✅ Result / Impact

The judgement expanded the scope of Article 21 and demonstrated how the DPSP objective of adequate livelihood under Article 39(a) could support the interpretation of a Fundamental Right.

Case 4

⚖️ Unni Krishnan, J.P. v. State of Andhra Pradesh (1993)

🔴 Main Issue

The case involved the right to education and the regulation of private educational institutions. The major constitutional question was whether the right to education could be derived from Fundamental Rights.

📜 Relevant Constitutional Provisions

Article 21 protects life and personal liberty.

Article 41 directs the State to make effective provision for education within its economic capacity and development.

Article 45, as it then stood, directed the State to endeavour to provide free and compulsory education for children up to fourteen years of age.

🏛️ Supreme Court Judgement

The Supreme Court held that the right to education is implicit in Article 21, particularly for children up to the age of fourteen years, subject to the constitutional framework existing at that time.

✅ Result / Impact

The judgement became an important step toward constitutional recognition of education as a Fundamental Right.

Later, the 86th Constitutional Amendment, 2002 inserted Article 21A, making free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14 a Fundamental Right.

Case 5

🌳 M.C. Mehta v. Union of India

🔴 Main Issue

The Supreme Court dealt with various environmental matters and the responsibility of the State and industries to prevent environmental harm.

📜 Relevant Constitutional Provisions

Article 48A directs the State to protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife.

Article 21 has also been interpreted by the Supreme Court to include important aspects of a healthy environment.

🏛️ Supreme Court Judgement

Through its environmental jurisprudence in the M.C. Mehta cases, the Supreme Court developed strong constitutional protection for the environment and applied principles such as the precautionary principle and polluter pays principle in appropriate cases.

✅ Result / Impact

These decisions significantly strengthened environmental protection in India and showed the importance of reading Article 21 together with Article 48A and other constitutional environmental duties.

Case 6

⚖️ State of Kerala v. N.M. Thomas (1976)

🔴 Main Issue

The case concerned reservation and special measures for disadvantaged sections of society and the meaning of equality under the Constitution.

📜 Relevant Constitutional Provisions

Article 16 guarantees equality of opportunity in public employment.

Article 46 directs the State to promote the educational and economic interests of weaker sections and protect them from social injustice and exploitation.

🏛️ Supreme Court Judgement

The Supreme Court adopted a broader understanding of equality and recognised that genuine equality may require special measures for disadvantaged groups.

✅ Result / Impact

The judgement became an important decision in the development of the idea of substantive equality and demonstrated the relevance of Article 46 while understanding constitutional equality.

Case 7

🏠 Property Owners Association v. State of Maharashtra (2024)

🔴 Main Issue

The Supreme Court considered the meaning of “material resources of the community” under Article 39(b) and whether every privately owned resource can be treated as a material resource of the community.

📜 Relevant Constitutional Provision

Article 39(b) directs that ownership and control of the material resources of the community should be distributed so as best to serve the common good.

🏛️ Supreme Court Judgement

A nine-judge Constitution Bench examined the scope of Article 39(b). The Court rejected an overly broad interpretation under which every privately owned resource would automatically become a “material resource of the community”.

The Court emphasised that whether a resource falls within Article 39(b) depends on its nature and the constitutional objective of serving the common good.

✅ Result / Impact

The judgement is an important modern interpretation of Article 39(b) and clarifies the constitutional limits of treating privately owned resources as community resources.

🌟 What These Cases Show

These judgements demonstrate that DPSPs are not merely political instructions. Although they are not directly enforceable by courts, they have played an important role in the development of constitutional law.

Fundamental Rights + DPSPs = Constitutional Welfare & Justice

The Supreme Court has often used DPSPs to give broader meaning to Fundamental Rights, particularly in areas such as livelihood, education, labour welfare, equality and environmental protection.

📌 Key Understanding:

DPSPs guide the State in making laws and policies, while courts can also use their values as an aid in interpreting constitutional rights and provisions.
