Meaning of Income:

Income refers to the flow of earnings received by individuals, firms or the economy during a particular period from economic activities.
Meaning of Employment:

Employment refers to the engagement of people in productive economic activities in return for income or remuneration.
Macroeconomic Focus:

In this chapter, we mainly study the determination of the level of income and employment in an economy and the factors that influence Aggregate Demand and Aggregate Supply.
1. AGGREGATE DEMAND

📈 Aggregate Demand (AD)

Definition:

Aggregate Demand refers to the total planned expenditure on final goods and services in an economy during a given period.
In a simple two-sector economy:

Aggregate Demand = Consumption + Investment

AD = C + I

Components of Aggregate Demand

  • Consumption Expenditure (C)
  • Investment Expenditure (I)
Example:

Suppose households plan to spend ₹800 crore on consumption and firms plan to invest ₹200 crore.

AD = C + I

AD = ₹800 crore + ₹200 crore

AD = ₹1,000 crore
PYQ / Exam Point ⭐

For a two-sector economy:
AD = C + I
2. AGGREGATE SUPPLY

📊 Aggregate Supply (AS)

Definition:

Aggregate Supply refers to the total value of goods and services that firms plan to produce during a given period.
In the simple Keynesian model, Aggregate Supply is represented by the level of income.

AS = Y
Example:

If the total income generated in an economy is ₹5,000 crore, Aggregate Supply in the simple model is represented by ₹5,000 crore.
Remember:

In the simple income determination model:
AD = C + I
AS = Y
3. EX-ANTE & EX-POST

⏳ Ex-Ante and Ex-Post Concepts

Ex-Ante:

Ex-ante refers to planned or intended values of economic variables before the actual economic activity takes place.
Ex-Post:

Ex-post refers to actual or realised values after the economic activity has taken place.
Basis Ex-Ante Ex-Post
Meaning Planned / intended Actual / realised
Time Before activity After activity
Example Planned investment Actual investment
PYQ Tip ⭐

Ex-ante = Planned
Ex-post = Actual
4. INVENTORY

📦 Planned and Unplanned Inventory Changes

Inventory:

Inventory refers to stocks of goods held by firms, including finished goods, semi-finished goods and raw materials.

Planned Inventory Change

A planned inventory change is the change in stock that firms intentionally decide to maintain as part of their business plans.

Example:

A shopkeeper intentionally keeps 100 extra notebooks before the school session begins because he expects higher demand.

Unplanned Inventory Change

An unplanned inventory change occurs when actual sales differ from what firms had expected.

Example:

A firm expected to sell 1,000 units but actually sold only 800 units. The unsold 200 units become an unplanned increase in inventory.
Important:

At the equilibrium level of income, there is no unintended change in inventories.

Therefore, planned expenditure becomes equal to actual output.
5. CONSUMPTION

🛒 Consumption Expenditure

Definition:

Consumption expenditure is the expenditure made by households on goods and services for the satisfaction of their wants.

Consumption Function

The consumption function shows the functional relationship between consumption expenditure and income.

C = C̄ + cY

C = Consumption expenditure
C̄ = Autonomous consumption
c = Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC)
Y = Income
Autonomous Consumption:

Autonomous consumption is the level of consumption that does not depend on current income.
Example:

Even when current income is zero, a household may still have some essential expenditure by using past savings or borrowing.
Key Point ⭐

Autonomous consumption is generally represented by C̄ in the consumption function.
6. SAVING

💰 Saving

Definition:

Saving is that part of income which is not spent on consumption.
S = Y − C

Saving = Income − Consumption
Basic Relationship:

Y = C + S
If income = ₹10,000 and consumption = ₹8,000:

S = Y − C
S = ₹10,000 − ₹8,000

S = ₹2,000
7. SAVING FUNCTION

📉 Saving Function

Saving function shows the functional relationship between saving and income.

If:
C = C̄ + cY

Then:
S = −C̄ + (1 − c)Y
Important Relationship:

MPS = 1 − MPC

MPC + MPS = 1
PYQ / Board Point ⭐

If MPC = 0.8:

MPS = 1 − 0.8
MPS = 0.2
8. INVESTMENT

🏭 Investment Expenditure

Definition:

Investment refers to expenditure incurred on the creation or acquisition of capital goods that increase productive capacity.

Autonomous Investment

Definition:

Autonomous investment is investment which is independent of the level of income.
Example:

Government expenditure on infrastructure may be undertaken even when current income changes.
In the simple Keynesian model, investment is often assumed to be autonomous.

9. MPC

📐 Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC)

Definition:

Marginal Propensity to Consume is the ratio of change in consumption expenditure to change in income.
MPC = ΔC ÷ ΔY
Income increases from ₹1,000 to ₹1,500.

Change in income = ₹500

Consumption increases from ₹800 to ₹1,200.

Change in consumption = ₹400

MPC = ₹400 ÷ ₹500

MPC = 0.8
Range of MPC:

In the basic Keynesian framework:
0 < MPC < 1
10. MPS

💹 Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS)

Definition:

Marginal Propensity to Save is the ratio of change in saving to change in income.
MPS = ΔS ÷ ΔY
Relationship:

MPC + MPS = 1

Therefore:
MPS = 1 − MPC
Exam Trick ⭐

If MPC = 0.75:

MPS = 1 − 0.75
MPS = 0.25
