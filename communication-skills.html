Communication Skills

Class 9 Information Technology (402) • Employability Skills

1. Introduction to Communication

Communication is the process of sharing information, ideas, thoughts, feelings and messages between two or more people.

Communication takes place when a sender sends a message to a receiver through a suitable medium and the receiver understands the message.

Example:

A teacher explains a topic to students.
Teacher → Sender
Explanation → Message
Speaking → Medium
Students → Receivers
Students' response → Feedback

Communication is an essential part of our personal, social, educational and professional life.

2. Importance of Communication

Communication helps us to:

  • Share information
  • Express thoughts and feelings
  • Build relationships
  • Understand other people
  • Solve problems
  • Make decisions
  • Give and receive instructions
  • Work effectively in a team
  • Avoid misunderstandings
  • Develop confidence
  • Improve professional relationships

Communication in the Workplace

Good communication helps employees to:

  • Understand their responsibilities
  • Follow instructions correctly
  • Coordinate with colleagues
  • Communicate with customers
  • Report problems
  • Work as a team
  • Complete tasks efficiently

3. Elements of Communication

The major elements of communication are:

  1. Sender
  2. Message
  3. Encoding
  4. Communication Channel / Medium
  5. Receiver
  6. Decoding
  7. Feedback
  8. Noise / Barrier
  9. Context
Communication Process:

Sender → Encoding → Message → Channel → Receiver → Decoding → Feedback

Noise or barriers can affect any stage of the communication process.

3.1 Sender

The sender is the person who starts the communication. The sender has an idea, thought or information that needs to be communicated.

3.2 Message

The message is the information, idea, thought or feeling that the sender wants to communicate.

3.3 Encoding

Encoding means converting an idea or thought into a form that can be communicated.

Ideas may be converted into:

  • Words
  • Symbols
  • Gestures
  • Pictures
  • Written text

3.4 Communication Channel / Medium

The channel is the method used to send the message.

  • Face-to-face conversation
  • Telephone
  • Email
  • Letter
  • SMS
  • Video call
  • Social media
  • Notice
  • Presentation

3.5 Receiver

The receiver is the person or group for whom the message is intended.

3.6 Decoding

Decoding means interpreting and understanding the message received.

3.7 Feedback

Feedback is the response given by the receiver after receiving and understanding the message.

3.8 Noise

Noise means anything that interferes with communication.

  • Physical noise
  • Psychological disturbance
  • Technical problems
  • Language problems

3.9 Context

Context means the situation, environment or circumstances in which communication takes place.

4. Types of Communication

Communication can broadly be divided into:

  1. Verbal Communication
  2. Non-Verbal Communication
  3. Visual Communication

5. Verbal Communication

Verbal communication uses words to communicate information. It can be oral or written.

5.1 Oral Communication

Oral communication uses spoken words.

Examples:

  • Conversation
  • Telephone call
  • Speech
  • Interview
  • Classroom discussion
  • Meeting
  • Presentation

Advantages

  • Fast
  • Allows immediate feedback
  • Can clarify doubts quickly
  • More personal
  • Useful in discussions

Limitations

  • No permanent record unless recorded
  • Misunderstanding can occur
  • Not always suitable for formal information
  • Language or pronunciation problems may create confusion

6. Written Communication

Written communication uses written words.

Examples:

  • Letters
  • Emails
  • Notices
  • Reports
  • Messages
  • Applications
  • Forms
  • Memos

Advantages

  • Provides a record
  • Can be reviewed later
  • Useful for formal communication
  • Can communicate detailed information

Limitations

  • Feedback may be delayed
  • Requires writing skills
  • Misunderstanding may occur if the message is unclear
  • Preparation may take more time

7. Non-Verbal Communication

Non-verbal communication means communicating without using spoken or written words.

It includes:

  • Facial expressions
  • Gestures
  • Posture
  • Eye contact
  • Body movements
  • Appearance
  • Tone and manner of voice

7.1 Facial Expressions

Facial expressions communicate emotions.

  • Smile → happiness or friendliness
  • Frown → displeasure or concern
  • Raised eyebrows → surprise
  • Serious expression → seriousness

7.2 Gestures

Gestures are movements of hands, arms or other body parts used to communicate.

  • Waving → greeting
  • Nodding → agreement
  • Shaking head → disagreement
  • Thumbs-up → approval

7.3 Eye Contact

Appropriate eye contact can show attention, confidence, interest and respect.

7.4 Posture

Posture means the way a person sits, stands or holds their body.

7.5 Tone of Voice

Tone influences how a spoken message is understood. The same words may communicate different meanings depending on tone and context.

8. Visual Communication

Visual communication uses images, symbols, signs, charts, diagrams and other visual elements to communicate information.

Examples:

  • Traffic signs
  • Maps
  • Graphs
  • Charts
  • Diagrams
  • Posters
  • Symbols
  • Infographics
  • Icons

Advantages

  • Easy to understand
  • Attractive
  • Useful for complex information
  • Can communicate quickly
  • Helpful when language is a barrier

9. Formal and Informal Communication

Formal Communication

Formal communication follows an official or organizational structure.

Examples:

  • Official email
  • Notice
  • Report
  • Business letter
  • Meeting
  • Official application

Informal Communication

Informal communication is casual and generally does not follow an official structure.

Examples:

  • Conversation with friends
  • Casual messages
  • Conversation with family members
Formal Communication Informal Communication
Official and structured Casual and flexible
Used in professional situations Used in personal situations
Usually follows organizational rules Usually does not follow formal rules

10. Methods of Communication

Face-to-Face Communication

People communicate directly with each other.

Telephone Communication

Information is exchanged through a telephone call.

Email Communication

Messages are exchanged electronically.

Video Conferencing

People communicate through audio and video over the internet.

Written Documents

Information is communicated through letters, notices, reports and other documents.

11. Communication Barriers

A communication barrier is anything that prevents a message from being communicated or understood properly.

Major barriers include:

  1. Physical barriers
  2. Language barriers
  3. Psychological barriers
  4. Cultural barriers
  5. Semantic barriers
  6. Technological barriers
  7. Organizational barriers

12. Physical Barriers

Physical barriers are environmental conditions that interfere with communication.

Examples:

  • Noise
  • Distance
  • Poor lighting
  • Uncomfortable environment
  • Faulty equipment

Solutions

  • Choose a suitable environment.
  • Reduce background noise.
  • Use proper equipment.
  • Move closer when necessary.

13. Language Barriers

Language barriers occur when people do not understand the language, vocabulary or terminology being used.

Examples:

  • Different languages
  • Difficult vocabulary
  • Technical terms
  • Poor pronunciation

Solutions

  • Use simple language.
  • Explain difficult terms.
  • Avoid unnecessary technical words.
  • Speak clearly.
  • Confirm understanding.

14. Psychological Barriers

Psychological barriers are related to a person's mental or emotional state.

Examples:

  • Fear
  • Stress
  • Lack of confidence
  • Anger
  • Anxiety
  • Lack of concentration
  • Prejudice

Solutions

  • Stay calm.
  • Listen carefully.
  • Build confidence.
  • Avoid reacting emotionally.
  • Create a supportive environment.

15. Cultural Barriers

Different cultures may have different languages, customs, values, gestures, social practices and communication styles.

A gesture that is acceptable in one culture may have a different meaning in another culture.

Solutions

  • Respect cultural differences.
  • Avoid assumptions.
  • Learn about different cultures.
  • Communicate respectfully.

16. Semantic Barriers

Semantic barriers occur because of differences in the meaning or interpretation of words.

Examples:

  • Words with multiple meanings
  • Difficult terminology
  • Ambiguous statements
  • Incorrect use of words

Solution

Use clear, simple and precise language.

17. Technological Barriers

Technology can sometimes interfere with communication.

Examples:

  • Poor internet connection
  • Microphone failure
  • Low battery
  • Software problems
  • Poor audio or video quality

Solutions

  • Check equipment before communication.
  • Maintain a reliable internet connection.
  • Keep devices charged.
  • Have an alternative communication method.

18. Organizational Barriers

Organizational barriers occur because of problems in the structure, rules or communication system of an organization.

Examples:

  • Long communication chains
  • Unclear responsibilities
  • Lack of proper communication channels
  • Information overload

Solutions

  • Keep communication channels clear.
  • Define responsibilities properly.
  • Share relevant information.
  • Encourage feedback.

19. How to Overcome Communication Barriers

  • Use simple language.
  • Listen carefully.
  • Speak clearly.
  • Avoid unnecessary jargon.
  • Select the correct medium.
  • Give feedback.
  • Ask for clarification.
  • Maintain appropriate eye contact.
  • Be patient.
  • Respect cultural differences.
  • Control emotions.
  • Reduce distractions.

20. Listening Skills

Listening is an important part of communication.

Hearing vs Listening:

Hearing means receiving sound.
Listening means actively paying attention and trying to understand the message.

Therefore, hearing is mainly a physical process, while listening is an active process.

21. Importance of Listening

  • Understand information
  • Avoid misunderstandings
  • Learn better
  • Build relationships
  • Solve problems
  • Respond appropriately
  • Improve teamwork

22. Types of Listening

Active Listening

Actively paying attention to the speaker and trying to understand the complete message.

Passive Listening

Hearing the speaker without actively engaging with or processing the message.

Empathetic Listening

Listening while trying to understand the speaker's feelings and point of view.

Critical Listening

Listening carefully and evaluating the information before accepting it.

23. Active Listening Skills

A good listener should:

  • Pay attention.
  • Avoid unnecessary interruptions.
  • Maintain appropriate eye contact.
  • Observe non-verbal cues.
  • Ask relevant questions.
  • Give feedback.
  • Paraphrase or summarize when needed.
  • Keep an open mind.

24. The 7 Cs of Effective Communication

Effective communication should follow the 7 Cs:

7 C Meaning
Clear The message should be easy to understand.
Concise The message should avoid unnecessary words.
Concrete The message should be specific and supported by relevant facts.
Correct The information and language should be accurate.
Coherent The ideas should be logically connected.
Complete The message should contain all necessary information.
Courteous The message should be polite and respectful.

25. Effective Speaking

Effective speaking means expressing ideas clearly and confidently.

A good speaker should:

  • Speak clearly.
  • Use suitable vocabulary.
  • Maintain a suitable pace.
  • Use suitable tone.
  • Organize ideas.
  • Listen to questions.
  • Respond appropriately.
  • Avoid unnecessary repetition.

26. Effective Writing

Good writing should be:

  • Clear
  • Correct
  • Concise
  • Complete
  • Organized
  • Appropriate for the reader
Before writing, identify:

Who is the reader?
What is the purpose?
What information is necessary?

27. Communication in Different Situations

Classroom

Use clear and respectful language.

Workplace

Use professional and polite communication.

Interview

Be confident, clear and respectful.

Customer Service

Listen carefully and respond politely.

Email

Use a suitable subject, greeting, clear message and appropriate closing.

28. Feedback

Feedback is the response provided after receiving a message.

Good Feedback Should Be:

  • Clear
  • Specific
  • Respectful
  • Useful
  • Timely

Importance of Feedback

  • Confirms understanding
  • Corrects mistakes
  • Improves performance
  • Solves misunderstandings
  • Improves future communication

29. Communication Etiquette

Communication etiquette means following appropriate manners while communicating.

  • Be polite.
  • Listen when others are speaking.
  • Do not interrupt unnecessarily.
  • Use respectful language.
  • Be clear and honest.
  • Respect other people's opinions.
  • Avoid offensive language.
  • Maintain an appropriate tone.
  • Respond appropriately.

30. Telephone Etiquette

  1. Greet the person.
  2. Introduce yourself when necessary.
  3. Speak clearly.
  4. Listen carefully.
  5. Avoid unnecessary background noise.
  6. Be polite.
  7. Take notes when necessary.
  8. End the conversation politely.

31. Email Etiquette

  • Use a clear subject line.
  • Use an appropriate greeting.
  • Keep the message clear and concise.
  • Check spelling and grammar.
  • Use professional language.
  • Avoid unnecessary capital letters.
  • Check attachments before sending.
  • Use an appropriate closing.

32. Digital Communication

Digital communication uses electronic devices and digital networks to exchange information.

Examples:

  • Email
  • Messaging applications
  • Video conferencing
  • Online learning platforms
  • Social media
  • Websites

Advantages

  • Fast
  • Convenient
  • Global reach
  • Easy to store
  • Easy to share

Risks

  • Misunderstanding
  • Privacy issues
  • Cybersecurity risks
  • Misinformation
  • Digital distraction

33. Netiquette

Netiquette means appropriate and respectful behaviour while communicating online.

  • Be respectful.
  • Do not use abusive language.
  • Do not share private information unnecessarily.
  • Think before posting.
  • Avoid spreading unverified information.
  • Respect other people's opinions.
  • Use appropriate language.
  • Do not type everything in CAPITAL LETTERS unnecessarily.

34. Communication and Technology

Technology has changed the way people communicate.

Today we can communicate through:

  • Email
  • Smartphones
  • Video calls
  • Online meetings
  • Learning platforms
  • Social networks

Technology makes communication faster and easier, but users should understand its limitations and use it responsibly.

38. Practical Activities

Activity 1 — Identify Communication Elements

A teacher says: "Please submit your project by Friday."

  • Sender: Teacher
  • Message: Submit the project by Friday
  • Medium: Spoken communication
  • Receiver: Students
  • Feedback: Students' response

Activity 2 — Identify the Type

  1. Teacher gives a speech → Oral communication
  2. Student writes an application → Written communication
  3. Person waves → Non-verbal communication
  4. Traffic sign → Visual communication
  5. Video conference → Digital communication

Activity 3 — Identify the Barrier

A student is unable to understand a teacher because the teacher is using unfamiliar technical terms.

Barrier: Language / Semantic barrier

Solution: Use simpler words and explain the technical terms.

Activity 4 — Listening Activity

Work in pairs. One student speaks for one minute about a favourite topic while the other student listens without interrupting.

After listening, the second student should summarize the main points.

Learning Outcome: Develop active listening and summarizing skills.

Activity 5 — Non-Verbal Communication

One student communicates a simple emotion or action only through facial expressions or gestures. Other students try to identify the message.

Learning Outcome: Understand the importance of non-verbal communication.

Activity 6 — Email Communication

Write a short formal email to a teacher requesting information about an assignment.

Focus: Subject line, greeting, clear message, polite language and closing.

Activity 7 — Netiquette

Identify five good and five bad online communication practices.

Learning Outcome: Develop responsible digital communication habits.

39. Important Definitions

Communication: The process of exchanging information, ideas, thoughts and feelings.

Sender: The person who initiates communication.

Receiver: The person who receives the message.

Message: The information being communicated.

Encoding: Converting an idea into a communicable form.

Decoding: Interpreting the received message.

Feedback: The receiver's response to the sender.

Noise: Anything that interferes with communication.

Verbal Communication: Communication using words.

Non-Verbal Communication: Communication through gestures, facial expressions, posture and other cues.

Visual Communication: Communication using images, symbols, charts and other visual elements.

Active Listening: Listening carefully and consciously to understand the speaker.

Netiquette: Rules of appropriate and respectful behaviour while communicating online.
