The chapter Determination of Income and Employment explains how the level of national income and employment is determined in an economy.

The central idea of Keynesian economics is that the level of income and employment depends largely on the level of aggregate demand in the economy.

⭐ Board Exam Focus:

Questions based on Consumption Function, Saving Function, APC, MPC, APS, MPS and their relationships are important for both conceptual and numerical questions.
1. BASIC CONCEPT

💡 Income and Employment

Definition 1 — Income:
Income refers to the flow of earnings received by the factors of production for providing their services in the production process.
Definition 2 — Employment:
Employment refers to the utilisation of factors of production such as labour in productive economic activities.
Simple Understanding:

When firms produce goods and services, they require factors of production. These factors receive income in return for their services. At the same time, production generates employment opportunities.
Example:
A factory produces clothes. It employs workers and pays them wages. The workers' wages become their income, while their work represents employment.
2. KEYNESIAN APPROACH

👨‍🏫 Keynes and Determination of Income

Keynesian Idea:
According to Keynes, the level of income and employment in an economy is determined by the level of aggregate demand.

If aggregate demand increases, producers may increase production to meet the higher demand. This can lead to an increase in income and employment.

If aggregate demand decreases, firms may reduce production, which can reduce income and employment.

Aggregate Demand ↑ → Production ↑ → Income ↑ → Employment ↑
Aggregate Demand ↓ → Production ↓ → Income ↓ → Employment ↓
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

Question: According to Keynes, what determines the level of income and employment?

Answer: The level of aggregate demand.
3. AGGREGATE DEMAND

📊 Meaning of Aggregate Demand

Definition 1:
Aggregate Demand refers to the total demand for goods and services in an economy during a given period.
Definition 2:
Aggregate Demand is the total planned expenditure on final goods and services in an economy during a given period.
Simple Meaning:

Aggregate demand tells us how much total expenditure households, firms, government and the foreign sector plan to make on goods and services.
Important:

Aggregate Demand is also called planned expenditure in the Keynesian framework.
4. COMPONENTS OF AD

🧩 Components of Aggregate Demand

The components of aggregate demand depend upon the type of economy being considered.

In a Two-Sector Economy

A two-sector economy consists mainly of households and firms.

AD = C + I
C = Consumption Expenditure
I = Investment Expenditure

In a Three-Sector Economy

A three-sector economy includes households, firms and the government.

AD = C + I + G
G = Government Expenditure

In a Four-Sector Economy

A four-sector economy also includes the foreign sector.

AD = C + I + G + (X − M)
X = Exports
M = Imports
X − M = Net Exports
⭐ Board Exam Focus:

Remember the sequence:

2 Sector → C + I
3 Sector → C + I + G
4 Sector → C + I + G + (X − M)
5. CONSUMPTION

🛒 Consumption Expenditure

Definition:
Consumption expenditure is the expenditure incurred by households on goods and services for the satisfaction of their wants.
Examples:

• Food
• Clothing
• Education
• Transport
• Medical services
• Entertainment
• Household goods

Consumption is generally influenced by the level of income of households.

General Relationship:

Income increases → Consumption generally increases
Income decreases → Consumption generally decreases
6. AUTONOMOUS CONSUMPTION

🔹 Autonomous Consumption

Definition 1:
Autonomous consumption is the minimum level of consumption that takes place even when income is zero.
Definition 2:
Autonomous consumption is consumption that does not depend on the current level of income.
Example:
Even if a household has no current income, it may still need to spend on basic necessities by using past savings or borrowing.
Exam Point:

Autonomous consumption is represented by ā or C̄ in some textbook representations.
7. INDUCED CONSUMPTION

🔸 Induced Consumption

Definition:
Induced consumption is the part of consumption expenditure that varies with the level of income.
Income increases → Induced consumption increases

Income decreases → Induced consumption decreases
Total Consumption = Autonomous Consumption + Induced Consumption
⭐ Difference to Remember:

Autonomous Consumption: Independent of current income.

Induced Consumption: Depends on current income.
8. CONSUMPTION FUNCTION

📈 Consumption Function

Definition 1:
Consumption function shows the functional relationship between consumption expenditure and income.
Definition 2:
Consumption function expresses the relationship between consumption and disposable income.
C = C̄ + bY
Where:

C = Total Consumption
C̄ = Autonomous Consumption
b = Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC)
Y = Income
⭐ Board Exam Focus:

The consumption function is generally written as:

C = C̄ + bY

The intercept represents autonomous consumption, while the slope represents MPC.
9. SAVING

💰 Meaning of Saving

Definition:
Saving is that part of income which is not spent on consumption.
Saving = Income − Consumption
S = Y − C
Example:

Income = ₹10,000
Consumption = ₹8,000

Saving = ₹10,000 − ₹8,000
Saving = ₹2,000
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

The relationship between income, consumption and saving is frequently used in numerical and conceptual questions.
10. AUTONOMOUS SAVING

🔹 Autonomous Saving

Definition:
Autonomous saving refers to saving that is independent of the current level of income.

In the basic Keynesian consumption function, when income is zero, consumption may still be positive. In such a situation, saving can be negative.

Important:

Negative saving is also called dissaving. It means consumption is greater than current income.
11. DISSAVING

📉 Dissaving

Definition:
Dissaving occurs when consumption expenditure is greater than income.
If C > Y → Saving is Negative → Dissaving
Income = ₹5,000
Consumption = ₹7,000

Saving = ₹5,000 − ₹7,000
Saving = −₹2,000

Therefore, the household is dissaving ₹2,000.
12. SAVING FUNCTION

📊 Saving Function

Definition:
Saving function shows the functional relationship between saving and income.

Since saving is the difference between income and consumption, the saving function can be derived from the consumption function.

S = Y − C

If the consumption function is:

C = C̄ + bY

Then:

S = Y − (C̄ + bY)

S = −C̄ + (1 − b)Y
Here:

−C̄ = Autonomous Saving
(1 − b) = Marginal Propensity to Save
13. KEY RELATIONSHIP

🔗 Income, Consumption and Saving

Y = C + S
Income = Consumption + Saving

This relationship means that total income of a household is either spent on consumption or saved.

Income Consumption Saving
₹1,000 ₹900 ₹100
₹2,000 ₹1,700 ₹300
₹3,000 ₹2,400 ₹600
Board Trick:

Whenever you know any two of the following, the third can be found:

Y, C and S

Y = C + S
C = Y − S
S = Y − C
14. APC

📐 Average Propensity to Consume (APC)

Definition:
Average Propensity to Consume refers to the ratio of consumption expenditure to income.
APC = C ÷ Y
APC = Consumption / Income
Income = ₹10,000
Consumption = ₹8,000

APC = 8,000 ÷ 10,000

APC = 0.8
⭐ Important Board Point:

APC can be expressed as a ratio or percentage.

If APC = 0.8
APC = 80%
15. APS

📐 Average Propensity to Save (APS)

Definition:
Average Propensity to Save refers to the ratio of saving to income.
APS = S ÷ Y
Income = ₹10,000
Saving = ₹2,000

APS = 2,000 ÷ 10,000

APS = 0.2
16. IMPORTANT RELATIONSHIP

🔗 Relationship between APC and APS

We know:

Y = C + S

Dividing both sides by Y:

Y/Y = C/Y + S/Y
1 = APC + APS
Therefore:

APC + APS = 1
APC = 1 − APS
APS = 1 − APC
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

If APC = 0.75, then:

APS = 1 − 0.75
APS = 0.25
17. MPC

📈 Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC)

Definition:
Marginal Propensity to Consume refers to the ratio of change in consumption expenditure to change in income.
MPC = ΔC ÷ ΔY
ΔC = Change in Consumption
ΔY = Change in Income
Income increases from ₹10,000 to ₹12,000.

Change in Income = ₹2,000

Consumption increases from ₹8,000 to ₹9,500.

Change in Consumption = ₹1,500

MPC = ₹1,500 ÷ ₹2,000

MPC = 0.75
18. MPS

📉 Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS)

Definition:
Marginal Propensity to Save refers to the ratio of change in saving to change in income.
MPS = ΔS ÷ ΔY
ΔS = Change in Saving
ΔY = Change in Income
⭐ Board Exam Point:

MPC and MPS together explain how an additional unit of income is divided between additional consumption and additional saving.
19. KEY RELATIONSHIP

🔗 Relationship between MPC and MPS

MPC + MPS = 1
MPC = 1 − MPS
MPS = 1 − MPC
Example:

If MPC = 0.8

MPS = 1 − 0.8
MPS = 0.2
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

Questions asking to calculate MPC from MPS or MPS from MPC are common and very important.
20. APC vs MPC

⚖️ APC and MPC — Difference

Basis APC MPC
Meaning Ratio of total consumption to total income Ratio of change in consumption to change in income
Formula C ÷ Y ΔC ÷ ΔY
Based on Total values Changes in values
Easy Memory Trick:

Average → Total ÷ Total
Marginal → Change ÷ Change
21. APS vs MPS

⚖️ APS and MPS — Difference

Basis APS MPS
Meaning Ratio of total saving to total income Ratio of change in saving to change in income
Formula S ÷ Y ΔS ÷ ΔY
Based on Total values Changes in values
22. IMPORTANT PROPERTIES

⭐ Properties of APC and APS

Property 1:

APC + APS = 1
Property 2:

APC can be greater than 1 when consumption is greater than income.
Property 3:

APS can be negative when consumption is greater than income.
Property 4:

When income is zero and autonomous consumption is positive, saving is negative.
Property 5:

As income increases, APC generally tends to fall under the basic Keynesian consumption hypothesis.
⭐ Board Focus:

Do not confuse APC with MPC. APC deals with total consumption and income, while MPC deals with changes in consumption and income.

AGGREGATE DEMAND

📊 Aggregate Demand and Aggregate Supply

Definition 1 — Aggregate Demand:
Aggregate Demand refers to the total planned expenditure on final goods and services in an economy during a given period.
Definition 2 — Aggregate Supply:
Aggregate Supply refers to the total value of goods and services that firms plan to produce during a given period.
Key Idea:

In the Keynesian model, equilibrium level of income is determined at the point where Aggregate Demand = Aggregate Supply.
Equilibrium Condition: AD = AS
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

The condition of equilibrium level of income is an important conceptual and numerical question.
1. AGGREGATE SUPPLY

📦 Meaning of Aggregate Supply

Aggregate supply represents the total output planned to be produced by firms in an economy during a given period.

In the simple Keynesian model, the value of aggregate supply is represented by the level of income.
AS = Y
Remember:

In the basic two-sector Keynesian model:

AD = C + I

and

AS = Y
2. EQUILIBRIUM LEVEL

⚖️ Equilibrium Level of Income

Definition:
Equilibrium level of income is the level of income at which planned aggregate demand is equal to aggregate supply.
AD = AS

Since aggregate supply is represented by income:

AD = Y

For a two-sector economy:

C + I = Y
⭐ Board Exam Point:

At equilibrium, there is no tendency for producers to increase or decrease production, assuming other conditions remain unchanged.
3. EXCESS DEMAND

📈 Excess Demand

Definition:
Excess demand is a situation when aggregate demand is greater than the level of output corresponding to full employment.
AD > AS at Full Employment
Simple Meaning:

People want to buy more goods and services than the economy can produce at the existing full-employment level.
Example:

Suppose the economy is producing ₹1,000 crore worth of goods at full employment, but planned expenditure is ₹1,100 crore.

AD = ₹1,100 crore
AS = ₹1,000 crore

Therefore: AD > AS

This represents excess demand.
4. INFLATIONARY GAP

🔥 Inflationary Gap

Definition:
Inflationary gap is the excess of aggregate demand over the level of aggregate supply at full employment.
Inflationary Gap = Excess Demand at Full Employment
Effect:

Excess demand creates upward pressure on the general price level when the economy is already operating at full employment.
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

Excess Demand → Inflationary Pressure

This relationship is frequently tested through statements, assertion-reason questions and short-answer questions.
5. DEFICIENT DEMAND

📉 Deficient Demand

Definition:
Deficient demand is a situation when aggregate demand is less than the level of output corresponding to full employment.
AD < AS at Full Employment
Simple Meaning:

The economy has the capacity to produce more at full employment, but aggregate demand is not sufficient to purchase that output.
Effect:

Deficient demand can lead to a fall in production, income and employment and may create deflationary pressure.
6. DEFLATIONARY GAP

📉 Deflationary Gap

Definition:
Deflationary gap is the amount by which aggregate demand falls short of the level of aggregate supply corresponding to full employment.
Relationship:

Deficient Demand → Deflationary Gap → Downward pressure on prices, output and employment.
⭐ Exam Trick:

Excess Demand → Inflationary Gap
Deficient Demand → Deflationary Gap

⚖️ Excess Demand vs Deficient Demand

Basis Excess Demand Deficient Demand
Meaning AD is too high relative to full-employment output AD is too low relative to full-employment output
Gap Inflationary Gap Deflationary Gap
Price Pressure Upward Downward
Employment May create demand beyond full employment May lead to unemployment
Main Problem Inflationary pressure Deflationary pressure / unemployment
7. FULL EMPLOYMENT

👥 Full Employment

Definition:
Full employment refers to a situation in which all those who are willing and able to work at the prevailing wage rate can find employment, apart from unavoidable frictional or structural unemployment.
Important:

Full employment does not mean that every single person in the economy is employed at every moment.
⭐ Board Focus:

Do not write “zero unemployment” as the definition of full employment. Some normal unemployment may exist even at full employment.
8. UNEMPLOYMENT

👤 Involuntary Unemployment

Definition:
Involuntary unemployment exists when people are willing and able to work at the prevailing wage rate but cannot find employment.
Keynesian View:

According to Keynes, an economy can remain in equilibrium even when there is involuntary unemployment.
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

The possibility of involuntary unemployment equilibrium is an important feature of the Keynesian approach.
9. EX-ANTE AND EX-POST

🧠 Ex-Ante and Ex-Post

Ex-Ante:
Ex-ante refers to planned or intended values before the actual economic activity takes place.
Ex-Post:
Ex-post refers to actual or realised values after the economic activity has taken place.
Basis Ex-Ante Ex-Post
Meaning Planned / intended Actual / realised
Time Before activity After activity
Example Planned investment Actual investment
⭐ Board Exam Focus:

Ex-Ante = Planned
Ex-Post = Actual
10. INVENTORY

📦 Planned and Unplanned Inventory

Planned Inventory:
The change in inventory that firms intentionally plan to maintain as part of their business decisions.
Unplanned Inventory:
The unexpected change in inventories resulting from a difference between planned sales and actual sales.
Example:

A firm plans to sell 1,000 units but consumers purchase only 800 units.

The remaining 200 units become an unplanned increase in inventory.
Equilibrium Point:

At equilibrium, unplanned changes in inventories are zero.
11. ALTERNATIVE CONDITION

💰 Saving-Investment Approach

For a two-sector economy:

AD = C + I

At equilibrium:

Y = C + I

We also know:

Y = C + S

Therefore:

C + S = C + I
S = I
Equilibrium Condition:

In a simple two-sector Keynesian model:

Saving = Investment
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

Equilibrium can be expressed through two equivalent approaches:

AD = AS

or

S = I
12. EQUILIBRIUM DIAGRAM

📈 AD-AS Diagram — Concept

In the standard Keynesian cross diagram, income is shown on the horizontal axis and aggregate demand is shown on the vertical axis.

45° Line:

The 45° line represents points where:

AD = Y

It is also called the line of equality.
AD Curve:

The aggregate demand curve shows planned expenditure at different levels of income.
Equilibrium:

The point where the AD curve intersects the 45° line represents the equilibrium level of income.
13. AD AND OUTPUT

🔄 What Happens When AD Changes?

When AD > AS:

Firms experience lower-than-desired inventories and may increase production.
When AD < AS:

Firms experience unintended accumulation of inventories and may reduce production.
AD > AS → Production tends to increase
AD < AS → Production tends to decrease
⭐ Exam Point:

The economy tends towards equilibrium when planned aggregate demand becomes equal to aggregate supply.
14. MULTIPLIER — INTRODUCTION

🔢 Investment Multiplier

Definition 1:
Investment multiplier refers to the ratio of change in income to the initial change in investment.
Definition 2:
The multiplier shows how a change in autonomous expenditure can cause a more than proportionate change in equilibrium income.
K = ΔY ÷ ΔI
K = Investment Multiplier
ΔY = Change in Income
ΔI = Change in Investment
⭐ Board Focus:

Multiplier is one of the most important numerical topics of this chapter.
15. MULTIPLIER FORMULA

🧮 Relationship Between Multiplier and MPC

K = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC)
K = 1 ÷ MPS
Since:

MPC + MPS = 1

Therefore:

K = 1/MPS
Example:

If MPC = 0.8:

K = 1 ÷ (1 − 0.8)

K = 1 ÷ 0.2

K = 5
16. MULTIPLIER NUMERICAL

🧮 Numerical Example

Suppose autonomous investment increases by ₹100 crore and MPC is 0.75.

Step 1:
MPS = 1 − MPC
MPS = 1 − 0.75
MPS = 0.25

Step 2:
K = 1 ÷ MPS
K = 1 ÷ 0.25
K = 4

Step 3:
ΔY = K × ΔI
ΔY = 4 × ₹100 crore

Change in Income = ₹400 crore
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

Remember:

ΔY = K × ΔI

This formula is frequently useful in numerical questions.
17. MULTIPLIER MECHANISM

🔄 Working of Investment Multiplier

An initial increase in investment creates additional income for those who receive the expenditure. A part of this additional income is spent on consumption.

The consumption expenditure becomes income for others. They again spend a part of their additional income, creating further rounds of income generation.

Initial Investment → Income → Consumption → Income → Consumption → Further Income
ΔI → ΔY₁ → ΔY₂ → ΔY₃ → ... → Total ΔY
Why is the final increase in income greater than the initial increase in investment?

Because the additional income generated in one round creates additional consumption expenditure in subsequent rounds.

📊 Simple Multiplier Process

Round Income Generated Consumption if MPC = 0.8
Initial ₹100 ₹80
Second ₹80 ₹64
Third ₹64 ₹51.2
Fourth ₹51.2 ₹40.96
Each round becomes smaller because only a fraction of additional income is spent on consumption.

🔗 Important Multiplier Relationships

K = ΔY ÷ ΔI
K = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC)
K = 1 ÷ MPS
ΔY = K × ΔI
⭐ Quick Memory:

Higher MPC → Higher Multiplier

Lower MPC → Lower Multiplier

Higher MPS → Lower Multiplier

Lower MPS → Higher Multiplier

CONSUMPTION FUNCTION

🍽️ Consumption Function

Definition 1:
Consumption function shows the functional relationship between consumption expenditure and income.
Definition 2:
It explains how consumption expenditure changes when the level of income changes, other things remaining constant.
C = f(Y)
C = Consumption expenditure
Y = Income
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

Consumption function and its relationship with income are important for numerical as well as conceptual questions.
1. AUTONOMOUS CONSUMPTION

🛒 Autonomous Consumption

Definition:
Autonomous consumption is the minimum level of consumption that takes place even when income is zero.
It is independent of the level of current income.
Example:

Even if a person has no current income, some basic expenditure may still be made by using past savings, borrowing or other resources.
C = C̄ + cY
C̄ = Autonomous consumption
c = Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC)
Y = Income
2. INDUCED CONSUMPTION

📈 Induced Consumption

Definition:
Induced consumption is the part of consumption expenditure that changes with the level of income.
As income increases, induced consumption generally increases.
Induced Consumption = cY
Remember:

Autonomous consumption → Independent of current income
Induced consumption → Depends on income
3. LINEAR CONSUMPTION FUNCTION

📐 Linear Consumption Function

Definition:
A linear consumption function represents a constant marginal propensity to consume.
C = C̄ + cY

The intercept represents autonomous consumption, while the slope of the consumption function represents MPC.

Slope of Consumption Function = MPC
⭐ Board Exam Point:

If the consumption function is:

C = 100 + 0.8Y

then:
Autonomous Consumption = 100
MPC = 0.8
4. AVERAGE PROPENSITY TO CONSUME

📊 Average Propensity to Consume (APC)

Definition:
Average Propensity to Consume is the ratio of consumption expenditure to income.
APC = C ÷ Y
Example:

Income = ₹1,000
Consumption = ₹800

APC = 800 ÷ 1,000

APC = 0.8
⭐ Board Focus:

APC may be greater than, equal to, or less than 1 depending on the relationship between consumption and income.
5. MARGINAL PROPENSITY TO CONSUME

📈 Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC)

Definition:
Marginal Propensity to Consume is the ratio of change in consumption to change in income.
MPC = ΔC ÷ ΔY
Example:

Income increases from ₹1,000 to ₹1,500.
Consumption increases from ₹800 to ₹1,200.

ΔY = ₹500
ΔC = ₹400

MPC = 400 ÷ 500

MPC = 0.8
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

MPC is a very important numerical concept. In the simple model:

0 ≤ MPC ≤ 1

⚖️ APC vs MPC

Basis APC MPC
Meaning Consumption out of total income Additional consumption out of additional income
Formula C / Y ΔC / ΔY
Measures Average relationship Marginal relationship
Numerical Change Uses total values Uses changes in values
Memory Trick:

APC → Average → C/Y
MPC → Marginal → ΔC/ΔY
6. SAVING FUNCTION

💰 Saving Function

Definition 1:
Saving function shows the functional relationship between saving and income.
Definition 2:
It explains how saving changes when the level of income changes, other things remaining constant.
S = f(Y)
Since income is either consumed or saved:

Y = C + S
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

The relationship between income, consumption and saving is frequently used in numerical questions.
7. SAVING FUNCTION

🧮 Derivation of Saving Function

We know:

Y = C + S

Therefore:

S = Y − C

If:

C = C̄ + cY

Then:

S = Y − (C̄ + cY)
S = −C̄ + Y − cY
S = −C̄ + (1 − c)Y
Since:

1 − MPC = MPS

Therefore:

S = −C̄ + MPS × Y
8. AUTONOMOUS SAVING

💵 Autonomous Saving

Definition:
Autonomous saving refers to saving that occurs independently of current income.
When income is zero and autonomous consumption is positive, saving becomes negative.
At Y = 0:

S = −C̄
Negative saving is also called dissaving.
9. DISSAVING

📉 Dissaving

Definition:
Dissaving occurs when consumption expenditure is greater than current income.
C > Y
S < 0
Example:

Income = ₹500
Consumption = ₹700

S = Y − C
S = ₹500 − ₹700

S = −₹200

Therefore, dissaving = ₹200.
10. AVERAGE PROPENSITY TO SAVE

📊 Average Propensity to Save (APS)

Definition:
Average Propensity to Save is the ratio of saving to income.
APS = S ÷ Y
Income = ₹1,000
Saving = ₹200

APS = 200 ÷ 1,000

APS = 0.2
APS can be negative when there is dissaving.
11. MARGINAL PROPENSITY TO SAVE

💰 Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS)

Definition:
Marginal Propensity to Save is the ratio of change in saving to change in income.
MPS = ΔS ÷ ΔY
Example:

Income increases by ₹500.
Saving increases by ₹100.

MPS = 100 ÷ 500

MPS = 0.2
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

MPS is an important numerical concept and is directly related to MPC.
12. RELATIONSHIP

🔗 Relationship Between APC and APS

Since:

Y = C + S

Dividing both sides by Y:

1 = C/Y + S/Y
APC + APS = 1
⭐ Board Exam Formula:

APC + APS = 1
13. RELATIONSHIP

🔗 Relationship Between MPC and MPS

Since:

Y = C + S

Taking changes:

ΔY = ΔC + ΔS

Dividing by ΔY:

1 = ΔC/ΔY + ΔS/ΔY
MPC + MPS = 1
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

If MPC = 0.7:

MPS = 1 − 0.7

MPS = 0.3
14. IMPORTANT RELATIONSHIP

📈 Relationship Between APC and MPC

When the consumption function is linear with positive autonomous consumption:

APC > MPC
Reason:
APC includes autonomous consumption, while MPC measures only the change in consumption caused by a change in income.
As income increases, the contribution of autonomous consumption becomes smaller relative to total income. Therefore, APC tends to fall and approaches MPC.
15. SAVING RELATIONSHIP

📉 Relationship Between APS and MPS

For a saving function with negative autonomous saving:

APS < MPS
Reason:
The negative intercept of the saving function causes average saving to be lower than marginal saving.
As income increases, APS tends to rise towards MPS.
16. BREAK-EVEN POINT

⚖️ Break-Even Level of Income

Definition:
Break-even point is the level of income at which consumption is equal to income and saving is zero.
C = Y
S = 0
At break-even point:

APC = 1
and
APS = 0
If income = ₹1,000 and consumption = ₹1,000:

S = Y − C
S = ₹1,000 − ₹1,000

S = 0
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

Break-even level of income is an important graphical and numerical concept.
17. NUMERICAL PRACTICE

🧮 Consumption Function — Numerical

Suppose the consumption function is:

C = 100 + 0.8Y

If income is ₹500:

C = 100 + 0.8(500)
C = 100 + 400

C = ₹500
Since consumption is equal to income:

C = Y
Therefore:
Saving = 0

This is the break-even level of income.
18. NUMERICAL PRACTICE

🧮 Saving Function — Numerical

Suppose:

C = 100 + 0.75Y

Then:
MPC = 0.75
MPS = 1 − 0.75
MPS = 0.25

Saving function:
S = −100 + 0.25Y
Exam Tip:

To obtain the saving function, use:

S = Y − C

📊 Consumption and Saving Schedule

Income (Y) Consumption (C) Saving (S)
0 100 -100
100 175 -75
200 250 -50
300 325 -25
400 400 0
500 475 25
At income ₹400:

C = Y
S = 0

Therefore, ₹400 is the break-even level of income in this schedule.
19. GRAPHICAL CONCEPT

📈 Consumption and Saving Curves

The consumption curve generally slopes upward because consumption increases as income increases.

The saving curve also generally slopes upward because saving increases as income rises.

Consumption Curve:
Vertical intercept = Autonomous Consumption
Slope = MPC
Saving Curve:
Vertical intercept = Negative Autonomous Consumption
Slope = MPS
⭐ Board Focus:

The point where the consumption curve intersects the 45° line represents the break-even level of income.

EQUILIBRIUM

⚖️ Equilibrium Level of Income

Definition:
Equilibrium level of income is the level at which planned aggregate demand is equal to aggregate supply.
AD = AS
C + I = Y
S = I
At equilibrium, there is no tendency for firms to change production because planned expenditure equals planned output.
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

Equilibrium level of income can be determined by:

1. AD-AS Approach
2. Saving-Investment Approach

👥 Full Employment and Involuntary Unemployment

Full Employment:
A situation in which all those who are willing and able to work at the prevailing wage rate can find employment, apart from normal frictional or structural unemployment.
Involuntary Unemployment:
A situation in which people are willing and able to work at the prevailing wage rate but cannot find employment.
⭐ Exam Trap:

Full employment does not mean zero unemployment.

📦 Inventory — Important Board Concept

Planned Inventory:
Inventory that firms intentionally plan to maintain.
Unplanned Inventory:
Unexpected change in inventory caused by a difference between planned sales and actual sales.
At Equilibrium:

Unplanned change in inventory = Zero
If planned sales are 1,000 units but actual sales are only 800 units, the additional 200 units become an unplanned increase in inventory.
MULTIPLIER

🔢 Investment Multiplier — Final Revision

Definition:
Investment multiplier is the ratio of change in income to the initial change in investment.
K = ΔY ÷ ΔI
K = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC)
K = 1 ÷ MPS
ΔY = K × ΔI
Relationship:

Higher MPC → Higher Multiplier

Lower MPC → Lower Multiplier

Higher MPS → Lower Multiplier

Lower MPS → Higher Multiplier

🧮 Multiplier — Board Numerical

Suppose:

MPC = 0.75
Initial Investment = ₹200 crore

MPS = 1 − 0.75
MPS = 0.25

K = 1 ÷ 0.25
K = 4

Change in Income:
ΔY = K × ΔI
ΔY = 4 × ₹200 crore

ΔY = ₹800 crore
⭐ PYQ Focus:

Always convert percentages into decimals before applying the multiplier formula.
FISCAL MULTIPLIER

🏛️ Government Expenditure Multiplier

Definition:
Government expenditure multiplier shows the change in equilibrium income resulting from a change in government expenditure.
KG = ΔY ÷ ΔG
KG = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC)
An increase in government expenditure directly increases aggregate demand and can generate a multiplied increase in equilibrium income.
TAX MULTIPLIER

💸 Tax Multiplier

Definition:
Tax multiplier measures the change in equilibrium income resulting from a change in taxes.
KT = −MPC ÷ (1 − MPC)
Important:

The tax multiplier is negative because an increase in taxes generally reduces disposable income and consumption.
If MPC = 0.8:

KT = −0.8 ÷ (1 − 0.8)
= −0.8 ÷ 0.2

KT = −4
BALANCED BUDGET

⚖️ Balanced Budget Multiplier

Definition:
Balanced budget multiplier refers to the change in equilibrium income resulting from equal changes in government expenditure and taxes.
Balanced Budget Multiplier = 1
If government expenditure and taxes both increase by the same amount, equilibrium income increases by the same amount in the simple Keynesian model.
⭐ PYQ / BOARD FOCUS:

Balanced Budget Multiplier = 1

🏛️ Fiscal Policy and Employment

Fiscal Policy:
Fiscal policy refers to the use of government expenditure and taxation to influence economic activity, income and employment.
During Deficient Demand:

Government may increase expenditure or reduce taxes to increase aggregate demand.
During Excess Demand:

Government may reduce expenditure or increase taxes to reduce aggregate demand.
⭐ Exam Memory:

Deficient Demand → Expansionary Fiscal Policy

Excess Demand → Contractionary Fiscal Policy

🔄 Leakages and Injections

Leakages:
Leakages are flows of income that are not directly spent on domestic goods and services.
Injections:
Injections are additions to the circular flow of income.
Examples of Leakages:
Saving, taxes and imports.
Examples of Injections:
Investment, government expenditure and exports.
Equilibrium in Circular Flow:

Leakages = Injections
⭐ PYQ Focus:

Leakages: S + T + M

Injections: I + G + X

Meaning:
