Introduction to Health Hazards and First Aid

A workplace should not only be physically safe but should also protect the health and well-being of employees. Different workplace conditions can create health hazards, such as poor lighting, excessive noise, dust, chemicals, stress, unsafe posture and lack of cleanliness.

Employees should understand these hazards and follow safety procedures to reduce the possibility of illness or injury. First aid is also an important part of workplace safety because immediate basic assistance may be required when an accident or sudden health problem occurs.

Definition – Health Hazard:
A health hazard is a condition, substance, activity or situation in the workplace that can negatively affect the physical or mental health of a person.
Simple Meaning:
A health hazard is anything at the workplace that can make a person ill, uncomfortable or unhealthy.
📌 Example – Health Hazard in an Office
A worker continuously uses a computer while sitting in an incorrect posture. Over time, this may cause discomfort or strain. Poor posture can therefore be considered a workplace health concern.

Health Hazards at the Workplace

Health hazards may develop because of the physical environment, working conditions, equipment, substances or work practices. Some hazards produce immediate discomfort while others may affect health gradually.

Common Workplace Health Hazards

  • Poor lighting
  • Excessive noise
  • Dust and airborne particles
  • Unsafe or poor posture
  • Inadequate ventilation
  • Unclean workplace
  • Exposure to harmful substances
  • Work-related stress
  • Fatigue caused by unsuitable working conditions
📌 Example – Poor Lighting
If a workplace does not have adequate lighting, employees may experience difficulty while reading documents or using a computer. Proper lighting helps create a safer and more comfortable working environment.
📌 Example – Excessive Noise
Continuous exposure to very loud workplace noise can create discomfort and may affect a person's well-being. Appropriate controls and protective measures should be used where necessary.
📌 Example – Poor Ventilation
A room with insufficient ventilation may become uncomfortable and may allow dust or other airborne contaminants to remain in the environment.

Types of Health Hazards

Type Meaning Example
Physical Conditions in the physical environment that may affect health. Excessive noise or poor lighting.
Chemical Exposure to harmful chemical substances. Unsafe handling of certain chemicals.
Biological Exposure to harmful biological agents. Exposure to infectious organisms.
Ergonomic Problems caused by unsuitable posture, equipment or working arrangement. Working continuously in an uncomfortable posture.
Psychosocial Work-related conditions that may negatively affect mental well-being. Excessive work pressure or prolonged workplace stress.

Preventing Health Hazards

Health hazards can often be reduced by maintaining a clean workplace, following safety procedures and using equipment correctly.

  • Keep the workplace clean and organised.
  • Maintain proper ventilation.
  • Use suitable lighting.
  • Maintain correct posture while working.
  • Take appropriate breaks during prolonged computer work.
  • Use protective equipment where required.
  • Follow workplace safety instructions.
  • Report unsafe conditions to the responsible person.
  • Maintain equipment properly.
  • Follow health and safety policies.
Important: Safety measures should always follow the instructions of the workplace, teacher, supervisor or authorised safety personnel.

First Aid

Definition – First Aid:
First aid is the immediate and basic assistance given to a person who becomes ill or is injured before professional medical help is available.
Simple Meaning:
First aid means giving immediate basic help to an injured or unwell person until appropriate professional help is available.

Importance of First Aid

  • Provides immediate assistance.
  • Helps protect the affected person from further harm.
  • Helps manage an emergency until professional assistance arrives.
  • Supports a safer response to workplace accidents.
  • Helps employees respond calmly during emergencies.
📌 Example – Minor Workplace Injury
If a person experiences a minor injury at work, the situation should be reported and appropriate first-aid procedures should be followed according to workplace rules. If the injury is serious, professional medical assistance should be obtained immediately.

Basic Principles of First Aid

The purpose of first aid is to provide immediate basic support while ensuring that the situation is handled safely.

  1. Stay calm and assess the situation.
  2. Make sure the area is reasonably safe before approaching.
  3. Inform a responsible person or supervisor.
  4. Call appropriate emergency or professional help when necessary.
  5. Provide only the first aid that you are trained or authorised to provide.
  6. Do not unnecessarily move a seriously injured person.
  7. Keep the affected person comfortable and reassured.
  8. Follow workplace emergency procedures.
Safety Note: Do not attempt medical procedures beyond your training. Serious injuries or medical emergencies require professional medical assistance.

First-Aid Box

A workplace should have an appropriately maintained first-aid facility according to its safety requirements. A first-aid box contains basic supplies that may be required for minor injuries.

Examples of Basic First-Aid Supplies

  • Adhesive bandages
  • Sterile gauze
  • Clean dressings
  • Medical tape
  • Disposable gloves
  • Antiseptic supplies as appropriate
  • Scissors designed for first-aid use
🛠️ Practical Steps – Checking a First-Aid Box
  1. Locate the designated first-aid box.
  2. Check whether it is clearly identified.
  3. Check whether commonly required supplies are available.
  4. Check packaging and expiry information where applicable.
  5. Report missing or unsuitable supplies to the responsible person.
  6. Return the first-aid box to its designated location.

Common Workplace Situations and Safe Response

Minor Cut or Injury

A minor injury should be reported and handled using the workplace's approved first-aid procedure. The affected person should receive appropriate basic assistance.

Slip or Fall

The area should be made safe to prevent further accidents, and the incident should be reported. If the person appears seriously injured, professional medical assistance should be obtained.

Electrical Incident

Do not touch a person who may still be in contact with an electrical source. The electricity should be isolated by an authorised person where possible, and emergency assistance should be obtained according to workplace procedures.

Fire Emergency

Follow the workplace fire emergency procedure, alert others, leave through the designated safe route and move to the designated assembly area. Emergency services should be contacted according to the established procedure.

Remember: The correct response depends on the emergency. Personal safety must always come first.

Safety Practices at the Workplace

Safety practices are regular actions followed to prevent accidents, injuries and health problems at the workplace.

General Safety Practices

  • Follow workplace safety rules.
  • Keep floors and pathways clear.
  • Do not leave cables in unsafe locations.
  • Use electrical equipment correctly.
  • Keep emergency exits accessible.
  • Know the location of emergency exits.
  • Know the location of the first-aid facility.
  • Report damaged equipment.
  • Maintain good housekeeping.
  • Use appropriate protective equipment where required.
  • Follow fire safety procedures.
  • Never ignore a workplace hazard.

Integrated Workplace Safety Practices

Situation Possible Hazard Safe Practice
Wet floor Slip and fall Clean or isolate the area and provide an appropriate warning.
Damaged electrical cable Electrical hazard Stop using it and report it to the responsible person.
Blocked emergency exit Emergency evacuation problem Keep emergency routes clear.

Incorrect computer posture Ergonomic health hazard Use an appropriate workstation arrangement and posture.

Practical Activity – Identify Health and Safety Problems

Observe a classroom, computer laboratory or workplace environment and identify possible health and safety problems.

🛠️ Practical Steps – Activity

Step 1 – Observe the Workplace

Look around the classroom, laboratory or workplace carefully without touching unsafe equipment.

Step 2 – Check Physical Conditions

Observe floors, pathways, lighting, ventilation, seating and general cleanliness.

Step 3 – Check Electrical Safety

Look for visible signs of damaged cables, overloaded connections or improperly placed electrical equipment. Do not touch damaged equipment.

Step 4 – Check Fire Safety

Identify emergency exits and check whether pathways are clear.

Step 5 – Check Health Conditions

Observe whether desks, chairs and computer workstations allow reasonably comfortable working conditions.

Step 6 – Record the Hazards

Observation Possible Hazard Suggested Safe Practice
Wet floor Slip Clean the area and provide an appropriate warning.
Loose cable Trip/Electrical hazard Report and arrange the cable safely.
Blocked exit Evacuation problem Keep the exit clear.
Poor workstation arrangement Ergonomic problem Improve the workstation arrangement.

Step 7 – Report

Report serious or unsafe conditions to the teacher, supervisor or responsible authority rather than trying to repair them yourself.

Important Questions

1. What is a health hazard?
A health hazard is a condition, substance, activity or situation in the workplace that can negatively affect the physical or mental health of a person.
2. Give any four examples of workplace health hazards.
Examples include poor lighting, excessive noise, poor posture and inadequate ventilation.
3. What is first aid?
First aid is the immediate and basic assistance given to a person who becomes ill or is injured before professional medical help is available.
4. Why is first aid important?
First aid provides immediate basic assistance, helps prevent further harm and supports the affected person until appropriate professional help is available.
5. What is the importance of workplace safety practices?
Safety practices help prevent accidents and injuries, reduce workplace hazards and create a safer and healthier working environment.
6. What is an ergonomic health hazard?
An ergonomic health hazard is a workplace condition related to unsuitable posture, equipment or work arrangement that may cause physical discomfort or health problems.
7. Mention any five ways to prevent workplace health hazards.
Five ways are:
  • Maintain cleanliness.
  • Provide proper lighting.
  • Maintain adequate ventilation.
  • Use appropriate workstation arrangements.
  • Follow workplace safety procedures.
8. What should an employee do after noticing a serious workplace hazard?
The employee should avoid unnecessary contact with the hazard, warn others where appropriate and report the situation immediately to the responsible person or supervisor.

Application-Based Questions

Situation 1 – Wet Floor
Question: A student notices water on the floor near a computer laboratory entrance. What should the student do?

Answer: The student should avoid the wet area, warn others if appropriate and inform the responsible person so that the area can be made safe.
Situation 2 – Damaged Electrical Cable
Question: A computer has a visibly damaged electrical cable. What should be done?

Answer: The equipment should not be used. The damaged cable should be reported to the responsible person or authorised technician.
Situation 3 – Blocked Emergency Exit
Question: Boxes are stored in front of an emergency exit. Why is this unsafe?

Answer: Blocked exits can delay evacuation during an emergency. Emergency routes should always remain accessible.
Situation 4 – Poor Sitting Posture
Question: A student regularly works at a computer in an uncomfortable posture. What type of hazard may this represent?

Answer: It may represent an ergonomic health hazard. The workstation and sitting arrangement should be adjusted appropriately.
Situation 5 – Workplace Fire
Question: What should employees do during a workplace fire emergency?

Answer: They should follow the established fire emergency procedure, alert others, use the designated safe evacuation route and move to the designated assembly area. Emergency assistance should be obtained according to workplace procedures.

Two practices are keeping pathways clear and reporting damaged or unsafe equipment.
