Money and banking are essential parts of a modern economy. Money makes exchange easier, while banks mobilise savings, provide loans and support economic activities.

1. MONEY

💰 Meaning of Money

Definition:
Money is anything that is generally accepted as a medium of exchange for goods and services and for the settlement of debts.
Simple Meaning:
Money is anything generally accepted by people for buying and selling goods and services.
Example:
When a student purchases a book for ₹500, the student gives money to the seller and receives the book.
2. FUNCTIONS

🔄 Functions of Money

1. Medium of Exchange

Money acts as an intermediary in the exchange of goods and services. It removes the need for direct exchange of goods.

Example:
A student pays ₹100 for a book instead of giving another product to the bookseller.

2. Measure of Value

Money provides a common unit in which the value of different goods and services can be expressed and compared.

A pen may cost ₹20 and a book ₹200. Their values can be compared through money.

3. Store of Value

Money allows purchasing power to be stored for future use.

4. Standard of Deferred Payments

Money provides a standard for payments that are to be made in the future, such as repayment of loans.

Function Meaning
Medium of Exchange Used for buying and selling
Measure of Value Measures value of goods and services
Store of Value Stores purchasing power for future
Standard of Deferred Payments Helps make future payments
3. BARTER SYSTEM

🔁 Barter System

Definition:
Barter system is a system in which goods and services are exchanged directly for other goods and services without the use of money.

Problems of Barter System

  • Double coincidence of wants
  • Lack of common measure of value
  • Difficulty in storing wealth
  • Difficulty in making deferred payments
  • Difficulty in transferring value
Double Coincidence of Wants:
It means that two persons must want each other's goods at the same time for direct exchange to take place.
Example:
A has wheat and wants shoes. B has shoes but wants clothes. Since B does not want wheat, direct exchange becomes difficult.
4. MONEY SUPPLY

💵 Money Supply

Definition:
Money supply refers to the total stock of money held by the public at a particular point of time.
Exam Point:
Money supply is a stock concept because it is measured at a particular point of time.

Basic Components

  • Currency held by the public
  • Net demand deposits held by commercial banks
Money Supply = Currency held by public + Net Demand Deposits
5. COMMERCIAL BANKS

🏦 Commercial Banks

Definition:
A commercial bank is a financial institution that accepts deposits from the public and provides loans and advances.

Major Functions

1. Accepting Deposits

Commercial banks accept deposits from individuals and organisations.

2. Granting Loans and Advances

Banks provide loans and advances to households, businesses and other borrowers.

3. Credit Creation

Commercial banks create deposits through their lending activities, subject to reserve requirements and other conditions.

4. Agency Functions

  • Collection of cheques
  • Payment of bills
  • Transfer of funds
  • Collection of dividends and interest

5. General Utility Services

  • Locker facilities
  • Bank drafts
  • Electronic payment facilities
6. CREDIT CREATION

📈 Credit Creation by Commercial Banks

Meaning:
Credit creation refers to the process through which commercial banks create deposits through lending activities.

Commercial banks keep a part of deposits as reserves and lend the remaining amount, subject to regulations. The loan may be redeposited in the banking system, leading to multiple expansion of deposits.

Simple Example

Suppose a bank receives a deposit of ₹1,000 and the reserve ratio is 20%.

Required Reserve = ₹1,000 × 20%
= ₹200

Potential Loan = ₹1,000 − ₹200
= ₹800

Money Multiplier

Money Multiplier = 1 ÷ Reserve Ratio
Reserve Ratio = 20% = 0.20

Money Multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.20

= 5
The simple money multiplier model works under specified assumptions. Actual credit creation may differ because of reserve behaviour, cash withdrawals, demand for loans and other factors.
7. CENTRAL BANK

🏛️ Central Bank

Definition:
A central bank is the apex monetary institution of a country which regulates the monetary and banking system.
India's Central Bank:
Reserve Bank of India — RBI
Important Facts:
RBI was established in 1935 and was nationalised in 1949.
8. RBI FUNCTIONS

🏛️ Functions of Reserve Bank of India

1. Issue of Currency

The RBI has the authority to issue banknotes in India, subject to the provisions of law. The ₹1 note and coins are issued by the Government of India.

2. Banker to the Government

RBI acts as banker, agent and adviser to the Central Government and State Governments.

3. Banker's Bank

RBI acts as the banker to commercial banks and provides them financial and banking support.

4. Lender of Last Resort

RBI can provide financial assistance to commercial banks when they are unable to obtain funds from other sources, subject to applicable conditions.

5. Controller of Credit

RBI uses monetary policy instruments to regulate the availability and cost of credit in the economy.

6. Custodian of Foreign Exchange Reserves

RBI manages India's foreign exchange reserves and helps maintain orderly conditions in the foreign exchange market.

9. CREDIT CONTROL

🎛️ Methods of Credit Control

The central bank uses different instruments to regulate the availability of credit in the economy.

Quantitative / General Instruments

  • Bank Rate
  • Repo Rate
  • Reverse Repo Rate
  • Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)
  • Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)
  • Open Market Operations (OMO)

Qualitative / Selective Instrument

  • Margin Requirement
10. BANK RATE

📊 Bank Rate

Definition:
Bank Rate is the rate at which the central bank provides certain long-term funds or rediscounts eligible bills for commercial banks, as applicable under the monetary framework.
Bank Rate increases:
Borrowing becomes relatively expensive → credit may contract.
Bank Rate decreases:
Borrowing becomes relatively cheaper → credit may expand.
11. REPO RATE

🔄 Repo Rate

Definition:
Repo rate is the rate at which the central bank lends short-term funds to commercial banks against eligible securities under a repurchase arrangement.
Repo Rate increases:
Cost of borrowing from RBI increases → credit conditions may become tighter.
Repo Rate decreases:
Cost of borrowing from RBI decreases → credit conditions may become easier.
12. REVERSE REPO

↔️ Reverse Repo Rate

Definition:
Reverse repo rate is the rate at which the central bank borrows funds from commercial banks against eligible securities under a repurchase arrangement.
When banks park funds with RBI, liquidity available with banks for lending may decrease.
13. CRR

💵 Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)

Definition:
CRR is the percentage of a bank's deposits that it is required to maintain as cash reserves with the RBI.
CRR increases:
Banks have fewer funds available for lending → credit creation may decrease.
CRR decreases:
Banks have more funds available for lending → credit creation may increase.
14. SLR

🏦 Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)

Definition:
SLR is the percentage of a bank's net demand and time liabilities that it is required to maintain in the form of specified liquid assets, as prescribed by the RBI.
Higher SLR generally reduces funds available for lending, while lower SLR can increase lending capacity, other things remaining constant.
15. OMO

📜 Open Market Operations

Definition:
Open Market Operations refer to the buying and selling of government securities by the central bank in the financial market.

RBI Buys Government Securities

RBI pays money to sellers of securities. This generally increases liquidity in the banking system.

RBI Sells Government Securities

Money moves from buyers to RBI. This generally reduces liquidity in the banking system.

RBI Action Effect on Liquidity
Buys Government Securities Liquidity increases
Sells Government Securities Liquidity decreases
16. MARGIN REQUIREMENT

📐 Margin Requirement

Definition:
Margin requirement is the difference between the value of the security offered as collateral and the amount of loan granted against it.
Security value = ₹1,00,000

Loan granted = ₹80,000

Margin = ₹20,000

Margin percentage = 20%
Higher Margin:
Borrower gets a smaller loan against the same security.
Lower Margin:
Borrower can get a larger loan against the same security.

📈 Expansionary Monetary Policy

Expansionary monetary policy aims to increase liquidity and encourage credit and economic activity.

  • Lower repo rate
  • Lower CRR
  • Purchase of government securities
  • Lower reserve requirements, where applicable

📉 Contractionary Monetary Policy

Contractionary monetary policy aims to reduce excess liquidity and control inflationary pressure.

  • Higher repo rate
  • Higher CRR
  • Sale of government securities
  • Higher reserve requirements, where applicable

🧮 Important Formula Sheet
